# Recency and skeptic audit of blocker complaints

**Status date: 2026-11-08.** Audited `strict_desktop.md`, `cloud_desktop.md`, `open_source.md`, `mobile_popular.md`, and `freemium_blockers.md`; none was edited. This audit prioritizes the highest-severity and broadest claims, then checks primary issue state, release notes, vendor replies, later comments by the same user, and newer reports. **Resolved** means a source records a fix for that issue, not independent verification on every device. **Current recurrence** means a report or vendor document applies to a current-era build at the cutoff, not that most users are affected. **Open/unverified** means neither current persistence nor causation is established.

## What not to overstate: recency ledger

### Takeaway

Most of the underlying notes are already appropriately qualified. The clearest recency correction is LeechBlock’s linked Firefox/MV3 defect: Mozilla and LeechBlock’s author recorded a fix in 2024, so it should not be described as lacking resolution evidence. Other important corrections are narrower: Freedom’s changelog confirms the Android reboot-startup fix but not every symptom in the review; Opal has both a same-user resolution and a later independent recurrence; RescueTime’s April 2026 log-processing problem and Firefox-detail problem must be separated. The most dramatic permanent-damage, factory-reset, and permanent-lock claims remain the least verified.

### Inferences

- **Apply fixes only to the symptom they name.** Freedom 7.14.1 directly supports the reboot-startup fix, not every Accessibility/Firefox symptom; RescueTime’s green status does not resolve its separate Firefox-detail issue; Opal’s same-user repair does not close a later independent configuration case.
- **A closed/completed label is weaker than a fix record, but an open label is weaker than a current reproduction.** Mozilla supplies the former for LeechBlock’s linked issue; SelfControl #879/#852 supply only the latter for severe allegations.
- **Permanent words need permanent evidence.** “Permanently breaks,” “nearly destroyed years of work,” “impossible to uninstall,” and “factory reset required” should remain quotations/paraphrases of a user’s experience unless diagnostics or follow-up establishes more.
- **Several themes survive precisely because newer evidence cuts both ways.** Opal remediation followed by recurrence, ScreenZen reports on the newest iOS build, Forest persistence reports on 5.33, and AppBlock’s current 4.14 entitlement report are stronger than stale grievance recycling.
- **The phrase “current” must include a build boundary.** one sec Android 3.0.0, BlockSite Chrome 8.0.1, StayFocusd 4.6.24, and AppBlock iOS 4.14.0 are not tested by the older reports most readily attached to them.

### Gaps

- **Search/access limitations:** Tavily CLI returned “plan’s set usage limit” exceeded; the provided web-search fallback returned HTTP 432 for all attempted queries. Exa CLI supplied recency leads. Firecrawl later returned HTTP 429; direct `curl` succeeded for most primary pages. A surfaced Register URL returned HTTP 404 in direct retrieval. These are retrieval failures, not evidence about any product.
- No BlockSite vendor response to the February 2026 study was located in the accessible primary/policy pages or CSO text during this audit. That is **not proof no response exists**. No current 8.0.1 packet capture was found.
- GitHub’s unauthenticated comments endpoint returned HTTP 403 late in the audit. Issue metadata and Bugzilla’s complete primary comments were accessible; the LeechBlock resolution therefore rests on Mozilla’s primary record rather than a newly reread #404 comment thread.
- This audit rechecked the highest-impact statuses and current Apple metadata but did **not** independently replay every original Reddit, Trustpilot, Google Play, or App Store review in the five notes. Some of those sources were already bot-gated, dynamically rendered, mirrored, or index-only in the underlying research. Trustpilot allegations are not treated as vendor-verified facts.
- Vendor changelogs and replies establish what the vendor says changed, not hands-on confirmation on affected hardware. No new device tests, heap profiles, energy traces, packet captures, receipts, cancellation timestamps, or system diagnostics were produced here.
- Later user outcomes are missing for Freedom’s non-reboot Android symptoms, RescueTime’s Firefox app detail fix, Opal’s October rule interaction, ScreenZen’s current unlock reports, and Forest’s 5.33 persistence/login reports. Do not infer either continued failure or resolution from silence.

## Robust themes surviving scrutiny

### Takeaway

The durable story is not “blockers are scams” or “blockers never work.” It is that blocking combines strict security controls, OS/browser integration, time/account rule state, and sometimes cloud synchronization; each boundary can fail in either direction—leaking distractions or blocking essential work. Recent vendor documents and current-version complaints positively confirm several of these risks, while active maintenance prevents blanket abandonment narratives.

### Cited Findings

- **Integration boundaries are the strongest cross-product technical theme.** Cold Turkey’s current-era releases repeatedly fix browser/profile detection and explicitly allow supported browsers to close when extension protection stops; FocusMe’s Linux guide documents X11/Wayland/Flatpak limits; Freedom documents proxy/VPN conflicts; SelfControl documents VPN/DoH limits; Plucky documents partial current Chromium support. ### Inferences

- The most defensible synthesis is **“strict blockers have high-consequence integration and state-management edges”**, not a generic reliability rating. Testing should record product build, OS/browser/app versions, profile/private/debug context, permission/battery/VPN state, rule semantics, exact lock/protection mode, and purchase channel.
- Severity and prevalence must remain separate axes. A single residual-shield/factory-reset story can deserve a recovery-design test without becoming a common-outcome claim; dozens of minor current reports still do not produce a measured incidence rate.
- The evidence supports prioritizing **undo/recovery safety, truthful shield/timer state, entitlement persistence, and explicit sync matrices** alongside raw blocking coverage. These are directly implicated by AppBlock, ScreenZen, Forest, Opal, LeechBlock, and the strict desktop products rather than being speculative feature requests.
- Vendor acknowledgment should raise confidence in a mechanism, not automatically accept blame allocation or fix completeness. Apple/Google/OEM constraints coexist with app-state defects; vendor updates coexist with regressions; user error coexists with inadequate warnings.

### Gaps

- No representative survey, controlled cross-device benchmark, support-ticket denominator, or verified failure/repair rate supports “most users,” “typical,” or cross-product numerical rankings.
- Public reviews often omit exact OS/device builds, settings, logs, and final outcomes; edited store reviews and stale version labels further weaken chronology. Current-version reports prove current allegations, not technical reproduction.
- No source set establishes permanent wholesale destruction of personal files, a universal irreversible device lock, systematic fraudulent charging, or company-wide malicious intent across these products. Claims not meeting that threshold should not be implied through language such as “ransomware,” “spyware,” or “scam” without attribution.
- Several official policies and troubleshooting pages change without granular revision histories. The dates above describe the document/version visible or reported at the cutoff, not necessarily the first date the limitation appeared or the last date it could occur.
