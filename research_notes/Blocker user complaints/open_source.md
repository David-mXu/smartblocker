# Pluckeye/Plucky, SelfControl, LeechBlock NG, and StayFocusd: user complaints, reliability, and project practices

**Research cutoff:** 2026-11-08. Scope is exactly **Pluckeye/Plucky**, **SelfControl (SelfControlApp)**, **LeechBlock NG**, and **StayFocusd**. No replacement blockers are assessed or recommended. Current prices and ratings are intentionally omitted.

**Evidence conventions:** “Recurring” means at least two independent firsthand reports of a similar symptom; it does not imply a measured incidence rate or a common root cause. “Isolated” means one report was found. “Official” means an author/maintainer documented the behavior or defect, not that users commonly encounter it. **STALE** marks reports that predate 2025 and must not be presented as current defects without retesting. An open issue is not proof that a defect still exists, and a closed issue is not proof of a fix unless an explanation, commit, or release note says so. GitHub reports are firsthand allegations until explicitly described as maintainer-diagnosed. StayFocusd reviews below come from ExtensionDownloader’s **review aggregator/mirror**, not a first-party support tracker; they remain unverified user reports. No severe claim is upgraded to fact merely because its title is dramatic.

## Which complaints recur across independent users/platforms, and on which OS/version?

### Takeaway

The strongest multi-user evidence is for SelfControl’s unreliable/overbroad network blocking and LeechBlock’s Android/MV3, synchronization, and configuration problems; Plucky’s best-supported concerns are deliberately broad blocking, difficult recovery/configuration, current Chromium-family incompatibility, and officially fixed historical failures. Recent StayFocusd reviews establish isolated timer and bypass complaints, not recurrence or a diagnosed Manifest V3 defect, so its evidence base should not be presented as equally strong.

### Cited Findings

**Pluckeye/Plucky — broad blocking, compatibility, and official defect history**

- **Intended high-impact scope, not a false-positive bug:** Plucky says it is known for “blocking all images and videos” by default. With its `system` feature enabled, it controls Internet access for the whole device rather than only a browser; the manual says non-browser apps such as Steam, Discord, and Spotify can be mistaken for browsers and supplies program-specific allow rules. On Windows and Linux, only supported browsers work with `system` enabled, and on macOS Safari is completely blocked by default. These are architectural/design tradeoffs with potentially **high usability severity**, but an app becoming unreachable under this configuration is not by itself evidence of a defect. [Overview](https://docs.pluckeye.net/overview); [system-feature documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/system)
- **Usability/documentation burden is explicit:** The manual offers three configuration routes—an in-browser interface, typed commands for power users, and the user site—but its delay-pattern section calls the patterns “somewhat complex, hard to describe” and potentially requiring significant thought. Rule specificity also changed in 1.14.10: an allow rule for a URL that worked before that version no longer had the same effect, requiring a block of the base URL and a more specific allow. These version-sensitive semantics can produce “nothing is blocked” or “too much is blocked” outcomes without a software malfunction. [Configuration documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/how-to-configure); [delay documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/delay); [1.14.10 release note](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)
- **Current browser complaint has a maintainer-confirmed external cause:** The browser page, updated 2026-08-06, lists Edge and Firefox as supported and Chrome, Brave, and Vivaldi as only partially supported because those browsers broke MV2 support. The Chrome page says Chrome broke MV2 extensions, including Plucky, in **July 2025**; its Linux workaround requires Chrome **138**, the last compatible version, to “never upgrade it.” Thus current Chrome/Brave/Vivaldi blocking failures are foreseeable compatibility limits, while the workaround creates a separate frozen-browser security/maintenance tradeoff rather than restoring normal support. [Browser-support matrix](https://docs.pluckeye.net/browsers); [Chrome documentation, updated 2026-09-08](https://docs.pluckeye.net/chrome)
- **Current Android performance/site-compatibility limitations are official:** The Android manual says Plucky “gets rather slow” with too many tabs and advises only one or two tabs and a small configuration. It also says the browser is “a bit old,” some websites do not work well, and WiX sites refuse to load; a replacement browsing solution was still described as beta at the cutoff. These are directly documented performance and compatibility complaints, but the manual does not quantify affected devices or establish a current regression version. [Plucky on Android](https://docs.pluckeye.net/pluckeye-on-android)
- **Official recovery documentation acknowledges all-access failures and several possible causes:** Plucky’s troubleshooting page covers `ERR_ADDRESS_UNREACHABLE` in Chrome/Edge on Windows/macOS, recommends inspecting verdicts before assuming Plucky is responsible, and says some other filtering products can cause either all access or no access depending on configuration. It also notes that the same symptom can come from a broken network card, router, or ISP. The page supplies delayed system-disable and expedite workflows, demonstrating both a potentially **high-severity loss-of-connectivity symptom** and the need to avoid attributing every outage to Plucky. [Troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting)
- **Officially fixed blackout defect — STALE for current builds:** Version **1.16.13 (2024-04-19)** says “Fix lingering blackout problem (block everything lingers on after reboot).” This confirms a historical block-everything-after-reboot defect and an explicit release fix; it is not evidence that current Plucky still has the problem. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)
- **Officially fixed synchronization defect — STALE:** The **1.14.30 (2023-07-01)** note says changes made on `u.pluckeye.net` in versions **1.14.10–1.14.29** had no effect until another configuration change was made locally. This is a narrow, maintainer-confirmed sync failure with affected versions and a dated fix, not a claim that all Plucky synchronization remains unreliable. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)
- **Official performance caveats are workload-dependent:** The troubleshooting manual says advertising-related JavaScript on some pages can repeatedly try to load blocked ads and make Firefox’s CPU “spin in a loop”; the suggested mitigation is closing tabs/restarting the browser. This is an official architectural interoperability explanation, not a benchmark of normal Plucky CPU use or proof that Plucky alone is the active process. [Troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting)

**SelfControl — failures to block, collateral blocking, and stuck state**

- **Recurring false negatives across years, browsers, and the current official release:** The open maintainer-created meta-issue **#680**, “X website isn't blocked, even though it's on my blocklist,” contains multiple independent cases. Examples include sites blocked in Chrome/Firefox but not Safari in 2021–2024, a 2024 report of only roughly half a blocklist working around an upgrade to **macOS Sequoia 15.1**, a **SelfControl 4.0.2 (410)** reporter with Substack/Instagram failures on 2025-03-06, and YouTube navigation escaping a block on 2026-08-30. These reports recur as a symptom class but do not prove one cause or a failure rate. [SelfControl #680](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680); [Sequoia-era comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680#issuecomment-2489249218); [4.0.2 report](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680#issuecomment-2704831693); [2026 YouTube report](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680#issuecomment-5469668557)
- **Current native-app limit, with unusually concrete version/network evidence:** On **2026-11-06**, a user on **SelfControl 4.0.2 and macOS 27.2**, without VPN/proxy, said the native **WhatsApp 26.43.75** app remained reachable during every block despite `whatsapp.com`, `whatsapp.net`, and Facebook domains being listed. The reporter supplied an observed IPv6 connection to port 5222 and distinguished the native app from browser troubleshooting. This is a recent, isolated technical report—not proof that every native app bypasses SelfControl and not yet a maintainer-confirmed diagnosis. [#680 comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680#issuecomment-6677986144)
- **Recurring unintended/CDN/shared-IP blocking:** Open maintainer-created meta-issue **#681**, “X website is blocked, even though it's NOT on my blocklist,” contains independent reports involving GitLab assets, Okta help, railways/courses, Terraform Registry, PayPal assets, Apple Music, developer documentation/package hosting, Workplace, GitHub Pages, and—on 2026-01-14—Bitwarden. The official FAQ explains the underlying scope: SelfControl blocks sites sharing servers or domain names with a listed site and recommends a short test block before a long one. The comments establish recurring collateral blocking, while the meta-issue’s open state alone does not establish that every domain remains affected today. [#681](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681); [official FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **Concrete shared-IP evidence rather than only speculation:** A 2023-11-08 reporter found unlisted `bun.sh` and blocked `vas3k.ru` resolving to the same Cloudflare addresses, with network tests showing the collateral failure. A later 2025 commenter generalized many cases to Fastly/Cloudflare reverse proxies, but that later universal theory and proposed detection algorithm are community explanations, **not** a maintainer-approved fix or proof every case has this cause. [Shared-IP diagnostic comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-1801326223); [community reverse-proxy explanation](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-2927805003)
- **Work-relevant collateral severity can exceed inconvenience:** A 2022 PayPal user said `www.paypalobjects.com` was unintendedly blocked even though PayPal itself loaded, leaving external assets unavailable and the PayPal site “functionally broken” for shipping work orders. Separate users reported Terraform Registry on **macOS 11.6.1 / SelfControl 4.0.2** and Python package-hosting/documentation failures on later macOS versions. These are direct accounts of blocked workflows, not verified cases of lost files or permanent system damage. [PayPal comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-1281616886); [Terraform comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-982385445); [Python package-hosting comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-1781886191)
- **VPN bypass is recurring but officially disclosed:** Open issue **#906** (created 2025-05-12) reports that an active VPN bypasses the blocklist even after installation/resetting attempts; a September commenter linked the FAQ rather than announcing a repair. The official FAQ states SelfControl “will not block websites properly” with a VPN, calls proper VPN blocking technically infeasible for the project, and warns that corporate/university partial VPNs can save/restore network state and make an expired block return. This is an architectural limitation and possible bypass, not a newly discovered defect. [#906](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/906); [official FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **Firefox DoH/browser-state sensitivity is official:** The FAQ says Firefox’s DNS-over-HTTPS behavior can make blocks unreliable or fail entirely and instructs users to quit/restart Firefox after every block starts. The independent Safari-versus-Chromium/Firefox comments in #680 further show why a blocked-domain report must specify browser, cache/DNS state, and version rather than being generalized to macOS-wide coverage. [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ); [#680](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680)
- **STALE recurring start failure, still open but not current-proof:** In **#729** (created 2021-10-08), several users reported 4.x hanging on “Starting Block”; the original reporter said waiting 30 minutes did not help while **3.0.3** worked. The maintainer suggested that one case might merely be very slow, separated other reporters’ different defects, and later cited the old macOS/hardware as a possible factor. The issue remained open when last updated in 2021, so it documents a historical multi-user problem, not verified behavior of the currently downloaded 4.0.2 on modern macOS. [#729](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/729)
- **STALE recurring expiry/cleanup failure, closed without a demonstrated general fix:** In **#642** (2020), the original reporter said SelfControl stayed “finishing” and that they had been “blocked” for about a week despite deleting/reinstalling and receiving “Success!” from manual block clearing; another user associated recurrence with a VPN, and another later said deleting SelfControl’s `/etc/hosts` entries worked. The maintainer specifically asked whether sites were actually inaccessible or only the app still said a block was running, and the public thread does not answer that question. He offered recovery/debugging steps and explicitly called out VPN incompatibility for another commenter. GitHub marks #642 closed/completed on 2020-10-03, but the visible discussion provides neither a code fix nor proof that the original machine recovered; later comments must not be silently folded into a “fixed” resolution. [#642](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/642)
- **Isolated total-connectivity failure on a named current-app version:** On 2024-08-02, a **SelfControl 4.0.2 / macOS Ventura 13.0.1** user said that rebooting with 77 hours left caused **all** Internet traffic to remain blocked across browsers and Wi-Fi networks, with no VPN. Issue **#879** was open with no comments or diagnosis at the cutoff; its severity is potentially high, but it is one firsthand report and does not establish prevalence or root cause. [#879](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/879)

**LeechBlock NG — setup errors, MV3/Android, sync, and resource use**

- **Official documentation predicts common configuration failures:** The FAQ’s “Why is nothing being blocked?” checklist (updated 2025-02-09) lists an empty site list, protocol prefixes such as `http://` in entries, and other setup mistakes; the documentation exposes numerous per-block-set time/exception/access-control options. This supports a learnability/configuration complaint but means a user saying “nothing is blocked” is not automatically reporting a blocking-engine failure. [Nothing-blocked FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/nothing-blocked/); [documentation](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/documentation/)
- **Maintainer-confirmed Android/MV3 availability problem with a partial, explicit resolution:** In **#337** (2023-12), a user could no longer find/install LeechBlock from Firefox for Android after the update. The maintainer uploaded **1.6.1**, marked it Android-compatible, and closed the issue after confirming availability on 2023-12-12—but explicitly said the timer still might not appear until an extension page was opened. The closure therefore proves restored store availability, **not** resolution of the remaining Android timer defect. [#337](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/337)
- **Recurring Android runtime symptoms linked by maintainer to MV3:** A **1.6.3 / Samsung M30 / Firefox Mobile and Beta** user in **#404** said “Immediately block sites once blocking conditions are met” failed until reload and the timer did not display. Opening LeechBlock’s add-on menu temporarily restored both, reproducing the wake-up behavior described in #337; the maintainer linked #390 and a Mozilla bug and said there was no general workaround at that time. #404 was closed on 2025-01-19, but the retrieved discussion does not identify a fix commit/version, so closure alone is not current-fix evidence. [#404](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/404); [related #390](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/390); [Mozilla bug 1872600](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1872600)
- **MV3 migration and active later maintenance are both verified:** LeechBlock’s own history records the MV3 migration in **1.6**; issue **#387** explicitly discusses the migration. At the cutoff the history listed **1.8 (2026-09-27)**, after 1.7.x releases with additional fixes and platform options. Thus early 1.6 Android problems are important evidence, but they must not be used to claim that LeechBlock never migrated or that no later maintenance occurred. [Version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md); [#387](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/387)
- **Incognito desynchronization has an identified fix, unlike loose anecdotes:** Issue **#432** documents inconsistent blocking/state in private/incognito operation. The maintainer diagnosed and closed it with commit **`79420c7`**, which is explicit resolution evidence; the report should not be cited as a current unfixed incognito defect without a newer reproduction and version. [#432](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/432); [fix commit 79420c7](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/commit/79420c7)
- **Open experimental-sync complaint across two desktop OSes:** In **#623** (created 2025-08-08), a Windows/Mac user said time limits did not transfer between devices. The maintainer proposed closing/reopening browsers in a sequence to force storage updates; the user tested both directions and reported no time-allowance transfer. On 2025-08-21 the maintainer wrote, “I don’t know why it isn’t working” and reiterated that it had always been an experimental feature. The issue remains open, making this a maintainer-acknowledged but unexplained single-case failure—not a proven universal sync outage. [#623](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/623)
- **STALE recurring settings loss with explicit mitigation, not guaranteed eradication:** **#63** and duplicate **#75** contain several independent 2018–2019 reports of blocklists/options disappearing, especially on Linux/Firefox after crashes, power loss, or unclean shutdown; updates were only a maintainer/user hypothesis, not a proven universal trigger. Version **0.9.10 (2019-03-19)** included changes the maintainer hoped would “reduce, if not altogether eliminate” the issue. #63 was then closed with “Reopen if the problem persists,” so these old issues prove historical loss and mitigation, not current recurrence or a guaranteed permanent fix. [#63](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/63); [#75](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/75); [version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md)
- **Maintainer-diagnosed performance case depends on configuration size:** In **#533** (2025-01-11), a Firefox user reported pages/options failing to load and browser shutdown hangs, then connected the problem to massive text in four block-set lists. The maintainer explained that LeechBlock converts a list into a regular expression and repeatedly tests tab URLs against what can become an “extremely complex” expression. The issue was closed after this explanation; it supports a large-list performance limitation, not a general claim that ordinary LeechBlock installations have high energy use. [#533](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/533); [performance FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/slow-browser/)
- **Browser/private-mode anti-bypass limits are disclosed:** The official FAQ says an extension generally cannot prevent its own removal in Chrome and instead describes enterprise policy force-installation for managed machines. It also says LeechBlock cannot disable private browsing. These are platform/enforcement boundaries, not bugs; they explain why removing the extension or opening an uncontrolled browsing mode can defeat browser-only protection. [Extension-removal FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/remove-extension-chrome/); [private-browsing FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/private-browsing/)

**StayFocusd — current isolated complaints and MV3 uncertainty**

- **Recent timer complaint, single anecdote and version-qualified:** A mirror review dated **2026-11-04** for **StayFocusd 4.6.22** says, “Doesn't work at all. Timer always on pause.” The official store listing subsequently showed **4.6.24, updated 2026-11-07**, but neither source identifies the timer’s cause or says 4.6.24 fixes this report. The symptom is potentially **high severity** because time accounting is central to the product, yet one review cannot establish recurrence, a regression rate, or persistence in the newest build. [Aggregated review page](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews?q=timer); [official store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)
- **Recent incognito/removal bypass allegation, also isolated:** A mirror review dated **2026-10-26** for **4.6.18** alleges that incognito and removing the extension enabled bypass. This is direct firsthand evidence that a reviewer believed the controls were easy to evade, not independent technical reproduction, evidence of all incognito configurations, or proof the behavior remains in 4.6.24. [Review aggregator](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews); [current official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)
- **MV3 is current fact; its connection to current timer complaints is unproven:** The official listing identifies StayFocusd as **Manifest V3**. Separately, in January 2022 StayFocusd’s developer opened a W3C ServiceWorker request for a persistent worker to run `setInterval()`, warning that MV3’s move from background pages to service workers would “kill the app” because per-second accounting could be interrupted by suspension. That issue establishes a developer-identified **architectural risk in 2022**, not a diagnosis of the 2026 paused-timer review or permission to attribute all current failures to MV3. [Official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji); [w3c/ServiceWorker #1624](https://github.com/w3c/ServiceWorker/issues/1624)
- **Evidence of active maintenance, but not of specific fixes:** The store’s 4.6.24 / 2026-11-07 metadata establishes a very recent update. The store text also advertises time restrictions, active days/hours, analytics, component blocking on major social platforms, and synchronization of usage limits with Android devices/other browsers. It supplies no public per-release changelog tied to the two cited complaints, so release recency cannot be converted into a claim that either was repaired. [Official Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)

### Inferences

- The most robust recurring pattern is **boundary failure**: SelfControl intersects with VPN/DoH, shared CDN addresses, browser caches, and native apps; LeechBlock intersects with MV3 worker lifecycles on Android and browser sync/storage; Plucky intersects with browser-extension support and system-wide network enforcement.
- Symptom recurrence is not root-cause recurrence. For example, Safari-only self-control failures, a native WhatsApp connection, and a YouTube navigation path can all appear under “listed site not blocked” while requiring different explanations.
- Strictness and usability are coupled. Plucky’s broad default media/system blocking and SelfControl’s server-level enforcement can stop desired work just as effectively as distractions; those are sometimes documented tradeoffs rather than fixable false positives.
- LeechBlock has unusually useful resolution nuance: #337’s closure fixed availability but left a timer issue, #404’s closure lacks visible fix evidence, and #432 has a concrete commit. Treating all closed issues alike would erase these distinctions.
- StayFocusd should be described as **actively updated but weakly diagnosed in public evidence**. Neither the 2022 MV3 warning nor the 2026 store update supplies the missing causal bridge to a particular review.
- Plucky’s Chrome 138 workaround exchanges extension compatibility for inability to update the browser; that is a materially different support proposition from normal support for a maintained Chromium release.

### Gaps

- There is no representative denominator, telemetry sample, or controlled reproduction set from which to calculate failure rates, typical CPU/RAM use, or the percentage of users affected by any complaint.
- Plucky evidence is dominated by official manuals/release notes rather than a rich current firsthand complaint sample. No reliable current OS/version-specific recurrence rate was found for its outages, Android slowness, or sync behavior.
- SelfControl’s meta-issues aggregate different sites, macOS releases, browsers, and possible causes over many years. Open status and comment recency do not verify every historical site against 4.0.2 today.
- LeechBlock #404 lacks a visible closure explanation in the retrieved discussion, so its behavior on current 1.8/Firefox Android remains unresolved here; #623 is one unexplained case, not a measured sync-failure rate.
- StayFocusd has no comparable first-party public issue tracker or detailed release notes in the examined sources. Mirror reviews often omit OS, browser build, settings, screenshots, logs, and developer responses, and no independent reproduction of either recent review was found.
- No reliable source established current StayFocusd false-positive/CDN behavior, measurable performance overhead, or the actual reliability of its advertised cross-device sync.

## Which severe claims (unrecoverable lock, lost work, inability to uninstall, broken browser, unexpected charge) have direct firsthand evidence versus hearsay?

### Takeaway

The strongest severe evidence is narrow: SelfControl users directly reported week-long/total connectivity and cleanup failures, while Plucky’s own notes confirm historical post-uninstall/blackout/configuration-loss defects with explicit fixes. LeechBlock’s clearest lockout risk is a warned consequence of its optional access controls, and StayFocusd’s recent reviews establish malfunction/bypass allegations rather than catastrophic loss. No researched source independently verified permanent loss of work files, unrecoverable installation, or an unexpected recurring charge for these four products.

### Cited Findings

**Plucky — vendor-confirmed historical severe defects versus intended delay enforcement**

- **Historical inability to uninstall cleanly on macOS — confirmed and fixed, STALE:** Version **1.13.18 (2022-11-02)** says that on **macOS 13.1** Plucky should “uninstall completely instead of continuing to block the Internet after being uninstalled!” The note says it was unknown whether 13.0 was also affected. This is direct vendor confirmation of a potentially **high-severity historical defect**, an explicit fixed version, and a precisely bounded OS qualifier—not firsthand proof that all uninstalls failed or that current macOS remains affected. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)
- **Historical block-everything persistence — confirmed and fixed, STALE:** Version **1.16.13 (2024-04-19)** explicitly fixes a block-everything state lingering after reboot. Together with the current emergency troubleshooting workflow, this supports treating total-connectivity complaints as technically plausible, but the dated fix means it cannot be cited as an unresolved 2026 defect. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes); [troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting)
- **Historical configuration loss, not lost user documents:** Version **1.0.56 (2021-06-23)** says a “horrible upgrade bug” introduced in **1.0.52** wiped local configuration; users who had synchronized configurations were unaffected. This is official evidence of severe settings loss and recovery advantage from sync, not evidence that Plucky deleted documents, photos, or other work files. The old affected/fixed versions must remain attached. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)
- **Intentional uninstall delay is recoverable in principle, not permanent malware-like persistence:** The manual states that with nonzero delay, greater-access changes—including reducing delay and uninstalling—are delayed, and “uninstalling is only permitted when the delay is 0.” It recommends a 10-second delay on the first day and documents expedite approvals. This behavior can feel like a lockout, but it is the core advertised self-control mechanism; a defect would need separate evidence that access remains blocked **after** the applicable delay/workflow completes. [Delay documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/delay)
- **Emergency recovery is conditional rather than a guaranteed instant escape:** For users with delay over one hour who have lost Internet and registered on the user site, Plucky documents `pluck - system` followed by `pluck expedite`, then adding a note to the request. That supplies an official recovery path, but it depends on configuration/synchronization and approval rather than disproving every alleged stuck state. [Troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting)
- **No firsthand catastrophic Plucky case was located:** The sources verify official historical failures and documented all-access recovery, but do not supply a current, independently reproduced case of destroyed work, permanent device damage, or an impossible uninstall after following the documented delay/expedite process. Those stronger conclusions belong in the gaps, not in a summary of the release notes. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes); [troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting)

**SelfControl — direct severe reports, with design limits and uncertainty separated**

- **The advertised irreversible timer is not itself a defect:** The official site says a running block remains despite restarting the computer or deleting the application; the FAQ says to wait for expiry before uninstalling because one cannot uninstall during a running block or stop it early. This is potentially **high-impact intended behavior**. It explains why deletion does not restore access and why testing a short block matters, but does not excuse a block persisting beyond expiry. [Official site/download page](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **Direct firsthand week-long stuck-block account — STALE and unresolved in public:** In #642 the original user reported being “blocked” for about a week, with deletion/reinstallation and the manual clearer reporting success but not removing the visible problem. The thread did not establish whether this meant continued site inaccessibility or only a stuck application state. Other users described similar symptoms and different workarounds, one involving `/etc/hosts`; the maintainer requested logs and identified VPN as the likely cause for a different commenter. There is no published diagnostic finding proving one shared cause, and closure is not proof the original case was fixed. [#642](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/642)
- **Direct total-Internet failure on 4.0.2/Ventura — isolated and undiagnosed:** The #879 reporter directly said all traffic was blocked after reboot during a 77-hour block, despite no VPN and changes of browser/network. This is stronger than hearsay because it is a named-version firsthand account, but no maintainer response, logs, or independent reproduction establishes whether SelfControl, another filter, or network state caused it. Its open state alone does not prove persistence to 2026. [#879](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/879)
- **“Permanently breaks iCloud Private Relay” is a reporter’s title, not an established permanent defect:** Open issue **#852** (created 2023-10-02) alleges Private Relay remained broken even after SelfControl was uninstalled. Another user suggested deleting/re-adding the network interface; there is no visible maintainer diagnosis or proof of permanence on every machine. The report is firsthand evidence of a severe network-cleanup allegation, while the word “permanently” and the proposed workaround remain unverified. [#852](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/852)
- **Direct work blockage is not the same as lost work:** The PayPal-assets, Terraform, Python-hosting, and other #681 reports directly describe services needed for work becoming unavailable. None of the cited comments demonstrates corrupted files or irreversible data loss. Shared-server/domain overblocking supplies a plausible mechanism, but individual cases still need network/version evidence before being labeled maintainer-confirmed defects. [#681](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681); [PayPal report](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-1281616886); [shared-IP report](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681#issuecomment-1801326223)
- **Official long-block design amplifies severe mistakes:** SelfControl hides durations beyond 24 hours because, in the FAQ’s words, it is “really easy to screw up longer blocks”; it explicitly imagines accidentally blocking something critical and waiting a long time for volunteer support. This is unusually direct documentation of the lockout/support tradeoff, not evidence of an unexpected software malfunction. It also reinforces why collateral server/domain blocking can have high severity during a long irreversible timer. [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **Old multi-user start failure did not establish permanent lockout:** In #729, version 3.0.3 continued working for the original reporter when 4.x would not start, and the maintainer proposed a slow-start explanation before later acknowledging uncertainty. The severe interpretation “4.x permanently locks the Mac” is unsupported by that thread; the supported complaint is failure to initiate reliably on certain old configurations. [#729](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/729)

**LeechBlock NG — direct settings loss, self-created option locks, and limited catastrophic evidence**

- **Official accidental-lockout warning concerns settings access, not irreversible OS damage:** The FAQ explains that block-set options become disabled when the user selected prevention of access to options during blocking, and the documentation warns users to consider carefully how passwords/access restrictions interact with active blocks. This can produce a practical **self-created configuration lockout**; it is an intended access-control tradeoff and should not be described as LeechBlock spontaneously damaging the browser or making the computer permanently unusable. [Options-disabled FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/options-disabled/); [documentation](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/documentation/)
- **Direct recurring historical loss of blocklists/settings — STALE:** #63’s original Xubuntu/Firefox 59 user lost settings after initially successful reinstallation; other Linux/Firefox 60–61 users independently reported losses around crashes/power cuts or without identifying a trigger. #75 contains additional Windows/Waterfox, Ubuntu/Firefox, and Fedora reports. The affected artifacts were LeechBlock settings/blocklists, not user work files; maintainers did not establish one root cause. [#63](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/63); [#75](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/75)
- **Backup recommendation and 0.9.10 mitigation limit the severe-claim interpretation:** The maintainer explicitly said LeechBlock did not make its own backup in 2018 and recommended “Export Options to File”; 0.9.10 later attempted to reduce/eliminate losses. The historical absence of internal backup plus disappearing protection explains frustration, but the mitigation and absence of current corroboration in this research preclude describing settings loss as an active 1.8 defect. [#75](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/75); [#63](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/63); [version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md)
- **Current sync case shows inconsistent enforcement, not demonstrated permanent loss:** #623’s user directly reported time allowances failing to move between PC and Mac after following the maintainer’s sequence. That can weaken cross-device blocking, but it does not itself prove erased settings, an unrecoverable lock, or damage to the machines. The maintainer explicitly lacked an explanation. [#623](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/623)
- **Private-mode and uninstall limitations undermine strictness, not system integrity:** Officially, LeechBlock cannot prevent extension removal in ordinary unmanaged Chrome or disable private browsing. Those are bypass/enforcement limits, not evidence that the extension cannot itself be removed at the browser/OS level or that it damages the browser. Force-install policy is a managed-environment way to make removal harder, not a consumer recovery trap. [Removal FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/remove-extension-chrome/); [private-browsing FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/private-browsing/)
- **No direct severe current LeechBlock case was found beyond configuration/data-state consequences:** The examined current issues and documentation did not verify lost documents, browser corruption requiring OS restoration, a permanently impossible uninstall, or an unexpected charge. #533 describes severe browser hangs but was explained by user-supplied huge lists and closed after the diagnosis, so it should not be promoted to an unresolved malicious-persistence claim. [#533](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/533); [version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md)

**StayFocusd — recent direct reviews do not substantiate the strongest catastrophic categories**

- **Direct paused-timer report is severe functionally but limited as evidence:** The 2026-11-04 reviewer’s statement that the timer was always paused directly alleges that the core restriction failed on 4.6.22. It contains no evidence of data loss, system damage, unwanted charges, or a permanent lock, and 4.6.24’s later release cannot be assumed either affected or fixed. [Review mirror](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews?q=timer); [official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)
- **Direct bypass allegation is not an inability to uninstall:** The 2026-10-26/4.6.18 reviewer alleges incognito and extension removal as bypasses. Even if reproduced, that would show inadequate tamper resistance, **not** that StayFocusd prevented uninstallation or permanently broke Chrome. The examined evidence does not supply browser/OS details or developer confirmation. [Review mirror](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews)
- **The 2022 developer warning is not a firsthand 2026 breakage report:** The W3C issue is direct evidence that StayFocusd’s developer anticipated a severe MV3 service-worker lifecycle problem. It is not evidence that users subsequently lost files, could not remove Chrome/the extension, or experienced a particular paused timer because of MV3. [w3c/ServiceWorker #1624](https://github.com/w3c/ServiceWorker/issues/1624)
- **Ad/tracking accusations are unverified allegations, not established system damage:** Some mirror reviewers accuse the extension of injecting advertising or tracking behavior, but the examined reviews did not include reproducible network captures, code analysis, or independent security research. Such comments belong in an allegations/privacy discussion; they do not prove malware, lost work, unexpected payment, or an unremovable browser modification. [Review aggregator](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews); [official listing’s privacy description](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)

**Unexpected charges and irreversible loss across all four**

- **No direct unexpected-charge case was found:** Searches of the examined official documentation, issue histories, store/privacy pages, and collected reviews did not locate a firsthand report clearly documenting an unauthorized or unexpected recurring charge for Plucky, SelfControl, LeechBlock NG, or StayFocusd. This is an absence of found evidence, not proof that no billing dispute exists anywhere. [SelfControl official site](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [LeechBlock official site](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/); [Plucky manual](https://docs.pluckeye.net/overview); [StayFocusd store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)
- **The direct evidence of “loss” is primarily access and settings, not destroyed work:** SelfControl’s collateral/total-network reports concern inaccessible services, Plucky’s historical 1.0.52 issue concerns local configuration, and LeechBlock’s old #63/#75 issues concern extension options. The reviewed sources do not independently establish wholesale deletion/corruption of documents or an irreversible OS installation for any of the four. [SelfControl #681](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681); [Plucky release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes); [LeechBlock #63](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/63)

### Inferences

- **“Cannot stop/uninstall during an active block” and “cannot ever recover” must be separate categories.** The former is an advertised strictness feature in Plucky and SelfControl; the latter would require evidence of failure after expiry or the documented recovery workflow.
- The strongest firsthand severe reports are not automatically the strongest diagnoses. #879 and #852 describe serious symptoms but have no maintainer/root-cause confirmation; Plucky’s historical release notes provide less narrative but much stronger defect-and-fix confirmation.
- Lost configuration can be severe in this product category because it silently removes protection and may be time-consuming to reconstruct. It remains categorically different from destroyed personal/work files.
- A blocker that is easy to remove and a blocker that cannot be removed are opposite complaints. StayFocusd/LeechBlock’s bypass allegations should not be blended with Plucky/SelfControl’s intentional removal resistance.
- No unexpected-charge evidence should not be transformed into a positive guarantee about every payment channel; it only means this specific severe theme lacked a sourced case in the examined material.

### Gaps

- No forensic logs, packet captures for most severe cases, system images, receipts, charge statements, or independent reproductions were available for the strongest allegations.
- It is unknown whether the original #642 machine recovered, whether #879 persisted beyond its initial report, and whether #852’s network-interface workaround was validated by the original reporter or maintainer.
- No current Plucky firsthand case was found that tests whether nonzero-delay/expedite recovery fails after correctly following the documented process; historical fixed release notes cannot answer current incidence.
- LeechBlock’s optional option-lock warning establishes possibility and documented semantics, but no current firsthand permanent lockout with exact settings/version and eventual recovery outcome was located.
- StayFocusd mirror reviews lack the configuration, Chrome/OS build, reproduction steps, and responses needed to test the timer/bypass claims; no technical substantiation was found for ad/tracking accusations.
- No verified count of affected users, duration of outage, actual financial loss, or permanent work-product loss was found for any product.

## What company policies or support practices amplify frustration, and what do users still dislike despite praising effectiveness?

### Takeaway

The main amplifier is the collision between intentional friction and limited recovery/support: Plucky delays relaxation, SelfControl makes timers irreversible while warning of months-long volunteer replies, and LeechBlock lets users lock their own options behind complex settings. Maintenance is not uniformly stagnant—LeechBlock 1.8 and StayFocusd 4.6.24 are recent, Plucky documentation is current but Chromium support is constrained, and SelfControl has merged work plus an unreleased rewrite—yet current release activity does not eliminate unresolved usability, privacy-clarity, or compatibility concerns.

### Cited Findings

**Plucky — strict recovery, narrow browser support, and configuration/sync burden**

- **Support scope is deliberately narrow and can feel dismissive at compatibility boundaries:** The browser manual says unsupported browsers should be assumed not to work and, for undocumented browsers, asks users not to message because there is no time/resources to support every niche derivative. For “accidentally supported” browsers it says not to expect fixes if compatibility disappears. This is transparent resource scoping, but it leaves users outside Edge/Firefox with little supported recovery when behavior changes. [Browser documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/browsers)
- **Delay policy amplifies the cost of mistakes but includes safeguards:** Plucky’s manual explicitly aims at inconvenience, recommends beginning with only a 10-second delay, and provides expedited approvals and per-rule delay patterns. A broad `block everything`/system setup combined with a long delay therefore creates high recovery friction by design; the initial short-delay advice and expedite route are counterevidence to a claim that the project offers no safety guidance. [Overview](https://docs.pluckeye.net/overview); [delay documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/delay); [troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting)
- **Configuration portability/support is split across local UI, CLI, and a hosted user site:** The manual identifies those three configuration modes; troubleshooting advises saving configuration before uninstall/reinstall and notes that successful user-site setup makes a separate manual export unnecessary. This gives recovery options but means users must understand which configuration source is authoritative and whether synchronization succeeded. The fixed 1.14.10–1.14.29 remote-change bug illustrates why that distinction matters without proving a current sync failure. [Configuration documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/how-to-configure); [troubleshooting](https://docs.pluckeye.net/troubleshooting); [release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)
- **Current Chromium policy imposes a particularly costly support tradeoff:** Users who want Plucky’s prior Chrome integration are directed to legacy Chrome 138 with updates blocked indefinitely, while normal Chrome/Brave/Vivaldi support is partial. This is more than a documentation caveat: the user must choose supported Edge/Firefox behavior, degraded current Chromium behavior, or a frozen browser. It is a design/support constraint established by MV2 removal, not evidence that maintainers can restore normal Chrome support through a small bug fix. [Browser documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/browsers); [Chrome documentation](https://docs.pluckeye.net/chrome)
- **Current Android limitations remain despite ongoing documentation/status work:** Instructions to use one or two tabs, keep configurations small, and expect old-browser/site incompatibilities directly limit day-to-day usability. The same page says a new browsing solution is in beta, so “no work is occurring” would be inaccurate, but a beta promise is not evidence that the released Android problems are resolved. [Plucky on Android](https://docs.pluckeye.net/pluckeye-on-android)
- **Historical fixes show maintenance responses but not user praise or current quality:** The release notes explicitly repair lingering blackout, configuration sync, intermittent outages, macOS uninstall, and update/configuration-loss issues. These entries show maintainers addressing high-impact failures; they do not supply an independently verified satisfaction rate or prove all old complaints disappeared. The strongest paired “still disliked despite effectiveness” evidence for Plucky was not found, so official tradeoffs should not be relabeled user testimonials. [Release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)

**SelfControl — volunteer support, manual portability, privacy choices, and maintenance lag**

- **The support policy explicitly warns of months-long replies:** The FAQ says the free project is maintained almost entirely by one volunteer, who eventually tries to answer issues/email but whose response time “could be months” during busy periods or trips, and directs users to self-help through the Support Hub. In #729 the maintainer said there was no support staff and later estimated at least a six-month issue backlog at his then-current pace. This can greatly amplify an irreversible-block mistake; it is also clear disclosure of volunteer capacity rather than evidence of intentional neglect. [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ); [#729 maintainer exchange](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/729#issuecomment-988288869)
- **Irreversibility plus long-block hiding is an explicit risk-management policy:** The official homepage promises that restart/deletion will not end a block. The FAQ hides durations over 24 hours because users can accidentally block critical resources and then wait for volunteer assistance. This policy intentionally favors commitment over convenience, while making shared-server/domain mistakes much more consequential; the recommendation to test a short block is the project’s principal preventative safeguard. [Official site](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **Platform support is intentionally limited:** The official download is a macOS application, and the FAQ specifically says iOS is too restricted and that App Store/GPL constraints prevent an official iOS version. This is a platform limitation, not cross-device failure; users who assume the desktop app’s irreversible system-level model extends to phones may be disappointed. [Official site](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **List transfer is documented as manual rather than seamless cloud sync:** The FAQ tells users to export using **File > Save Blocklist** and import on another machine with **File > Open Blocklist**, replacing the current list. This permits reuse/backup but adds operational burden compared with automatic multi-device state synchronization; no evidence here establishes automatic timer/list sync between computers. [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)
- **Users can explicitly praise the app while still finding a crucial failure unacceptable:** A 2025-04-22 #680 commenter wrote “I love the app” while reporting a site that remained unblocked; the 2026-08-30 YouTube reporter likewise said “I love the app but there’s one crucial issue” and described needing the blocker for addiction. These are direct examples of residual dissatisfaction from beneficiaries, not evidence that the product has universally failed or that praise cancels the reported bypass. [2025 comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680#issuecomment-2822049003); [2026 comment](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680#issuecomment-5469668557)
- **Privacy collection is optional and more nuanced than “zero data”:** Since version 4.0, the official policy says SelfControl can send anonymized system/update-check data and anonymized Sentry error reports. The latter may include settings such as block duration/type but not the blocklist or identifying details, and both categories can be disabled in preferences. This is the developer’s disclosure rather than an independent audit; it supports informed opt-out, not either extreme claim of mandatory full browsing-history collection or absolutely no network telemetry. [SelfControl privacy policy](https://selfcontrolapp.com/privacy)
- **Maintenance evidence is mixed: old official download, active work, unreleased rewrite:** The official site still distributes **SelfControl 4.0.2** for macOS. Touch ID pull request **#918** was merged on **2025-11-29**, and a maintainer said on **2026-10-20** that a rewrite was essentially complete but had not been released. Therefore “completely unmaintained” is contradicted, while “the rewrite fixes current user issues” would also be unsupported because it was not released and no affected-issue resolution was attached. [Official download page](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [PR #918](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/pull/918)
- **Open meta-issues are support organization, not resolution evidence:** #680 and #681 consolidate distinct site-specific reports so users can supply a standard debugging template. Their continued activity makes long-standing problems visible, but comments range from actionable browser/version reports to weak or unrelated submissions; maintainers have not confirmed every proposed domain or community reverse-proxy solution. [#680](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/680); [#681](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/681)

**LeechBlock NG — feature complexity, experimental sync, privacy posture, and active fixes**

- **Complexity is simultaneously the product’s strength and a support burden:** The documentation offers many block-set combinations and access restrictions, while the FAQ has a dedicated checklist for common “nothing blocked” mistakes and another for users who disabled their own options. Users can create precise enforcement, but mistakes can remove protection or lock configuration without a browser/system defect. The project’s examples/access-control warnings mitigate this but do not make state semantics trivial. [Documentation](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/documentation/); [examples](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/examples/); [nothing-blocked FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/nothing-blocked/); [options-disabled FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/options-disabled/)
- **Sync is explicitly experimental and its open support case ended without diagnosis:** The maintainer’s #623 response did not promise a fix; after the user’s test failed, he said he did not know why and emphasized experimental status. That candor is useful, but users relying on cross-device time accounting may reasonably find the limitation frustrating because a visible option does not guarantee coherent enforcement. [#623](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/623)
- **Backup guidance is a direct response to costly historical settings loss:** In #75 the maintainer recommended exporting options because LeechBlock itself did not make a backup in 2018. #63 later announced 0.9.10 mitigation and invited reopening if losses persisted. This demonstrates responsive troubleshooting but also places routine export responsibility on the user and does not supply proof that every storage-loss path was eliminated. [#75](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/75); [#63](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/63)
- **A grateful user still disliked unreliable sync:** The #623 reporter opened with “Thank you for helping people with your extension :)” before documenting failed bidirectional PC/Mac allowance transfer. This is direct paired praise/complaint evidence, though it remains one support exchange and should not be generalized to overall satisfaction. [#623](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/623)
- **Privacy claim is narrow and developer-stated:** The official FAQ says LeechBlock does not collect, store, or transmit personal information other than settings entered by the user, such as blocked sites, and explains that it may access page content for features such as keyword blocking. This is not an independent audit, but it provides a first-party basis for evaluating concerns about browsing/page access without treating necessary extension permissions as proof of surveillance. [LeechBlock privacy FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/privacy/)
- **Performance support appropriately asks for attribution before assigning blame:** The slow-browser FAQ first recommends disabling LeechBlock to determine whether it is actually responsible; #533 then demonstrates a specific mechanism in which very large lists generate complex regular expressions and repeated URL tests. This is a useful evidence-based support practice, but also means users importing huge lists bear a significant configuration/performance burden. [Slow-browser FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/slow-browser/); [#533](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/533)
- **Anti-bypass expectations must account for browser ownership policies:** LeechBlock cannot itself stop unmanaged Chrome uninstall or disable private browsing; its suggested stronger uninstall protection requires external enterprise policy. This can frustrate users expecting standalone tamper-proof enforcement, but the project explains the boundary instead of claiming a browser extension can override its host. [Removal FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/remove-extension-chrome/); [private-browsing FAQ](https://www.proginosko.com/leechblock/faq/private-browsing/)
- **Recent version history contradicts an abandonment narrative:** The history lists **1.8 on 2026-09-27**, 1.7.3 on 2026-08-04, and 1.7.2 on 2026-01-04, including localization, managed storage, blocking/timer options, and bug fixes. It establishes continuing releases but does not itemize a resolution for #623 or prove that every Android/MV3 symptom is gone. [Version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md)
- **MV3 support communication exposes upstream dependency:** #337’s maintainer explicitly restored Android availability in 1.6.1 while retaining the known timer limitation, and #404 points to Mozilla’s handling/bug report rather than promising an extension-only workaround. Users experience a product defect/bypass regardless of whether upstream Firefox or LeechBlock owns the cause; the public response helps explain why “just update” may not resolve all mobile behavior. [#337](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/337); [#404](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/404)

**StayFocusd — active store maintenance, broad permissions/features, and privacy/support ambiguity**

- **The official listing promises broad control and cross-device visibility:** StayFocusd advertises restricting time on distracting sites, active days/hours, analytics, social-platform component blocking, and syncing usage limits across Android devices/other browsers. These features increase configuration and permission surface area, but the listing is a capability claim—not independent evidence that every component block or sync operation works reliably. [Official Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)
- **Recent maintenance is clear; support resolution is not:** The official listing showed **4.6.24, updated 2026-11-07**, and directs questions/problems to StayFocusd support. The two cited reviews concern older builds and do not record a support outcome, so there is no basis either to say support ignored them or to claim the newest release resolved them. [Official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji); [review mirror](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews)
- **Store and privacy-policy statements have different scopes and should be read together:** The store text says StayFocusd “does not collect personal data from the web pages you visit,” while its privacy-practices disclosure says the developer handles **web history, user activity, and website content**. Sensor Tower’s multi-app panel policy, effective **2026-09-23**, says users may consent to collection of some personal information and describes use of anonymized data. These formulations are not automatically contradictory—permission/data-category disclosures, personal-webpage-content claims, consent, and anonymization refer to different things—but they can be difficult for a user to reconcile without an extension-specific data-flow explanation. [Official store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji); [Sensor Tower policy](https://sensortower.com/page-stayfocusd-privacy)
- **Review claims of ads/tracking must not override official disclosures without technical evidence:** The mirror contains unverified reviewer allegations about advertising/tracking; the official listing and Sensor Tower policy provide the developer’s side. No independent code audit or network trace was found in this research, so the supportable conclusion is **privacy concern/uncertainty among some reviewers**, not confirmed undisclosed tracking and not a categorical clean bill of privacy. [Review mirror](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews); [official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji); [Sensor Tower policy](https://sensortower.com/page-stayfocusd-privacy)
- **MV3 development constraints are publicly acknowledged, but no current remediation path is documented in the cited issue:** In 2022 the developer sought persistent timing capability from the web platform and was pointed to prior ServiceWorker discussion. The 2026 MV3 store listing shows the product continued shipping, but neither source explains how current per-second accounting is implemented or whether the old risk was fully mitigated. This uncertainty can amplify user distrust when a paused-timer complaint appears, without establishing causation. [w3c/ServiceWorker #1624](https://github.com/w3c/ServiceWorker/issues/1624); [official listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)
- **No robust paired praise-and-dislike sample was established for StayFocusd:** The recent mirror reports document timer/bypass and other dissatisfaction, but this research did not verify a comparable current review that both praises effectiveness and identifies a specific remaining dislike with version/configuration detail. Such a nuanced claim should not be invented from the overall store rating or from unrelated positive reviews. [Review mirror](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews)

### Inferences

- **Support design is part of blocking safety.** A strict blocker with delayed/irreversible changes needs particularly clear defaults, short test periods, recovery documentation, and realistic support expectations; without them, an intended feature becomes the source of severe frustration.
- Plucky and SelfControl disclose unusually high-friction behavior rather than hiding it. Disclosure does not remove the usability cost, but it changes the ethical classification from unexpected malfunction to a known tradeoff unless the behavior exceeds documented bounds.
- “Open source” does not imply staffed support or rapid fixes. SelfControl explicitly describes one-volunteer/months-long support; LeechBlock and Plucky also expose limited compatibility resources. Conversely, merged pull requests, current documentation, and recent releases prevent a blanket abandonment conclusion.
- User praise often reflects the same strictness that generates complaints: beneficiaries can value strong enforcement while rejecting a single bypass, collateral work blockage, or failed sync as a critical exception.
- StayFocusd’s privacy discussion is primarily a **clarity/evidence problem** in these sources. Store declarations, a cross-app policy, and unsupported reviewer allegations answer different questions and should not be collapsed into either “privacy violation proved” or “no data concern possible.”
- Recent releases are necessary maintenance evidence but weak resolution evidence. Product-specific changelogs, issue-linked commits, and affected-user confirmation are much stronger than an “updated on” date alone.

### Gaps

- No response-time statistics, support SLA, satisfaction survey, representative praise/complaint sample, or independent privacy/security audit was found for these projects.
- Plucky’s documentation does not, in the reviewed sources, provide an independent current privacy audit or enough firsthand reviews to establish what satisfied users most dislike; its hosted configuration sync should not be assigned unverified data-retention claims.
- SelfControl’s merged Touch ID work and “essentially complete” rewrite do not reveal a release date or which of #680/#681/#879/#852 will be fixed; the privacy policy is a developer disclosure rather than audit evidence.
- LeechBlock’s FAQ/privacy statements do not measure actual browser-vendor sync behavior or independently verify data handling; current 1.8 release notes do not close the evidence gap for the specific open sync case or all historical Android issues.
- StayFocusd lacks examined public per-version changelogs and first-party review responses tying fixes/support to the cited complaints. Its cross-app policy does not by itself enumerate the exact extension-specific fields, purposes, retention, and consent flow needed to resolve every store/policy ambiguity.
- No verified unexpected-charge policy complaint, current catastrophic-loss case, or generalizable “users love it but hate X” pattern could be established for all four products; such a cross-product conclusion would exceed the available evidence.
