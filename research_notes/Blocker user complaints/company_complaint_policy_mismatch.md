# Company complaint–policy mismatches for 15 blockers

**Research cutoff and source-access date: 2026-11-08.**

## Executive findings

The strongest mismatches are not a uniform pattern of companies breaking their promises. They fall into three different classes:

1. **Disclosed terms users experience as unfair.** Cold Turkey deliberately resists removal; SelfControl deliberately makes an active block irreversible; Plucky deliberately delays relaxation. Those designs can cause serious disruption, but intentional friction is not itself evidence of a policy violation. Failure *after* the documented recovery process would require separate evidence.
2. **Scope and wording gaps.** “Local,” “private,” “offline,” or “free” frequently describes only the core blocking function. Accounts, synced rules, diagnostics, analytics, purchases, support attachments, research, advertising, and cloud features can be exceptions. BlockSite and StayFocusd require particular care because broad official disclosures coexist with narrower reassuring language; the older BlockSite network study cannot establish what the current build does.
3. **Unresolved service and recovery allegations.** Repeated reports describe stale entitlements, post-cancellation charges, stuck blocking, inaccurate timers, lost settings, and unanswered support. Most lack receipts, account chronologies, logs, or independent reproduction. Public responses and fixes matter: FocusMe’s Linux unsaved-work defect, Opal’s same-user Always Allowed repair, RescueTime’s November 2025 sync incident, and several LeechBlock Android defects have resolution evidence; they should not be portrayed as uniformly unresolved.

Apple App Store and Google Play largely control store cancellation and refunds; Paddle/Stripe/top-up and website purchases have different routes. A store denial therefore must not be treated as a developer violating the developer’s website guarantee.

No prevalence estimates are possible from self-selected reviews, issue threads, or support cases. **“Recurring” below means repeated independent-looking reports in the collected sample, not a failure rate, verified identity count, common root cause, or current-version defect.** No located official policy acknowledges systemic overcharging or deceptive behavior. No regulator or court finding establishing the serious accusations was established in the searched record; that scoped absence is not affirmative legal, privacy, or security clearance.

## Evidence and comparison rules

- **Verified policy** means the vendor/store/intermediary text was checked; it does not mean the claim was independently audited.
- **Observed conduct** means a documented incident, developer response, release fix, source inspection, or user-described observation, with its source identified.
- **Unresolved allegation** means firsthand-looking public evidence exists but causation, transaction history, or recovery was not independently established.
- **Resolution evidence** is confined to the symptom, build, and reporter it actually addresses. “Fixed” is not a generic product endorsement.
- **Current-build issue** requires a current or near-current source; older reports remain historical negative evidence. An open issue is not current reproduction; a closed issue is not necessarily a fix.
- Official documents are interested sources for their own promises. Store permissions disclose capability, not observed collection. A privacy policy is not a network audit.
- Direct quotations preserve denominators and qualifiers such as “usually,” “initial purchase,” and “other than settings.” Prices are **USD**, excluding tax, at the cited page on **2026-11-08**, unless noted otherwise. Undated sources are marked **“no visible date; accessed 2026-11-08.”** Source-page “help updated” dates are not silently substituted for an underlying policy’s effective date.
- Historical sources are useful failure warnings, not ownership or current-build proof. In particular, BlockSite research on **6.9.7** is separate from Chrome **8.0.1**, and StayFocusd’s static **4.6.24** inspection is not evidence of runtime transmission.

## Practical reading rule

Treat each blocker as four separate contracts: **the store/payment contract**, **the account/deletion contract**, **the local enforcement contract**, and **the support/recovery contract**. A favorable or unfavorable fact in one does not settle the others. Before reproducing any severe complaint, record product build, OS/browser, configured lock mode, purchase channel, exact date sequence and eventual outcome; without those fields, a complaint is a warning to investigate, not a verified company practice.
