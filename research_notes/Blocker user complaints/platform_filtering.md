# Platform filtering: real user concerns and company issues

**Products:** Google Digital Wellbeing, Apple Screen Time, Net Nanny, and Deledao  
**Research cutoff / retrieval date:** **2026-11-08**

## Scope, evidence rules, and limitations

This note concerns the named products, not every similarly named parental-control or classroom product. In particular:

- **Google Family Link is separate from Digital Wellbeing**; supervised-account integration is noted only where Google explicitly connects them.
- **Apple Classroom, Apple School Manager, and third-party MDM are separate from Screen Time.** Screen Time can coexist with family or device management, but school products are not treated as interchangeable with it.
- **Net Nanny's parent app, Net Nanny Child App, and protected-device software are different components.** An old parent-app store update does not, by itself, prove the state of child-side filtering.
- **Deledao ActiveScan (web filtering), ActiveInstruct (classroom management), and ActivePulse (student wellness) are different product functions.** A complaint about a blocked page is not automatically evidence about wellness alerts or live-tab monitoring.

Evidence labels used below:

- **[User report]** — a firsthand statement in a forum, store review, petition, review site, or student article. These are accounts of experience, not independently reproduced defects.
- **[Official]** — the vendor's documentation, policy, release note, or support advisory. These document intended behavior or the company's position, not necessarily actual reliability.
- **[Secondary / research]** — reporting or security research outside the vendor. A reporter quoting a company response is distinguished from an official support page.
- **[Analysis]** — an inference or testable concern, explicitly not a claim that users experienced it.

Historical bugs stay attached to their reported OS/device/app version. Store ratings, petition signatures, “I have the same question” clicks, and counts of complaints do **not** provide a denominator and cannot establish current failure rates. No broad “usually broken,” “always bypassable,” or “safe/private” conclusion is supportable from this evidence.

Apple Support Discussions, Trustpilot, and some direct third-party pages returned security checks or HTTP 403 during this research; inaccessible content is not summarized. Later Firecrawl requests also encountered rate limiting, but the cited findings below were successfully retrieved either directly or in the earlier source pass. Search absence is treated as a gap, not proof that no other complaints or incidents exist.

# 1. Google Digital Wellbeing

## Current role and official limitations

- **[Official] Soft enforcement is intentional in important respects.** App timers reset at midnight, and a user who wants access before then can delete the timer. An optional four-digit **App limits PIN** can restrict changes, but setting it requires unlocking the device. Focus mode pauses selected apps and their notifications, with user-controlled breaks. Current documentation also warns that **some work and school accounts might not work with app timers**. These are documented behaviors, not evidence that a timer has malfunctioned. (Android Help, accessed 2026-11-08: https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420?hl=en)
- **[Official] Supervised accounts do not have the same interface.** Google says this Digital Wellbeing integration is available to supervised accounts on **Android 15 and up**. App-limit and bedtime controls are found through **Parental controls**, while the Digital Wellbeing screen leaves controls such as Focus mode, Flip to Shhh, Heads Up, and Mindful Nudge available to the child. This must not be generalized into a claim that ordinary Digital Wellbeing is itself a complete Family Link parental-control system. (Google for Families Help, accessed 2026-11-08: https://support.google.com/families/answer/15701527?hl=en)
- **[Official] Device variation matters.** Google prefaces its instructions by saying the feature is available “on some phones” and steps vary by device. A Fairphone, Samsung, Motorola, and Pixel report therefore should not be merged into one undifferentiated Android-wide defect. (Android Help, accessed 2026-11-08: https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420?hl=en)

## Parent, student, teacher, support, billing, and privacy perspectives

- **Parents — [Official + evidence boundary].** The Android 15+ supervised-account path is the direct parent/child evidence located for Digital Wellbeing itself. The crash reports above cannot simply be relabeled as Family Link failures just because Android's settings menu says “Digital Wellbeing & parental controls.” No robust, recent parent sample evaluating this supervised integration was established. [Google's supervised-account documentation, accessed 2026-11-08](https://support.google.com/families/answer/15701527?hl=en)
- **Students and teachers — [Official + scarcity].** Google's warning that some work/school accounts may not work with timers is directly relevant, but no verified teacher-facing Digital Wellbeing deployment complaint or school administration workflow was found. Ordinary school-account incompatibility is not evidence of a school filtering outage. [Android Help, accessed 2026-11-08](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420?hl=en)
- **Support and billing — [Analysis grounded in product form].** Digital Wellbeing is an OS feature rather than a separately sold Net Nanny-style subscription; this research found no product-specific renewal/refund dispute to report. Community Product Experts are volunteers and should not be quoted as Google engineering. The retrieved incidents therefore provide troubleshooting paths, not a measured support SLA or resolution rate.
- **Data/privacy — [Official, limited].** Google's current setup flow points users to **Digital Wellbeing → More → Manage your data**, and describes local views of app use, unlocks, notifications, and Chrome site use. That documents a user data-management surface; it is not an independent privacy audit. No Digital Wellbeing-specific breach was established in this search. [Android Help, accessed 2026-11-08](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420?hl=en)

## Reasoned concerns, not observed incidents

- **[Analysis]** Digital Wellbeing is better understood as a customizable friction/awareness tool than as a tamper-resistant accountability boundary. Midnight reset, early timer deletion, and optional PIN protection are documented choices; a user who controls the device and settings can often intentionally undo their own restriction.
- **[Analysis]** Useful validation would separately test foreground time, audible background playback, notification accessories/assistant channels, midnight transitions, work/school accounts, and PIN-protected changes. Those are test cases suggested by the reports, **not newly confirmed bypasses**.

# 2. Apple Screen Time

## Current controls and designed prerequisites

- **[Official] Current family controls extend beyond a simple timer.** Apple's guide published **2026-10-26** describes family app/web restrictions, Ask to Buy, app exceptions, and Ask to Browse. It states that on **iOS/iPadOS 26 and later** a child can request an exception to a Screen Time limit or restriction. These request/exception paths are intentional and should be included when assessing supposed “bypasses.” [Apple parental-controls guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105121)
- **[Official] Requests have operational prerequisites.** Apple's guide published **2026-10-26** says app-exception requests arrive through Messages and expire if not approved within 24 hours. If a request does not arrive, Apple tells families to update devices, add the Apple Account email to iMessage, have used iMessage in the last 30 days, enable Messages notifications, and sync iCloud contacts. A missing request can therefore be a configuration/delivery failure, but every missing request is not proof of a Screen Time enforcement defect. [Apple request-management guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125399)
- **[Official, older] Screen Time is not presented as a new system security boundary.** Apple's Platform Security chapter, published **2021-02-18**, documents Downtime, restrictions, app limits, web usage limits, reporting, and a parent-set passcode. This older architectural statement remains useful context but is not a current iOS 27 vulnerability assessment. [Apple Platform Security — Screen Time](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/screen-time-security-secd8831e732/web)

## Perspective, support, billing, and privacy findings

- **Self-control users:** The iOS 18 beta thread includes a person applying social-media limits to their own device, and the iOS 26-era “one minute” article concerns users being locked out. The stronger family-request evidence should not be assumed to apply identically to an adult configuring their own device. [Beta thread, started 2024-06-21](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/screen-time-not-working-right-on-ios18.2429779/)
- **Parents and children:** The 2023 acknowledgment, beta request failures, request prerequisites, and 2026 resolved YouTube report all show why enforcement, approval delivery, and next-day reset behavior need separate diagnoses. Official exception options are not necessarily defects. [Apple guide, published 2026-10-26](https://support.apple.com/en-us/125399)
- **Students and teachers — scarcity:** No solid teacher/classroom sample specific to Screen Time was established. Complaints about Apple Classroom, school MDM profiles, or managed iPad restrictions were not converted into Screen Time evidence. The available student perspective is mainly the child affected by a parent's controls, not a student evaluating a school-managed Screen Time deployment.
- **Support and billing:** Screen Time itself is built into Apple platforms; no separate Screen Time subscription, auto-renewal, or refund pattern was found. Third-party focus/parental-control app fees are outside this product's billing record. Apple Support Community “recommended” answers were not treated as Apple statements.
- **Privacy architecture — [Official, dated 2021-02-18]:** Apple says a user's synced Screen Time settings/data use CloudKit end-to-end encryption; parent/child settings and usage transfer through end-to-end encrypted Apple Identity Service, with encrypted data briefly queued until read. If analytics sharing is enabled, Apple says it collects listed feature-level events but **no specific app or web usage data**. This is a dated vendor architecture statement, not a newly performed audit or proof that every iOS 27 feature retains exactly the same implementation. [Apple Platform Security](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/screen-time-security-secd8831e732/web)

## Reasoned concerns, not observed incidents

- **[Analysis]** Four different events must remain separate in evaluation: a limit failing, an approval failing to arrive, a permitted exception being used, and an intentional security bypass. They have different user impact and fixes.
- **[Analysis]** A self-control user who knows the Screen Time passcode has a different threat model from a child prevented from changing settings. Neither the CVE research nor a parent anecdote automatically measures adult self-control efficacy.

# 3. Net Nanny

## Product/platform status and official limitations

- **[Official] The currently advertised protection scope is narrower than the brand's historical multi-platform reputation.** The product page accessed **2026-11-08** lists **Windows 10/11, macOS 10.13–11.x, and iOS 11+**; current annual packages cover PC/Mac or PC/Mac/iOS. [Net Nanny products](https://www.netnanny.com/products/)
- **[Official] A conspicuous stale support warning remains.** The Net Nanny 10 page says **“Android, Kindle Fire, and Chromebook devices are currently not supported, pending the release of our new version in 2024.”** That text was still present when accessed **2026-11-08**. It is not valid to advertise functioning Android child protection merely because an Android parent-management app remains downloadable. [Net Nanny 10 support](https://www.netnanny.com/support/net-nanny-10/)
- **[Official] iOS fragility is partly documented.** Net Nanny says its iOS Child App must remain running in the background or Internet access will stop; Location Access must be “Always” or connectivity can break; and the VPN may need to be toggled. It also says leaving the screen on continues counting time and consumes more battery, and can contribute to inaccurate reports. These are vendor-acknowledged operational constraints, not only hostile child reviews. [Support FAQ, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.netnanny.com/support/net-nanny-10/)
- **[Official] Other documented limits include** possible Chrome/Edge filtering failures associated with QUIC configuration, Firefox/security-setting conflicts, Windows slowdown from conflicts with other monitoring/security software, an estimated **120 common apps** manageable on Apple devices, and “Device Might Be Unavailable” when a device may be off or Net Nanny may have been removed/disabled. The same support page is the source and date snapshot (accessed 2026-11-08): https://www.netnanny.com/support/net-nanny-10/
- **[Store metadata] App maintenance signals are old but component-specific.** Apple's lookup API showed Net Nanny Child App **10.6.2**, currentVersionReleaseDate **2022-02-15**, seller Zift Software LLC, when accessed **2026-11-08**. The parent app's listing showed version **10.0.66**; Google Play's parent listing showed an update dated **2021-06-15**. Store dates do not prove that server-side filtering, definitions, or every child component went unmaintained, but they heighten the importance of the recent compatibility reports. [Child metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1211447197&country=us), [parent metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1211763414&country=us), [Android parent listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.contentwatch.ghoti.cp2.parent)

- **Children and students:** Recent child reviews voice practical schoolwork, texting, app-access, timing, and family-trust concerns. ## Reasoned concerns, not observed incidents

- **[Analysis]** A filtering product that can remove ordinary Internet connectivity creates a separate availability risk from ordinary overblocking. Evaluation should test restart, app swipe-away, dead battery, location-permission changes, VPN/profile failure, mesh Wi-Fi, and recovery when the disable/password workflow itself needs connectivity.
- **[Analysis]** The combination of dated store packages, an old macOS support ceiling, and a stale 2024 Android promise is a maintenance/transparency concern. It does **not** justify declaring every Net Nanny component abandoned without backend/update evidence.

# 4. Deledao

## Product boundaries and official behavior

- **[Official] Three functions must be separated:** Deledao's current site identifies **ActiveScan** for web filtering, **ActiveInstruct** for classroom management, and **ActivePulse** for student wellness. Complaints about blocked research belong primarily to filtering; stale teacher-visible tabs concern classroom management; false crisis alerts concern wellness. [Security/data-privacy page, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.deledao.com/security-data-privacy)
- **[Official] Teachers have several unblock mechanisms, subject to district policy.** A support article modified **2026-11-03** says students can request a URL and give a reason; teachers may approve/deny, choose a URL or whole site, choose student/class/OU scope, and set expiration if their administrator permits. Proactive Unblock/Share Links can temporarily allow instructional resources in live classes, but admins may forbid exceptions to explicit block lists or require second approval. [Teacher unblock workflow](https://support.deledao.com/support/solutions/articles/66000486540-teachers-approve-and-manage-website-unblock-requests)
- **[Official] An important scope detail:** an unblock approved during a live class is **not automatically limited to that class session**; it remains until expiration or administrator removal. Teachers are advised to set a short expiration and use URL-only exceptions. This explains why policy configuration matters, but it is not evidence that teachers routinely over-allow sites. [Same article, modified 2026-11-03](https://support.deledao.com/support/solutions/articles/66000486540-teachers-approve-and-manage-website-unblock-requests)
- **[Official] Confirmed macOS 27 compatibility limitation:** An advisory modified **2026-09-22** says upgrading an Apple Silicon Mac to macOS 27 may remove Rosetta; because Deledao's current proxy/filter uses Intel components through Rosetta, **filtering may stop** until Rosetta is reinstalled. Deledao recommends adding Rosetta installation to the MDM upgrade workflow and verifying filtering afterward, and says a native Apple Silicon proxy is in development. This is a concrete vendor-acknowledged upgrade hazard with a mitigation, not a reported company-wide outage. [Deledao advisory](https://support.deledao.com/support/solutions/articles/66000536469-macos-27-requires-rosetta-reinstallation-for-apple-silicon-filtering)
- **[Official company/channel change]** Deledao's current pages warn that **Hāpara stopped being a Deledao reseller on 2025-09-01** and direct users unable to log in to note that change. This is relevant to support/account continuity, but does not itself establish lost features or data. [Current vendor notice, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.deledao.com/security-data-privacy)

## Reasoned concerns, not observed incidents

- **[Analysis]** Dynamic/page-level filtering creates a different operational burden from host-only blocking: an otherwise allowed site or school survey can become unusable because of page content, and ad content can change classification. That is a plausible explanation to test for the reported events, not a proven root cause for all of them.
- **[Analysis]** A good school evaluation should measure time-to-unblock, who can approve, whether an exception outlives the class, behavior under admin second-approval rules, stale-tab handling, wellness-alert false positives, and filtering immediately after OS upgrades. These are evaluation criteria derived from the evidence, not assertions that every district experiences each problem.

# Cross-product conclusions and explicit evidence gaps

1. **Different failure modes dominate.** Digital Wellbeing evidence centers on soft self-control and isolated platform/accessory/crash behavior; Screen Time adds family synchronization, approval delivery, and security fixes; Net Nanny adds connectivity, maintenance, subscription/support, and parent-child trust issues; Deledao adds institutional overblocking, unblock workload, classroom monitoring, wellness triage, and school procurement/privacy governance.
2. **Accidental defects and deliberate bypasses are not interchangeable.** The Spotify/background, crashes, request delivery, connectivity, and false-positive reports are alleged accidental faults. Apple's crafted URI and Assisted Access cases, Net Nanny's historical alternate-VPN claim, and school-student attempts to evade filters are intentional circumvention reports and must be tested separately.
3. **Current evidence is not a reliability league table.** Net Nanny has many store reviews because consumer app stores expose them; Deledao has visible student petitions because school deployment affects groups; built-in Apple/Google controls have different support channels. Counts across these sources are non-comparable.
4. **Teacher evidence is uneven by design.** Deledao has direct teacher/admin evidence. Net Nanny is principally a home product, Digital Wellbeing is principally personal/with supervised-account integration, and Screen Time teacher evidence is easily contaminated by unrelated Classroom/MDM reports. The note deliberately leaves those cells sparse rather than manufacturing parity.
5. **Billing evidence belongs mainly to Net Nanny.** Digital Wellbeing and Screen Time are built-in controls, while Deledao evidence is B2B procurement rather than consumer refunds. Net Nanny's BBB record shows both cancellation friction and actual refunds; it does not establish the overall proportion of satisfied or dissatisfied subscribers.
6. **Privacy policies are not incident reports.** Apple's encryption description, Net Nanny's retention/tracking disclosures, and Deledao's processor/retention/ISO claims document vendor positions. User discomfort with monitoring is meaningful evidence of acceptance and trust concerns, but it is not proof of undisclosed data collection or a breach.
7. **Most important unresolved checks:** reproduce current background-media timing and accessory notifications on current Android builds; verify Screen Time request/limit behavior on current stable OS releases with prerequisites controlled; test current Net Nanny child-side components on supported OS versions rather than inferring from parent apps; and measure Deledao false positives and unblock latency in a specific district configuration. Until then, current prevalence and universal cross-version claims remain unproven.
