# Open-source research notes: citation and evidence audit

Audit performed **2026-11-08** against [`open_source.md`](open_source.md). “Verified” means the cited page or record was retrieved and its wording checked; it does not turn a user allegation into independently reproduced technical fact. The audit keeps firsthand reports, maintainer diagnoses, vendor-designed limits, and documented fixes separate. The source notes were not edited.

## Source quality, link health, and quotation fidelity

### Takeaway

The notes are unusually careful about stale reports, issue closure, architectural limits, and unsupported causal claims. The main corrections are nevertheless substantive: StayFocusd has recurring timer allegations under the notes’ own definition and a direct paired praise/complaint review; SelfControl’s VPN wording overstates the direct-report sample; and material later LeechBlock evidence distinguishes upstream repairs, a fixed regression, configuration errors, and an open memory complaint.

### Cited Findings

- **Use the mirror’s individual reviews, not its generated summary:** ExtensionDownloader expressly describes itself as an independent service, not affiliated with Google or the Chrome Web Store. Its StayFocusd page’s **AI-generated summary was generated 2026-10-14 from 120 collected reviews** and warns that it may be biased, incomplete, or outdated. The useful evidence is the individually readable author/date/version/body text, not that summary, its rating aggregates, or its reconstructed history. A mirror-hosted report remains a firsthand allegation relayed by a third party—not independent technical reproduction or a first-party support ticket. [Timer-filtered mirror reviews](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews?q=timer); [general mirror reviews](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews). The mirror lacks stable standalone review links in the retrieved material, so preserve each review’s exact date/version and excerpt alongside the query URL.
- **StayFocusd’s newest metadata is not a diagnosis:** The official listing, checked **2026-11-08**, identifies **Sensor Tower**, **Manifest V3**, **4.6.24**, and an update on **2026-11-07**. It discloses handling of **web history, user activity, and website content**. These establish current product/version/disclosure metadata, not that earlier complaints persist or were fixed. [Official Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji).
- **The privacy clarity concern should remain a clarity concern:** Sensor Tower’s multi-app policy, effective **2026-09-23**, includes StayFocusd among its apps, describes consent to collection of some personal information, website/app usage data, and use or sale of anonymized information. The store’s narrower statement says it does not collect personal data from webpage content. These statements can describe different data categories and processes. Neither unsupported ad/tracking reviews nor the policy alone proves undisclosed tracking, and the disclosure is not a categorical privacy clearance. [Sensor Tower policy](https://sensortower.com/page-stayfocusd-privacy); [store disclosure](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-block-distract/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji).
- **The W3C thread is closed, but not because a StayFocusd repair was demonstrated:** W3C ServiceWorker **#1624 was opened 2022-01-15 and closed as completed 2022-01-17** after referral to earlier discussion #1558. StayFocusd’s developer described persistent per-second accounting as an MV3 architectural risk. No implementation or timer fix was demonstrated in this issue. [w3c/ServiceWorker #1624](https://github.com/w3c/ServiceWorker/issues/1624).
- **Several existing source cautions were confirmed:** SelfControl **#642** is closed without a visible diagnosis proving the original reporter recovered; [the maintainer specifically asked whether sites were inaccessible or only the app still displayed a running block on 2020-10-03](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/642#issuecomment-703172195). SelfControl [#879](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/879), [#852](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/852), [#729](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/729), and LeechBlock [#623](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/623) are open, with the respective scope/version/diagnostic limits already described in the source. LeechBlock [#404](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/404) is closed without a visible explanation for its immediate-block symptom. Preserve these distinctions.
- **Do not confuse review automation or deleted work with repair evidence:** SelfControl’s PR [#918](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/pull/918) supplies real merge evidence, but its CodeRabbit summaries are not maintainer confirmations of the cited complaints. Separately, the Safari alternative-app comment in [#867 on 2026-01-10](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/867#issuecomment-3733633183) links **#920**, whose route currently says “This issue has been deleted”; it supplies no reviewable SelfControl fix. [PR #920](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/pull/920).
- **Official pages are not interchangeable:** Plucky’s manuals distinguish intended delay/uninstall friction, unsupported browser choices, possible network failures, and explicitly fixed old defects. Date all of those separately. Likewise, LeechBlock’s `VERSIONS.md` is more reliable for release dates than an inconsistent generated release body: the [v1.6.8 release body](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/releases/tag/v1.6.8) says “09 Jan 2024,” while the [version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md) and January 2025 incident establish **2025-01-09**. This is a citation pitfall, not an existing source error.
- **Weak/SEO corroboration is unnecessary:** The source’s principal technical claims use official manuals, first-party repositories, Bugzilla, W3C, and official storefront disclosures rather than “best blocker,” removal-guide, or affiliate/SEO pages. Do not introduce such pages as confirmation of recurrence or severity. ExtensionDownloader requires the separate third-party-mirror qualification above even when it reproduces readable reviews.
- **Old-open does not mean current, and recent-commit does not mean released:** SelfControl’s public `master` history contains merge commit **`34774c4` on 2025-11-29**, with no later displayed public commit at the audit cutoff; the next older displayed commit is dated **2021-10-27**; its GitHub releases page says there are no releases. The official site still offers **4.0.2**, and the maintainer said on **2026-10-20** that a rewrite was basically done but unreleased. This supports “mixed maintenance and release lag,” neither abandonment nor completed repairs. [Commit history](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/commits/master/); [empty releases page](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/releases); [official download](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [PR #918 maintainer exchange](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/pull/918).

### Inferences

- The best evidence chain is **a dated firsthand reproduction → maintainer confirmation of the cause → associated fix → user or release confirmation**. A closed state, open state, recent version, or single documentation edit supplies only one part of that chain.
- “Recurring” in the source is a deliberately minimal count of independent-looking firsthand symptom reports. It is not a population estimate, an identity-verification result, or proof of one root cause. Apply that definition consistently rather than lowering the threshold only for some products.
- When two reports use the same words—“not working,” “blocked,” “timer”—the platform, configuration, affected version, and mechanism still decide whether they are comparable. Android hostname matching, API breakage, and content-script injection are different defects or configuration cases.

### Gaps

- This audit rechecked the evidence underlying corrections and material new findings; it is **not a fresh HTTP census of every source URL**. No blanket statement that every original link remains live should be inferred.
- No forensic logs, packet captures for most network cases, controlled performance benchmarks, or hands-on retesting on current browsers/OSes were available. User supplied measurements and maintainer explanations were not independently reproduced.
- Reddit JSON and old-page retrieval returned network-security blocks, and the retrieved HTML did not contain usable substantive post text. Do not retain or add specific Reddit allegations from indexed snippets or an inaccessible page. Tavily also returned a plan-usage-limit error, and unauthenticated GitHub API requests became rate-limited; these retrieval limitations are not evidence of inactivity or absence of complaints.

### Inferences

- The product comparison should change from “SelfControl/LeechBlock recur, StayFocusd does not” to **different confidence in diagnosis and remediation**. StayFocusd now meets the notes’ minimal symptom-recurrence definition through weak mirror evidence, while SelfControl and LeechBlock retain much stronger technical diagnosis and issue history for some themes.
- Fixed historical Android defects are counterevidence to an undifferentiated “Android remains broken” narrative. They do not eliminate open or undiagnosed Android concerns. Surface the upstream fix, 1.6.8 recovery, hostname configuration outcome, and #404 uncertainty in the same discussion rather than selecting only favorable or unfavorable examples.
- The new SelfControl evidence supports narrower formulations: allowlist mode can fail with particular entries/settings; unlisted-site failures can be coupled to shared infrastructure or a particular imported list; Safari and Private Relay still lack a general public diagnosis.

### Gaps

- The mirror cannot verify distinct reviewer identities, completeness of store coverage, or whether reports were edited/removed after collection. Its visible date/version fields are useful metadata, but its counts and search results should not be used to estimate prevalence or time trends.
- The new issue evidence was read from public issue history, not reproduced on the named hardware. There is no verified current fix for #722/#904, no universal common cause for #681/#903, no confirmed full mitigation for #124, and no public closure rationale for #404.
- No fresh, high-quality firsthand Plucky complaint sample was established. The additions therefore concern official recovery/support/version boundaries rather than an assertion of new firsthand recurrence.

### Inferences
