# Freedom (freedom.to), RescueTime, and Focus (heyfocus.com): user complaints and company practices

Research cutoff: **2026-11-08**. Scope is restricted to Freedom by Eighty Percent Solutions, RescueTime, and the macOS Focus app at heyfocus.com—not FocusMe or Apple Focus. “Recurring” below means multiple independent reports, not a measured prevalence or consensus. Store reviews are attributed allegations, not verified facts; official troubleshooting documents establish documented limitations, not necessarily user frequency. Historical claims retain their dates. Trustpilot pages were retrieved through search-result content because direct requests hit bot verification; Reddit was likewise researched through indexed page content when its JSON endpoint blocked retrieval.

## What do users explicitly say they hate or regret paying for?

### Takeaway

The clearest payment regrets are (1) paying for a blocker that silently stops blocking or damages connectivity, (2) RescueTime users receiving incomplete or misleading-looking tracking data and being billed after disuse, and (3) Focus buyers encountering bugs followed by unanswered support requests. Focus has far fewer public complaints than the other two, so its apparent support pattern rests on a small number of reports.

### Cited Findings

#### Freedom

- **Recurring payment regret: unreliable enforcement.** An undated App Store review visible at the cutoff praised Freedom’s uninstall protection and disabled early-exit option as “ironclad,” but said protection would “sometimes randomly cease to work”; rebooting or starting another session restored it. The reviewer called this “a pretty big hole in the defenses” even while otherwise liking the purchase. A separate Windows reviewer dated **2025-08-27** said the app would not open and a web-started session neither blocked the intended site nor prevented easy cancellation. These are different platforms and configurations, not proof of one common defect. — [Freedom App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews); [Freedom Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/freedom.to)
- **Current severe mobile complaint:** on **2026-08-17**, an Android reviewer wrote that Freedom had become “flaky,” falsely warned that Accessibility was disabled, intermittently stopped blocking Firefox, and did not run after a phone restart until manually opened: “This really messes things up for me.” The vendor answered on August 20 that Android version **7.14.1** would resolve the issue, making this an unusually concrete report with a fix claim. — [Freedom on Google Play, review and developer response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=to.freedom.android2&hl=en_US)
- **Older bypass regret, with an important qualification:** a **2023** Reddit poster said they had paid for the full version and it worked until they discovered they could quit Freedom on a MacBook and reach blocked sites/apps. The post does **not** say Locked Mode or uninstall protection was enabled, so it establishes disappointment with default friction, not a demonstrated Locked Mode bypass. Another **2023-03** Reddit context confirms the poster was seeking something that could not simply be quit. — [“Freedom App Alternative,” r/digitalminimalism](https://www.reddit.com/r/digitalminimalism/comments/1220frc/freedom_app_alternative/)
- **Connectivity/resource disappointment:** an **April 2024-era** r/productivity thread titled “Problems with Freedom.to app” reported that merely installing Freedom, with no blocking enabled, made the internet “very very slow.” Freedom’s own March 2026 Mac documentation independently acknowledges that its proxy can produce “slower computer performance,” giving a plausible documented mechanism but not proving that user’s diagnosis. — [Reddit firsthand report](https://www.reddit.com/r/productivity/comments/1by3ilu/problems_with_freedomto_app/); [Freedom, Non-Proxy Mode for Mac](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385704-non-proxy-mode-for-mac)
- **Historically repeated billing/support anger, not a verified fraud pattern.** A **2024-05-16** Trustpilot reviewer alleged a renewal charge on an expired card, inability to remove the card or delete the account after three attempts, and no support reply for a week; their characterization “complete scam” is opinion, not an established legal finding. A **2023-09-02** reviewer said they never used the product and were refused a refund based on terms they claimed changed after purchase. These accounts should remain dated allegations. — [Freedom Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/freedom.to)
- **Privacy plus refund regret in one older detailed review:** on **2022-10-06**, an iOS reviewer said setup mistakenly blocked the entire internet, including email, and support could still see their settings after they uninstalled. They also said they sought a refund after roughly two days, were sent to Apple, but could not find the reported $40 transaction in their Apple account despite a bank withdrawal. Freedom’s posted reply rejected “collect or sell your data” and said cross-device blocklist synchronization explained the visible settings; it did not substantively resolve the reviewer’s broadband block or refund account in the quoted exchange. — [Freedom App Store review and developer response](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews)
- **Current product-direction regret:** on **2026-03-03**, a self-described five-year user with a lifetime plan wrote “Paid for lifetime subscription. Disappointed at the lack of progress,” describing releases as maintenance rather than meaningful new features. This is a single current opinion, not proof development has stopped. — [Freedom Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/freedom.to)

#### RescueTime

- **Current unwanted-subscription/refund complaint:** on **2026-07-07**, a Trustpilot reviewer called the app buggy and said they had unknowingly ended up with a two-seat Team subscription despite being an individual, stopped using it, and were refused a refund. The current refund policy offers 60 days only on an **initial purchase**, explicitly excluding renewals, so the refusal described is consistent with published policy even though the plan-selection and cancellation experience created the user’s anger. — [RescueTime Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.rescuetime.com); [RescueTime refund policy, updated 2026-06-14](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/430-refund-policy)
- **Current trial/privacy resentment:** on **2026-05-21**, a reviewer said the offer appeared free during download, but a card was requested only after installation; they deleted the app because computer-activity tracking plus that reveal felt “shady.” An older review titled “Misleading ‘Free’ Trial and Privacy Concerns” similarly objected to sharing activity data for a 14-day evaluation and believed there was no ongoing free tier. The latter’s platform matters: current desktop/Android listings do have Free/Lite, while current iOS help explicitly says **no free plan on iOS**. — [RescueTime Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.rescuetime.com); [RescueTime for iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features); [plan comparison](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/81-what-are-the-differences-between-premium-and-lite-accounts)
- **Long-term paying users regret taxonomy and product direction, not just price.** In a 2025 product-history thread, a customer since **2012** said YouTube had “wrecked my tracking” because the same service can be work or entertainment; RescueTime’s Mark replied on **2025-02-24** that this remained unsolved for YouTube accounts, webmail, and similar services after Google path changes broke an earlier heuristic. A paying freelancer since **2018** valued Classic’s quiet consolidation and customizable wording and rejected coaching notifications as distracting, illustrating resentment toward forced workflow changes. — [RescueTime product-history discussion](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/recent-product-history-and-hopes-going-forward-2025/1311)
- **Explicit non-renewal risk tied to connectivity:** on **2026-07-31**, a user appended to that product-history discussion that their employer had progressively closed the ports used by the Windows app to reach RescueTime’s backend; unless another route or export path were offered, they were “very unlikely to renew.” This is an isolated environment-specific report, not proof RescueTime normally requires unusual ports. — [RescueTime product-history discussion, July 2026 post](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/recent-product-history-and-hopes-going-forward-2025/1311)
- **Mobile expectations can waste users’ time.** An App Store reviewer said they had happily paid for RescueTime roughly a decade earlier, returned to track iPad streaming, and found only two paid options; they criticized the app for not clearly disclosing what mobile could track and where results appeared. Current documentation now explains that iOS activity appears only in the local Mobile tab and that iOS requires a paid plan, but the review is useful firsthand evidence of expectation/setup friction. — [RescueTime App Store listing and review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rescuetime/id1629924958); [current iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features)
- **Support experience is not uniformly negative.** A **2023-11-27** five-star Trustpilot reviewer requested a refund because subdomain tracking did not meet their needs but praised support’s “responsiveness, clarity and availability.” A review updated **2023-05-20**, titled “Unwanted charges—refunded,” complained about lacking a renewal warning but also records that a refund was obtained. These contradict a blanket claim that support never responds or refunds. — [RescueTime Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.rescuetime.com)

#### Focus (heyfocus.com)

- **Small-sample recurring paid-app/support grievance:** on **2025-02-07**, a Trustpilot reviewer wrote “Great app, but non-existent support.” They said daily, weekly, and monthly statistics disagreed, some sessions only appeared in monthly view or nowhere, promised remediation followed two weeks of repeated contact, and communication eventually stopped. They explicitly called it a terrible experience for a paid app they had come to rely on. — [Focus Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com)
- **Second 2025 functional/support failure:** on **2025-03-30**, another Trustpilot reviewer wrote only “Very buggy, stopped working entirely. The support doesn’t respond to bug reports.” The platform/version and symptom were not specified, so it cannot establish macOS-wide breakage; it independently echoes the support concern. — [Focus Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com)
- **Recent lifetime-license maintenance anxiety:** in a thread created **2026-06-19**, a Mac Power Users poster who bought Focus Lifetime in **2024** said developer email communication still existed in October 2025 but had since stopped, and reported that switching profiles caused script content not to be locked/saved. Their speculation that Focus might be abandoned is just speculation; no vendor abandonment announcement was found. Together with the two 2025 reviews, it forms three support anecdotes, still too few to characterize all customers. — [Mac Power Users, Focus support/development thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/focus-heyfocus-com-stop-supporting-and-abandon-development/45890)
- **A “blocked too well” complaint was user configuration, not demonstrated malfunction:** on **2021-04-06**, a user said they accidentally entered **600 hours** rather than 60 in Hardcore Mode and could not comfortably escape. Contemporary commenters recommended contacting support rather than publicizing a bypass. Current Focus 2’s changelog caps a session at 24 hours, so this old anecdote should not be presented as a current 600-hour defect. — [Mac Power Users firsthand thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/website-blocker-heyfocus-blocking-too-well/22715); [Focus changelog, v2.2.0](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)

### Inferences

- Users are least tolerant of **silent failure**: a blocker that appears active but leaks, or a tracker whose reports omit/misclassify work, undermines the reason for paying more than an explicit error would. The evidence includes Freedom’s “randomly cease[d]” protection, RescueTime’s multi-user sync gaps, and Focus’s missing statistics. — [Freedom App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews); [RescueTime April 2026 sync thread](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-april-21-2026/1687); [Focus Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com)
- Billing anger is usually a **policy/expectation problem layered on a technical problem**, rather than proof of unlawful charging: users describe discovering renewal terms after losing confidence in the product. Freedom and RescueTime both provide self-service cancellation, while RescueTime explicitly excludes renewals from its 60-day refund guarantee. — [Freedom billing FAQ](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4530040-freedom-subscription-and-billing-faq); [RescueTime refund policy](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/430-refund-policy)
- Focus support responsiveness is a **plausible recurrent concern but low-confidence as a population claim**: only two relevant Trustpilot reviews and one 2026 forum report were identified. — [Focus Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com); [June 2026 Mac Power Users thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/focus-heyfocus-com-stop-supporting-and-abandon-development/45890)

### Gaps

- No tested prevalence, representative sample, or reliable “percentage of users” was found for any product. App-store and Trustpilot self-selection prevents estimating how common these failures are.
- Freedom complaint searches produced many irrelevant results for Freedom Mobile/FreedomPop/F-Secure Freedome; those were excluded. No direct BBB complaints were validated, so no BBB-based company-practice conclusion is offered.
- Focus’s refund guarantee is documented, but no detailed firsthand account of a successful or refused Focus refund was found.

## Which complaints are technical product flaws versus policy/support/company issues?

### Takeaway

Technical complaints and documented constraints dominate blocking/tracking: proxy/VPN interference, permissions, platform APIs, idle heuristics, taxonomy, cloud synchronization, and browser hooks. Company-practice complaints concern renewals, limited refund coverage, nonresponsive support, product direction, and the privacy tradeoff of cloud-backed activity/configuration data. Official documentation often shows a design tradeoff rather than a simple coding bug.

### Cited Findings

#### Technical product flaws and officially documented limitations

##### Freedom

- **Desktop proxy/connectivity breakage is vendor-documented.** In the article dated **2026-03-11**, Freedom says its default Mac mode requires a proxy, which can conflict with other proxies, VPNs, and managed/public computers, sometimes cause slower performance, and make unrelated desktop apps lose internet access. Its remedy is Non-Proxy Mode. That remedy has tradeoffs: a **1–2 second delay** before the block screen, restricted browser compatibility, Firefox being blocked on Mac in that mode, and no Freedom Safari extension; an optional browser extension can remove the delay where supported. This is strong evidence of architecture-related limitations, not merely user error. — [Freedom, Non-Proxy Mode for Mac](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385704-non-proxy-mode-for-mac)
- **Windows has corresponding blocking leaks and VPN conflicts.** Non-Proxy Mode is enabled by default in the current Windows instructions; Freedom acknowledges that when Non-Proxy Mode or Website Exceptions is active, blocking youtube.com does not stop YouTube videos embedded in Google search results. It recommends the browser extension for the same 1–2 second initial enforcement delay. Separate current Windows troubleshooting lists NordVPN, ExpressVPN, ProtonVPN, Surfshark, TunnelBear, CyberGhost, Private Internet Access, and corporate VPNs as possible interferers. — [Freedom, Non-Proxy Mode for Windows](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4523542-non-proxy-mode-for-windows); [Freedom Windows troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529917-freedom-troubleshooting-for-windows)
- **Cross-device sync and scheduling require a functioning cloud/app path.** The Mac sync guide dated **2025-05-29** says dashboard session/blocklist edits may not immediately reach a device and, more rarely, network problems can prevent blocks starting or stopping on time; it lists network instability, antivirus/VPN/other blockers, old app versions, and incomplete reinstalls. The **2025-08-20** start/stop guide tells desktop users to launch the app and choose “Sync Freedom,” and mobile users to open the app or pull down Today to force a server sync. A newly added device must also be manually included in each desired session before it will block. These are documented operational dependencies; their incidence in the user base is unknown. — [Freedom Mac syncing guide](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385684-syncing-freedom-on-mac); [Freedom session start/stop troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/1347494-issue-blocks-not-ending-properly); [Freedom iOS troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting)
- **Current iOS constraints are unusually specific.** The guide dated **2026-09-18** requires Screen Time app blocking for apps, Safari Content Blocker for Safari, and the Freedom Profile/VPN for non-Safari website blocking. Only one iOS VPN can be active, “Always Allowed” and conflicting Apple Screen Time limits can create leaks, active blocklists can include at most **50 apps/categories**, and low background activity can delay scheduled start/end. On **iOS 27**, Apple’s default-on Connectivity Assist can make VPN blocking unreliable; Freedom’s fix is to disable Connectivity Assist. Blocking shared domains can collateral-block related apps or cut an app’s connectivity, and the all-websites setting can cut all internet access. — [Freedom iOS troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting)
- **iOS permission and historical Crash handling are partly Apple dependencies.** Since iOS 16, app blocking requires Screen Time access; Freedom says inability to grant it may require enabling Face ID or Touch ID. The app-selection picker has crashed because of Apple’s Screen Time API, but Freedom says Apple made major improvements beginning with **iOS 26**. This is both a limitation and a dated improvement, not an unresolved claim that every picker still crashes. — [Freedom iOS troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting)
- **Android can lose blocking privileges rather than fail visibly.** The guide dated **2026-07-14** requires Accessibility access for app/website blocking, separate permission for notification blocking, and possible battery-optimization exceptions. It says battery savers can revoke access even while settings appear enabled. On **Android 17**, opt-in Advanced Protection automatically revokes and prevents re-granting Freedom’s Accessibility permission because app blockers do not meet Google’s accessibility-tool categories; Freedom’s workaround is disabling that phone security feature. Blocking system apps can also break core device functionality, a configuration risk rather than necessarily a code defect. — [Freedom Android troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529927-freedom-for-android-troubleshooting)
- **Cloud dependency is for control/sync, not routing user traffic through Freedom’s servers.** Freedom says blocking runs locally via desktop proxy/browser plugins or the iOS limited VPN, while cloud sync occurs at login, blocklist creation/update, and session/schedule start. The iOS activity guide says sessions may need internet to receive start/stop instructions, Background App Refresh must be enabled, and Low Power Mode can interrupt schedules. It also requires the user to remain logged into the correct account. Thus “cloud account dependency” is supported, but “Freedom sends all browsing traffic through its cloud” is contradicted by its documentation. — [Freedom privacy Q&A](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/1347493-freedom-privacy-questions-and-answers); [Ensuring Freedom is Active on iOS](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529936-ensuring-freedom-is-active-on-ios)

##### RescueTime

- **Idle handling intentionally undercounts some real work.** RescueTime defines activity as keyboard/mouse input. If input resumes within five minutes it treats the person as thinking; after longer inactivity it completes the record and subtracts five minutes. A pause just over the threshold can therefore disappear, and RescueTime says a perfect 60-minute hour is rare, with under-tracking particularly possible for people repeatedly consulting printed material. Support can adjust the internal timer. This is an officially documented heuristic—not proof that every “missing minutes” complaint is a sync bug. — [“Why don’t I see 60 minutes…?”, last updated 2026-06-25](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/19-why-dont-i-see-60-minutes-in-my-by-hour)
- **Reports cannot always distinguish work from distraction by domain alone.** Current reporting documentation says uncategorized activity is neutral and drags Productivity Pulse toward 50, so users must categorize frequent activities and override defaults. The long-term customer’s YouTube complaint and RescueTime’s response demonstrate a harder context problem: work and entertainment can share the same domain/account infrastructure, and URL-path schemes change. New Timesheets can use hints, past reviewed assignments, and optional AI suggestions, but documentation expressly says suggestion quality depends on setup/history and review. — [Understanding Your Data](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/461-understanding-your-data); [product-history discussion](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/recent-product-history-and-hopes-going-forward-2025/1311); [Timesheets overview](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/400-rescuetime-timesheets)
- **Multi-computer reports can surprise users.** RescueTime allows unlimited computers but does not distinguish them in standard reports. It says simultaneous use can produce more reported activity than wall-clock time; individual activities are still logged independently. The current FAQ also says only the active window is tracked regardless of open apps, tabs, or monitors. These are limitations in interpretation rather than necessarily incorrect event capture. — [Multiple-computer support article](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/46-can-i-install-rescuetime-on-more-than-one-computer); [current RescueTime FAQ](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/469-frequently-asked-questions)
- **Recurring cloud report failures have concrete multi-user evidence.** In **November 2024**, Mac, Windows Classic, and Android users described stalled sync despite processes running, repeated reinstall/login, and green status displays; one user later confirmed recovery. On **2025-11-09/10**, users again reported no uploads after November 7 across multiple OSes, “unknown error,” and no time on three Macs or a Windows machine. In **April 2026**, Android/Windows users reported crashes, 80% missing activity and then no data; Firefox users additionally saw all activity collapsed into “Firefox.” These incidents support a recurring reliability concern, not an assertion of a permanent outage. — [November 2024 sync thread](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/rescuetime-not-syncing/1182); [November 2025 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-nov-7th/1605); [April 2026 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-april-21-2026/1687)
- **Offline connectivity and offline activities are different features.** The current FAQ says Internet loss should not lose activity: the client records locally and syncs after reconnection, while persistent “no internet”/“proxy” states warrant connection troubleshooting. In contrast, meetings, calls, and other non-computer activity require explicit Offline Activity logging, which is excluded from Free/Lite and included with paid plans. A user expecting passive capture of reading, watching a long video without input, or analog work must understand both distinctions. — [RescueTime FAQ](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/469-frequently-asked-questions); [plan comparison](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/81-what-are-the-differences-between-premium-and-lite-accounts); [idle heuristic](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/19-why-dont-i-see-60-minutes-in-my-by-hour)
- **Current mobile platforms are intentionally non-equivalent.** Android uploads app-use data and combines it with desktop reports; some vendors/models do not support its App Usage Data permission, while optional app blocking needs Accessibility and Display Over Other Apps. iOS uses Screen Time and blocks through Apple’s picker, but Apple’s rules keep activity on-device, so it appears only in the Mobile tab and cannot merge with website/desktop reports. Calling the missing iOS web data a sync defect contradicts the official design; calling mobile reporting fragmented is a valid limitation. — [RescueTime Android listing, updated 2026-11-03](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rescuetime.rtx&hl=en_US); [RescueTime for iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features)
- **A part-time user can see report distortions by design.** Current guidance says excluding personal devices naturally produces a different productivity picture from tracking work and personal machines; filtering work hours is recommended. DST can make a repeated autumn hour show extra time and a missing spring hour appear at 3 a.m. These should not be mislabeled random tracking failures. — [Understanding Your Data](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/461-understanding-your-data); [RescueTime FAQ](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/469-frequently-asked-questions)

##### Focus (heyfocus.com)

- **Browser blocking relies on macOS Automation permission and browser-specific integration.** Current help says that if Focus is not blocking, users should ensure each browser is enabled under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation, then restart Focus. Focus 1 documentation says Safari, Chrome, Opera, Brave, Edge, Arc, and Vivaldi work natively, while Firefox/other WebExtensions browsers require an official extension; unsupported browsers may need a manually loaded unpacked extension and Developer Mode. This makes “not blocking in one browser” potentially permissions/integration-specific, not proof all app blocking failed. — [Focus Automation permissions](https://heyfocus.com/docs/help/setting-up-focus-macos-automation-permissions); [Focus 1 browser-extension documentation](https://heyfocus.com/docs/v1/help/browser-extensions)
- **Historical release notes verify blocking/scheduling bug classes, but many have explicit fixes.** Focus 2.0.5 (**2023-09-25**) added macOS 14 Sonoma support and stability fixes. Focus 2.1.1 (**2024-10-19**) fixed schedules ending at midnight and Steam blocking. Focus 2.2.0 (**2024-11-09**) added overnight schedules, a 24-hour maximum, and crash fixes. Focus 2.4.0 (**2025-07-31**) fixed favicon crashes, improved URL matching, and improved automated unlocking; 2.4.1 (**2025-08-01**) fixed a Pomodoro break bug. These notes should be used as dated remediation, not as proof those old bugs persist. — [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)
- **Compatibility evidence is bounded.** The homepage currently advertises “macOS Sequoia Ready,” while the changelog separately documents Sonoma support and older Catalina/M1 compatibility work. No direct current-source confirmation of Tahoe/macOS 26 compatibility was located in this pass, so neither compatibility nor incompatibility should be inferred for that newer OS. — [Focus homepage](https://heyfocus.com); [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)
- **Published maintenance recency is a legitimate uncertainty, not proven abandonment.** As visible on **2026-11-08**, the latest listed Focus 2 release remains **2.4.1, 2025-08-01**, with no 2026 entry. A 2026 user’s unanswered-email complaint raises concern, but the changelog alone cannot establish that development or private support has permanently ceased. — [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog); [June 2026 support/development thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/focus-heyfocus-com-stop-supporting-and-abandon-development/45890)

#### Policy, support, billing, privacy, and company-practice complaints

##### Freedom

- **Renewal/cancellation depends on purchase channel.** The billing FAQ modified **2026-10-22** says all plans auto-renew except Forever; yearly plans have a seven-day free trial and charge unless canceled, while a status labeled “Trial” denotes the free tier and will not itself trigger a charge. Website users cancel via Settings → Manage Billing; App Store and Google Play purchases must be managed through those stores. Failed website renewals enter a retry/reminder grace period; immediate cancellation during that state requires contacting support. These instructions contradict claims that there is no supported cancellation route, but they do not disprove the older UI failures users reported. — [Freedom billing FAQ](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4530040-freedom-subscription-and-billing-faq); [Managing a Freedom subscription](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4623411-managing-your-freedom-subscription)
- **Refund transparency is weaker than cancellation transparency.** The current official FAQ and Terms explain renewal/cancellation but no explicit general customer refund window or guarantee was located; the Terms only discuss reversing affiliate commissions on refunded payments. The older App Store and Trustpilot allegations therefore cannot be compared against a clear published guarantee. Store purchases also introduce Apple/Google into the process. This absence should be described as “no general policy located,” not converted into a categorical “Freedom never refunds.” — [Freedom billing FAQ](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4530040-freedom-subscription-and-billing-faq); [Freedom Terms](https://freedom.to/terms); [Freedom App Store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews)
- **Privacy is local blocking plus identifiable account/configuration data, not “no data.”** Freedom’s privacy Q&A says it does not upload client personal activity/traffic, but synchronizes blocklists and schedules. The broader policy says it collects information needed to provide the service, uses account/order data, permits cookies and analytics under stated conditions, and reserves flexibility for types/use of information not presently applicable. The 2022 user’s alarm that support could see settings after uninstall is thus consistent with cloud-backed blocklists, though not evidence their browsing history was sold. — [Freedom privacy Q&A](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/1347493-freedom-privacy-questions-and-answers); [Freedom Privacy Policy](https://freedom.to/privacy); [Freedom App Store exchange](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews)

##### RescueTime

- **Current trial/cancel/refund rules are explicit but easy to resent.** All paid plans offer 14 days before charge and can be canceled in account settings; access continues through the paid term and then reverts to Lite. Pricing FAQs say partial-period refunds are not normally prorated. The separate policy updated **2026-06-14** promises a full refund within **60 days of the initial purchase**, excludes renewals, issues money to the original payment method, and refers Apple/Google subscribers to those stores. This supports a policy/guidance diagnosis for refund complaints, not an assumption every refund request qualifies. — [RescueTime pricing and FAQ](https://www.rescuetime.com/pricing); [RescueTime refund policy](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/430-refund-policy)
- **Activity surveillance concern has a real data basis but important limits.** RescueTime records active application name, site URL, **window title**, start/end time, device login/device/OS, and sometimes public IP. Paid detailed tracking can expose document names and window titles; the vendor says it never records keystrokes, form entry, screenshots, or page/window-body contents, discards query strings, allows pause/selective deletion, and says it does not sell/rent/share personal information. For teams, administrators control data sharing but the default is aggregate sharing with no access to another member’s individual data. User discomfort is legitimate; claims of screenshots/keylogging or mandatory manager access to every employee’s item-level timeline are contradicted by these documents. — [RescueTime privacy summary, policy dated 2025-04-25 and page last updated 2026-06-25](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/163-rescuetimes-privacy-policy); [plan comparison](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/81-what-are-the-differences-between-premium-and-lite-accounts)
- **Cloud/service processing deserves separate scrutiny from “sale.”** RescueTime lists AWS, payments, Help Scout, Customer.io, SurveyMonkey, ChartMogul, Google, and opt-in OpenAI as sub-processors; the OpenAI entry says data is never used to train its models. Google Play’s self-declared Data Safety panel says the Android app may share Location, Personal info and three other categories and may collect the same broad classes. These disclosures do not prove data sales, but they mean a simplistic “nothing sensitive ever leaves the device” claim is valid only for iOS Screen Time activity—not the whole RescueTime service. — [RescueTime sub-processors](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/122-what-are-your-sub-processors-as-defined-by-the-gdpr); [RescueTime Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rescuetime.rtx&hl=en_US); [RescueTime iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features)
- **iOS location wording is not fully consistent across vendor pages.** The detailed iOS guide calls Location a required permission, used to label Focus Sessions by place, and says GPS stays on-device and cannot be viewed by RescueTime. A **2026-09-21** launch article instead says location is recorded only when the user chooses, only for sessions they start. Both deny remote tracking, but the difference matters to permission-sensitive users and warrants vendor clarification. — [RescueTime iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features); [RescueTime mobile launch post](https://blog.rescuetime.com/new-rescuetime-for-ios-and-android/)
- **Status/support communication is part of the complaint, not just failed servers.** During the November 2025 data-processing interruption, users explicitly asked for acknowledgment and later asked why status.rescuetime.com had not reported it. In the 2024 thread, users likewise saw green status and no time appearing. RescueTime did ultimately respond and resolve at least the 2025 incident; therefore the substantiated issue is communication/status lag in those events, not universal absence of support. — [November 2025 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-nov-7th/1605); [November 2024 sync thread](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/rescuetime-not-syncing/1182)

##### Focus (heyfocus.com)

- **Focus is a one-time license with materially different update promises.** Current checkout lists Focus 2 and Lifetime as one-time purchases: the ordinary license includes one year of major updates and three years of minor updates; Lifetime includes lifetime updates. It advertises unlimited personal-Mac activations and a **30-day money-back guarantee**, with support by email. Therefore the 2026 Lifetime buyer’s fear concerns fulfillment of an update/support expectation, not a hidden recurring subscription. — [Focus checkout/license and guarantee](https://heyfocus.com/checkout); [June 2026 user thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/focus-heyfocus-com-stop-supporting-and-abandon-development/45890)
- **Privacy claims need both documents, but they are largely compatible.** Product/help pages say blocked sites and settings remain on the Mac and no sensitive data is sent off-device. The longer privacy policy says in-app analytics are collected to improve experience, while a crash prompts the user before sending anonymized crash information; website/order/cookies/ad services are separately described. The defensible summary is local blocklists/settings with some analytics/account/website processing—not literally zero telemetry. — [Focus privacy help](https://heyfocus.com/docs/help/focus-is-private); [Focus checkout privacy statement](https://heyfocus.com/checkout); [Focus full Privacy Policy](https://heyfocus.com/privacypolicy)
- **Support is email-based and responsiveness is the main policy concern found.** The support page gives support@heyfocus.com and promises a reply “as soon as possible,” but two 2025 Trustpilot users and one 2026 forum user allege nonresponse. There is no evidence here of ticket throughput, staffing, or a universal policy of ignoring customers. — [Focus support page](https://heyfocus.com/support); [Focus Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com); [June 2026 support/development thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/focus-heyfocus-com-stop-supporting-and-abandon-development/45890)

### Inferences

- **Different architectures predict different failures:** Freedom trades cross-device enforcement for cloud scheduling plus local proxy/VPN complexity; RescueTime trades passive analysis for cloud aggregation and semantic categorization; Focus keeps settings local but depends on macOS/browser automation and vendor updates. Each product should not be criticized using the other’s architecture—for example, Freedom is not documented as remote-routing all traffic and Focus is not a cross-device cloud blocker. — [Freedom privacy Q&A](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/1347493-freedom-privacy-questions-and-answers); [RescueTime privacy summary](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/163-rescuetimes-privacy-policy); [Focus privacy help](https://heyfocus.com/docs/help/focus-is-private)
- Some complaints are **correctly diagnosed platform constraints**: one active VPN on iOS, Apple Screen Time’s local-only iOS data, Android Accessibility policy, and mouse/keyboard idle detection cannot be fixed merely by more configuration UI. However, leaks, silent stale sync, incorrect reports, and failure to communicate are still product-experience failures even when an OS or heuristic contributes. — [Freedom iOS troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting); [RescueTime iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features); [RescueTime idle heuristic](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/19-why-dont-i-see-60-minutes-in-my-by-hour)

### Gaps

- No current, controlled performance/battery benchmark was found for Freedom; “battery drain” and general slowness cannot be quantified here. The high-confidence resource evidence is Freedom’s own acknowledgment of possible slower proxy-mode performance.
- No sufficiently detailed user platform/version was supplied for Focus’s March 2025 “stopped working entirely” report to identify a reproducible technical root cause.
- No current direct evidence established unique-domain/subdomain categorization accuracy rates for RescueTime, nor a current broad mobile-crash rate.

## Which severe complaints have firsthand evidence and vendor acknowledgment?

### Takeaway

The strongest severe case is Freedom’s August 2026 Android blocking failure because the reviewer gives concrete symptoms and the vendor names a corrective version. RescueTime has the strongest repeated service-level evidence: multiple users documented sync loss, followed in November 2025 by explicit vendor acknowledgment and a no-data-loss resolution. Recent severe Focus reports lack vendor acknowledgment in the cited threads.

### Cited Findings

- **Freedom / Android / high evidentiary quality (dated purchased-user storefront evidence + vendor fix):** the **2026-08-17** reviewer described incorrect Accessibility warnings, intermittent Firefox leaks, failure after reboot, and total blocking stopping until the app was opened. Eighty Percent Solutions replied on **2026-08-20**: “Please update … version 7.14.1. This will resolve the issue.” This is vendor acknowledgment of the reported issue, but there is no quoted post-update confirmation from the reviewer; “fixed” is the vendor’s claim, not independently verified here. — [Freedom Google Play review and vendor response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=to.freedom.android2&hl=en_US)
- **Freedom / iOS 27 / official severe-limitation acknowledgment without a linked individual case:** Freedom’s **2026-09-18** guide says websites can cease being blocked after the update because default-on Connectivity Assist undermines its VPN-based blocking, and supplies an off-switch workaround. It also acknowledges complete-connectivity side effects when required domains are blocked. This establishes current vendor-recognized failure modes and remedies, but not their frequency or a named-user outcome. — [Freedom iOS troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting)
- **Freedom / desktop / official acknowledgment of collateral connectivity failure:** the **2026-03-11** Mac guide explicitly says other desktop apps may lose internet access because of Freedom’s proxy and directs users to Non-Proxy Mode. This corroborates the technical plausibility of users’ severe “Freedom paralyzed the device/internet” reports, while the guide’s mode tradeoffs mean the workaround is not cost-free. — [Freedom Non-Proxy Mode for Mac](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385704-non-proxy-mode-for-mac)
- **Freedom / iOS privacy and blocking / firsthand evidence with only a partial vendor answer:** the **2022-10-06** review provides a detailed allegation of accidental full-internet blocking and support visibility after uninstall. Freedom replied that synchronization—not data sale—explained stored blocklists. That is acknowledgment of the support interaction and cloud configuration, not an acceptance that the reviewer’s privacy or refund interpretation was correct. — [Freedom App Store review and response](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews)
- **RescueTime / cross-platform data processing / strongest severe multi-user acknowledgment:** users reported in **November 2025** that activity stopped uploading across operating systems after November 7, despite clients appearing to run. On **2025-11-10**, RescueTime staff member Bianca said a data-processing interruption was “fully resolved,” the system was caught up, “No records were lost,” and the affected days would appear within hours. This is a completed severe incident with vendor acknowledgment; the delayed/incident-absent status-page communication remains separately criticized by users. — [RescueTime November 2025 sync incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-nov-7th/1605)
- **RescueTime / April 2026 / multiple firsthand reports plus workaround, but incomplete final confirmation:** users described crashes, most activity missing and then none, and Firefox-only aggregates. On **2026-04-27**, forum support responder JP distinguished a prior slow log-processing period from a separate latest-Firefox issue and prescribed the RescueTime Firefox extension until an app update. No reply in that thread confirms every missing-data case was restored or that a general Android/Windows crash fix shipped. — [RescueTime April 2026 sync incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-april-21-2026/1687)
- **RescueTime / reporting accuracy / firsthand complaint with limited vendor direction:** on **2024-12-23**, a user reported only 30 minutes logged for an hour actively spent on a site despite past accurate behavior; on **2025-01-03**, JP suggested updating the desktop app and contacting support if it persisted. This shows an official response but does not establish a root cause or completed fix; the documented five-minute heuristic may explain some undercounting but not necessarily this 50% discrepancy. — [“Severely underreporting time spent”](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/severely-underreporting-time-spent/1277); [official idle heuristic](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/19-why-dont-i-see-60-minutes-in-my-by-hour)
- **RescueTime / contextual categorization / confirmed longstanding limitation:** the paying customer’s **2025-01-30** “YouTube … wrecked my tracking” complaint received a direct vendor explanation on **2025-02-24** that separate work/leisure YouTube-account classification was still unsolved after changing Google paths broke prior URL analysis. This is not as severe as complete data loss, but it is an unusually clear acknowledged accuracy limitation. — [RescueTime product-history discussion](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/recent-product-history-and-hopes-going-forward-2025/1311)
- **Focus / recent severe bugs and support / firsthand allegations but no located vendor acknowledgment:** the February and March **2025** reviews describe inconsistent statistics, total failure, and ignored support; the June **2026** forum poster alleges vanished developer replies and profile-script saving failure. None of these threads contains a Focus response, resolution, platform/version detail sufficient for reproduction, or an official confirmation of abandonment. They should not be upgraded to “vendor-confirmed severe defects.” — [Focus Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com); [June 2026 Mac Power Users thread](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/focus-heyfocus-com-stop-supporting-and-abandon-development/45890)
- **Focus / historical severe defects acknowledged only through release notes:** old releases document fixes for Firefox service-worker sites being unblocked, browser-extension connection failures, crashes, schedules activating twice, and schedules failing; these are official acknowledgments of past bug classes but are not paired with the recent reviewers’ symptoms and should not be treated as current unresolved failures. — [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)

### Inferences

- Confidence is highest where a report has **platform, dates, symptoms, independent corroboration, and an explicit vendor response**; Freedom Android 7.14.1 and RescueTime November 2025 meet most of those criteria. Undated “scam,” “abandoned,” or “nothing works” language does not. — [Freedom Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=to.freedom.android2&hl=en_US); [RescueTime November 2025 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-nov-7th/1605)
- Vendor acknowledgment of a **design limitation** should not be mistaken for confirmation of an individual’s diagnosis or prevalence. Official proxy/VPN/heuristic guides explain mechanisms; they do not validate every connectivity, battery, or accuracy anecdote. — [Freedom Non-Proxy Mode](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385704-non-proxy-mode-for-mac); [RescueTime idle heuristic](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/19-why-dont-i-see-60-minutes-in-my-by-hour)

### Gaps

- No current severe Focus defect with both a reproducible user report and vendor acknowledgment was found.
- The Freedom 7.14.1 fix lacks a cited reviewer confirmation; the RescueTime April 2026 thread lacks comprehensive closure. Both should remain open to verification rather than declared fully fixed or still broken for everyone.
- No independent notification/outage auditing verified the vendors’ claims that all RescueTime November 2025 records were recovered or that Freedom’s current iOS workaround always restores blocking.

## Which old complaints no longer describe the current product?

### Takeaway

Several repeated talking points are obsolete or overbroad: Freedom is again available on iOS and no longer requires a VPN for iOS 16+ app blocking; RescueTime again has a free tier on desktop/Android and has consolidated Classic/New rather than leaving a separate “New RescueTime” track; Focus’s very old Vivaldi, schedule, and always-online-login bugs have dated fixes. Current limitations remain, so these updates must not be overgeneralized into “all old problems solved.”

### Cited Findings

#### Freedom

- **“Freedom was removed and cannot block on iOS” is historical, not current.** Apple-removal reporting dates to **2018**; the current App Store listing has ongoing versions, and Freedom’s current guide uses Screen Time app blocking plus Safari Content Blocker and optional local VPN tools. In **iOS 16+**, Screen Time handles app blocking so the Profile VPN is no longer required merely to block apps, although it remains useful/required for non-Safari-browser website coverage and suggested domain filters. — [2018 Fast Company removal report](https://www.fastcompany.com/90236881/apple-tight-lipped-on-removal-of-freedom-and-other-content-blocking-apps); [Freedom Profile VPN explained](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/8283287-the-freedom-profile-vpn-explained); [Freedom App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228)
- **Old Screen Time picker crashes should not be described as uniformly current.** Freedom says Apple substantially improved the app-selection API starting with **iOS 26**, and recommends updating. However, **iOS 27 Connectivity Assist** creates a different, currently documented website-blocking failure, so an old crash being mitigated does not imply current iOS reliability. — [Freedom iOS troubleshooting, updated 2026-09-18](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting)
- **Proxy complaints have a current alternative, not a blanket resolution.** Non-Proxy Mode can remove proxy/VPN interference and collateral app disconnections; current Windows setup enables it by default. Yet proxy avoidance introduces browser restrictions and a possible 1–2 second enforcement gap, and Windows’ embedded-search YouTube leak persists in that mode. Therefore old “no alternative to proxy breakage” remarks are outdated, while connectivity/leak concerns are not. — [Freedom Non-Proxy Mode for Mac](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385704-non-proxy-mode-for-mac); [Freedom Non-Proxy Mode for Windows](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4523542-non-proxy-mode-for-windows)
- **Old refund/support stories remain historical allegations rather than current policy facts.** The detailed App Store refund/privacy dispute is from **2022**, and strong Trustpilot “no reply/refund” examples cited above are from **2023–2024**. Current cancellation steps were recently updated, and a **2026-08-20** Android developer response offered a concrete version fix. That does not erase historical experiences, but it prevents presenting them as verified current practices. — [Freedom billing FAQ, modified 2026-10-22](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4530040-freedom-subscription-and-billing-faq); [Freedom Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=to.freedom.android2&hl=en_US); [Freedom Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/freedom.to)

#### RescueTime

- **“There is no ongoing free version” is currently false as a general statement but true for iOS onboarding.** Current plan comparison offers Lite Free with automatic app/website tracking, reports, two weeks of report history, one goal, and weekly summaries; the pricing page says canceled paid users revert to Lite. Current iOS documentation explicitly excludes iOS from the free plan. Current Android/Google Play also describes free basic automatic tracking. Older complaints should be localized to the platform and signup path instead of repeated globally. — [RescueTime plan comparison](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/81-what-are-the-differences-between-premium-and-lite-accounts); [RescueTime pricing](https://www.rescuetime.com/pricing); [RescueTime iOS guide](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/436-rescuetime-for-ios-features); [RescueTime Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rescuetime.rtx&hl=en_US)
- **“Classic and New RescueTime are separate, and mobile must switch to a coaching-only app” is outdated.** On **2025-01-29**, RescueTime’s Mark wrote “there is no longer a New RescueTime to explore,” said capabilities were being combined with Classic reporting, and offered passive/active notification choices. Long-term users’ difficulty understanding migration remains relevant, but a current critique should not tell readers to sign up for a separate “New RescueTime” track. — [RescueTime product-history discussion](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/recent-product-history-and-hopes-going-forward-2025/1311)
- **Old descriptions of mobile as static or effectively unable to track are stale after the 2026 rollout.** The **2026-09-21** vendor launch post describes automatic Android app upload/unified reporting and iOS Screen Time tracking/local-only Mobile reports. The Android listing updated **2026-11-03** includes app tracking, Focus Session blocking, project tracking, offline activity, and recent crash/feature fixes. These updates invalidate blanket “mobile has not changed” claims but do not prove old Android sync/crash reviews are now resolved for every device. — [RescueTime mobile launch post](https://blog.rescuetime.com/new-rescuetime-for-ios-and-android/); [RescueTime Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rescuetime.rtx&hl=en_US)
- **Specific old outages are closed events, not evidence the service is down now.** RescueTime staff said the November **2025** interruption was fully caught up without lost records, and a participant in the November **2024** thread said data appeared caught up. Nonetheless, later **2026** incidents establish recurrence; the correct current conclusion is intermittent sync/report reliability risk, not a years-long continuous outage. — [November 2025 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-nov-7th/1605); [November 2024 thread](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/rescuetime-not-syncing/1182); [April 2026 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-april-21-2026/1687)
- **“No refunds/customer support” is too categorical.** The current policy has a 60-day initial-purchase guarantee with an explicit support route, and older counterexamples include a user whose complaint title says “Unwanted charges—refunded” and another praising support while requesting a refund. The defensible current concern is renewal exclusion and non-prorating, not absolute refusal in every case. — [RescueTime refund policy](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/430-refund-policy); [RescueTime pricing](https://www.rescuetime.com/pricing); [RescueTime Trustpilot reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.rescuetime.com)

#### Focus (heyfocus.com)

- **The old Vivaldi breakage no longer describes official browser support.** A 2017 Vivaldi forum user reported Focus stopped working after Vivaldi updates and had received no response, but Focus 1.7.14 added native Vivaldi support that year, and current Focus 1 browser docs still list Vivaldi among native browsers. This old thread cannot substantiate current Vivaldi incompatibility, although the age of that documentation means a fresh Focus 2/browser compatibility test would still be useful. — [Vivaldi forum historical thread](https://forum.vivaldi.net/topic/20706/vivaldi-does-not-work-with-heyfocus-com-anymore-on-mac-since-the-most-recent-updates); [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog); [Focus browser docs](https://heyfocus.com/docs/v1/help/browser-extensions)

Older schedules activating twice, not re-enabling, calculating time zones incorrectly, and colliding with existing sessions likewise appear in old fix notes. They should not be repeated as known current behavior without new reproduction. — [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)
- **Old licensing complaints about internet access at every login are obsolete for the cited Focus 1 release.** Version **1.10.4, 2019-04-01**, explicitly says “Fixed login to not require Internet every time.” Current checkout instead advertises unlimited personal-Mac activations. This does not establish that activation never needs connectivity; it refutes only the historical every-launch requirement. — [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog); [Focus checkout](https://heyfocus.com/checkout)

- **“Focus 1 receives updates today” needs context rather than literal interpretation.** Current checkout says Focus 1 users from ten years ago still receive updates, but the public changelog’s latest Focus 1 entry is **v1.15, 2023-07-17**, while Focus 2’s latest is **v2.4.1, 2025-08-01**. This does not prove all support has stopped; it does show why a user could perceive maintenance as stale and why product-version-specific discussion matters. — [Focus checkout](https://heyfocus.com/checkout); [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)

### Inferences

- A durable complaint ledger should carry **first seen / last confirmed / resolved-by / regression** fields. Freedom’s iOS picker and Connectivity Assist entries, RescueTime’s repeated discrete sync events, and Focus’s fixed historical scheduling bugs show why “once reported” is not equivalent to “currently affects all users.” — [Freedom iOS troubleshooting](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting); [RescueTime April 2026 incident](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-april-21-2026/1687); [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)

### Gaps

- This was source research, not a hands-on regression test on macOS, Windows, iOS, or Android. Fix claims are therefore distinguished from independently observed fixes.
- No current official Focus 2 statement confirming **macOS Tahoe/26** compatibility was found; the homepage’s Sequoia claim should not be extrapolated. — [Focus homepage](https://heyfocus.com)
- Current Focus 2 browser-extension details could not be independently expanded beyond the accessible Focus 1 guide and Focus 2 changelog; claims about extension behavior are labeled by version rather than assumed identical. — [Focus browser docs](https://heyfocus.com/docs/v1/help/browser-extensions); [Focus changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog)
