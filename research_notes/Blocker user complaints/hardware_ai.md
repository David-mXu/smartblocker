# Brick, Unpluq, Focus AI, and focus-shield: actual complaints, policies, and maintenance

**Research cutoff and primary-source access date: 2026-11-08.** Scope is exactly [Brick (`getbrick.com`)](https://getbrick.com/), [Unpluq (`unpluq.com`)](https://www.unpluq.com/), [Focus AI (`getfocusai.com`)](https://www.getfocusai.com/), and [`moritzWa/focus-shield`](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield), not unrelated Brick hardware, other Focus products, or other Focus Shield projects.

**Evidence conventions.** “Firsthand” means a store reviewer or issue author reports their own experience; it is not independently reproduced or prevalence evidence. “Recurring” means at least two independent reports of a theme in this targeted sample, not that the problem is common. “Hands-on reviewer” means a named reviewer used the product, not an ordinary user complaint. “Official” means a vendor policy, FAQ, or store declaration, not a customer report. “Repository-verified” means the maintainer documents a diagnosis/test, not that a user reported the bug. Reviews can be edited; store-supplied versions are not necessarily the first affected version. Unless a date is printed separately, official pages and store metadata below were accessed on **2026-11-08**.

## Where do Brick and Unpluq create friction, fail to block, or lock users out?

### Takeaway

Brick’s best-supported dislikes are recurring schedule failures, NFC/setup failures, a same-day iOS 2026.43.1 crash cluster, accidental or unwanted lock states, and bypasses outside Strict Mode; Unpluq’s are schedules that do not start/stop correctly, failed Tag/unlock flows, app-selection failures, and residual restrictions after deletion. These are repeated firsthand themes rather than measured defect rates ([Brick App Store RSS](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Brick App Store helpful reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Unpluq App Store helpful reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json)). The physical barrier is valued by some users, so its inconvenience should not automatically be classified as a defect ([positive Unpluq Tag account, 2024-04-11](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Brick hands-on review, 2026-03-20](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbes-personal-shopper/article/brick-review/)).

### Cited Findings

**Brick — firsthand schedule, bypass, NFC, setup, and crash reports**

- **Recurring schedule failure after an earlier improvement:** App Store review **14076152274**, updated **2026-05-17** for **2026.18.0**, says Brick originally fixed its scheduling problems and worked for a couple of months, then again failed to unbrick on schedule. The earlier text also described schedules not starting and not blocking every selected app despite support’s suggested changes. This is evidence of recurrence for one reviewer, not a fleet-wide regression measurement. — [US App Store helpful reviews, review 14076152274](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json).
- **Unexpected travel lockout plus schedule trouble:** review **13539369559**, dated **2025-12-21** for **2025.50.0**, says a phone with no schedules set bricked itself while the reviewer was renting a car, then bricked again about five minutes after an Emergency UnBrick; the reviewer consumed emergency access, deleted the app, and later found schedules no longer changed automatically. The reviewer also says support did not answer the questions. These events were not independently reproduced. — [US App Store helpful reviews, review 13539369559](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json).
- **Android mini-window bypass:** a **2026-06-24** Google Play reviewer reported opening bricked apps in a mini-window; generic advice to disable that phone functionality reportedly did not work. The visible current Play review retains this account, but neither the reviewer’s device details nor a current-version test establish that every Android device or the current release remains affected. — [Brick Google Play listing/review, accessed 2026-11-08](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brickllc.brick&hl=en_US).
- **iOS Screen Time/Shortcut bypass before current Strict Mode guidance:** review **13484819036**, dated **2025-12-06** for **2025.48.1**, says turning off Brick’s Screen Time permission removed restrictions and that a Settings-redirect Shortcut did not automatically work after restart. The reviewer did not establish that every Strict Mode/device configuration has the same route. The [current FAQ, accessed 2026-11-08](https://getbrick.com/pages/faq) advertises Strict Mode to prevent app deletion/settings bypasses, but that current statement alone does not retest the reviewer’s configuration. — [US App Store helpful reviews, review 13484819036](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [current Brick FAQ](https://getbrick.com/pages/faq).
- **NFC/app-operation failures:** review **13663664312**, dated **2026-01-22** for **2026.02.0**, says the “brick phone” button first stopped producing a scan window and, after an approximately two-month wait for an update, NFC scans instead produced errors both with and without the physical device. Support was described as prompt but the app remained unusable after reinstall/restart. This is one extended firsthand account, not proof of a universal NFC defect. — [US App Store helpful reviews, review 13663664312](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json).
- **App selector/setup failure:** review **13745506052**, dated **2026-02-13** for **2026.05.1**, says search froze when selecting apps and an allow-only mode left more apps available than chosen; shortcuts and deletion/reconfiguration did not resolve the reviewer’s problems. The reviewer’s suspicion that positive reviews are fake is unsubstantiated and is not adopted here. — [US App Store helpful reviews, review 13745506052](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json).
- **A version-bound crash cluster, not a claim about every later build:** at least four independent **2026-11-01** reviews for **2026.43.1** report repeated crashing/freezing or a nonfunctional app. Review **14740418755** says a traveler could not use an Emergency UnBrick because the app crashed whenever opened and deleted it to regain access; review **14740461443** says reinstalling, restarting, updating iOS, and trying a new account did not help. These reports predate store metadata for **2026.44.0**, so they cannot be labeled current 2026.44.0 crashes without another test. — [US App Store recent reviews, reviews 14740418755, 14740461443, 14740435286, 14740831454, 14740427578, and 14740413600](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json); [iTunes version metadata, accessed 2026-11-08](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6448794069&country=us).
- **Unexpected broad restriction rather than a crash:** review **14714238418**, dated **2026-10-26** for **2026.43.1**, says enabling all Brick rules removed the App Store and blocked all downloads, while the reviewer expected only selected apps to be restricted. This is a concrete complaint about unclear consequences and an unwanted lockout, not proof that every Brick rule removes the App Store. — [US App Store recent reviews, review 14714238418](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json).
- **Desired feature, not a defect:** review **14744163347**, dated **2026-11-02** for **2026.44.0**, praises daily use but asks for timed unbricking that automatically rebricks after, for example, 30 minutes. This is direct evidence of a usability/feature gap the reviewer still wants, not an allegation that an existing timer malfunctioned. — [US App Store recent reviews, review 14744163347](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json).

**Brick — hands-on reviewer limitations are distinct from user reports**

- **Hands-on reviewer, 2026-03-20:** Forbes’ reviewer reports an NFC-position/angle learning curve, the lack of a daily per-app time allowance, accidental lockouts when the physical Brick was unavailable while phone/maps were needed, and disappearing notifications as part of the blocking model. The reviewer also found Screen Time/accessibility routes around protection without Strict Mode. These are reviewer-observed limitations, not a collection of customer complaints; the article’s observations also predate current app/policy changes. — [Forbes, “I Used the Brick to Limit My Screen Time—Here’s How It Went,” 2026-03-20](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbes-personal-shopper/article/brick-review/).

**Unpluq — firsthand schedule, barrier, selector, and lockout reports**

- **Recurring Android schedule/unlock trouble:** a **2024-06-30** Google Play reviewer said schedules did not consistently turn on/off and the app sometimes neither blocked nor permitted access after the barrier; the developer replied on **2024-07-01** that it had not seen those bugs and requested an email. A separate **2024-10-08** reviewer reported random failures and unsuccessful barrier completion. These are independent firsthand allegations, not proof of one root cause or current behavior after the app’s **2026-11-02** Android update. — [Unpluq Google Play reviews/replies and current update metadata, accessed 2026-11-08](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.unpluq.beta&hl=en_US).
- **App-selection failures recur into 2025:** App Store review **12646585135**, dated **2025-05-11** for **1.2.13**, says adding an app crashed the app and led the reviewer to cancel the trial. Review **13439933455**, dated **2025-11-25** for **1.2.13**, says months of attempts to scroll/search for apps produced freezes/load failures and the troubleshooting link did nothing. The [official FAQ, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq) confirms that Apple’s App Selector sometimes crashes, especially with many installed apps, and offers search/copy-paste workarounds while attributing the underlying issue to Apple. That is vendor acknowledgment of the selector problem, not independent verification of Apple’s root cause or a claim that every later release fails. — [US App Store helpful reviews, reviews 12646585135 and 13439933455](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq).
- **Disabled schedules still enforcing:** review **11821752448**, dated **2024-10-11** for **1.2.10**, gives detailed examples of a temporarily disabled weekday schedule continuing to block and “Disable for Today” making its apps unavailable before the scheduled start. The reviewer values the Tag but says these failures prevent removing Emergency Mode as a self-bypass. — [US App Store helpful reviews, review 11821752448](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json).
- **Tag scan/unblock freezes and missed schedules:** review **11768276056**, dated **2024-09-26** for **1.2.9**, says the app often froze after scanning the physical Tag, required a force quit, and sometimes neither unblocked nor blocked as expected. Review **11829908543**, dated **2024-10-13** for **1.2.10**, independently says countdown time elapsed without apps unblocking. These are historical recurring unlock-flow reports, not evidence that current **1.2.15** has the same defect. — [US App Store helpful reviews, reviews 11768276056 and 11829908543](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [current iTunes metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1673743726&country=us).
- **Residual blocks after deletion:** reviews **10971113380** (**2024-02-23**, **1.2.1**) and **11200464782** (**2024-04-25**, **1.2.4**) directly report apps remaining blocked after Unpluq was removed; the first describes restarting, permission resets, and reinstalling without recovery, while the second says revoking Screen Time access fixed it. The [current FAQ, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq) explicitly acknowledges this can happen when deletion occurs during a block, attributes it to an Apple Screen Time bug, and tells users to reinstall Unpluq and revoke its Screen Time permission. This strongly supports a real recovery limitation, but “permanently bricked” and the Apple-only attribution remain unverified generalizations. — [US App Store helpful reviews, reviews 10971113380 and 11200464782](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq).
- **Browser blocking is deliberately narrow, not a newly discovered outage:** review **12326090678**, dated **2025-02-18** for **1.2.13**, calls Unpluq effective but docks a star because non-Safari browsers are unsupported. The [current FAQ, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq) confirms iOS websites must first be visited in Safari, non-Safari browsers are not supported, and Android website blocking is not available. — [US App Store recent/helpful review 12326090678](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq).

### Inferences

- **Physical separation creates two opposite failure costs.** If software enforcement is porous, the NFC object adds friction without delivering a hard boundary; if the app/recovery path fails, the same physical dependency can prevent legitimate access. The mini-window/Screen Time reports and the traveling Emergency UnBrick failure test those opposite ends rather than one generic “hardware reliability” score ([Android bypass](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brickllc.brick&hl=en_US); [travel crash review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json)).
- **A useful evaluation matrix should separate schedule state, app-selector state, NFC state, and recovery state.** Several users explicitly report schedules or scans failing independently, so one successful manual tap cannot validate automatic schedules, and one successful schedule cannot validate emergency recovery ([Brick schedule recurrence](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Unpluq disabled-schedule report](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json)).
- **Official platform attribution is useful remediation evidence, not exculpatory proof.** Unpluq’s App Selector and Screen Time recovery instructions corroborate the symptom classes, but do not establish that every failure is Apple’s fault or that no Unpluq state-management bug contributes ([Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq)).

### Gaps

- No representative sample, telemetry denominator, or controlled reproduction establishes how often any Brick/Unpluq symptom occurs or whether two reviewers share a root cause. Store versions, device models, OS builds, and settings are often incomplete ([Brick RSS sample](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Unpluq RSS sample](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json)).
- The current Brick **2026.44.0** and Unpluq **1.2.15** releases postdate most detailed complaints above; their generic release notes do not identify which specific schedule, NFC, selector, or residual-block defects were fixed ([Brick metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6448794069&country=us); [Unpluq metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1673743726&country=us)).
- I found no controlled battery-life benchmark, hardware durability/failure-rate study, or recent independently reproduced permanent-lockout case for either hardware product. Forbes supplies hands-on observations, not laboratory failure rates ([Forbes review](https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbes-personal-shopper/article/brick-review/)).

## What AI, privacy, and transparency concerns are supported by user evidence or official architecture?

### Takeaway

Focus AI has limited but genuine review evidence about browser bypass, closed-source trust, and privacy; focus-shield has only a small public review set plus one external AI-availability issue. Official architectures establish remote AI processing for both, but there is no sourced basis here to claim recurring classifier false positives, measurable latency, a privacy breach, or typical model cost ([Focus AI Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib/reviews); [Focus AI privacy, updated 2026-10-14](https://www.getfocusai.com/legal/privacy-policy); [focus-shield reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-shield-ai-site-dist/ibmmihgadnkilmknmfmohlclogcifboj/reviews); [focus-shield issue #9](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/issues/9); [focus-shield README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md)).

### Cited Findings

**Focus AI — what named users actually reported**

- **Browser-level bypass, acknowledged:** on **2025-12-08**, Bob Johnson wrote that Guest Mode or another browser bypassed the extension and requested a system-wide app. On **2025-12-24**, the developer agreed browser tools can be bypassed in ways a system-wide app cannot and said a system-wide version was being worked toward. This is direct user evidence plus acknowledgment of the scope limitation, not a defect in Chrome’s own enforcement. — [Focus AI Chrome reviews and developer reply](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib/reviews).
- **Closed-source security concern:** on **2024-12-01**, Amit Levy praised execution but said they would switch when an open alternative appeared because of security concerns unless Focus AI became open source. This is one user’s trust preference, not evidence of malicious behavior or a vulnerability. — [Focus AI Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib/reviews).
- **Privacy and strictness concern on Firefox:** Lorenzo’s **2026-07-13** four-star AMO review says Focus AI works as hoped but pairs it with Cold Turkey to make bypass harder and expresses privacy concern because there are few users, while also saying it looks professional and trustworthy. This is ambivalent firsthand sentiment, not a reported data leak. The AMO API shows only one other textual review, a positive **2024-10-17** review, so the Firefox complaint base is extremely sparse. — [Firefox AMO public ratings API, reviews 2728103 and 2126055](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/ratings/rating/?addon=focus-ai&page_size=100).
- **Positive relevance testimony is not a controlled accuracy test:** a **2024-09-23** Chrome reviewer says Focus AI permits work-relevant pages while blocking other uses, which supports that at least one user valued contextual relevance. It does not establish an accuracy rate or prove that wrong classifications never occur. — [Focus AI Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib/reviews).

**Focus AI — official AI/privacy design, not a reported breach**

- **Current privacy disclosure, updated 2026-10-14:** during an active session, Focus AI sends the page URL, about the first **500 visible characters**, and the user’s task text to its Cloudflare server, which forwards them to Google Gemini for a relevance decision. It says this content is processed in transit and not stored, while local list/settings/history remain in browser storage. — [Focus AI privacy policy §§1–4](https://www.getfocusai.com/legal/privacy-policy).
- **Server-side records still exist:** the same policy says a random installation UUID, usage/attribution/subscription-status records, optional email, and optional uninstall responses are server-hosted; it names Cloudflare, Gemini, Resend, PostHog, Mixpanel, and ExtPay/Stripe as providers for their respective roles. “No browsing history on our server” and “no data leaves the device” are therefore not interchangeable statements. This is disclosed architecture, not evidence of sale or misuse. — [Focus AI privacy policy §§1–5](https://www.getfocusai.com/legal/privacy-policy).
- **Official wrong-block mitigation rather than a named false-positive complaint:** the Chrome listing says Quick Check gives one three-minute site window per session if “the AI got a page wrong,” and the current explanatory page acknowledges that rare mis-blocks happen because AI judgment is probabilistic. These statements confirm the vendor anticipates occasional wrong blocks; they do not supply a user-reported error rate or a specific false-positive incident. — [Focus AI Chrome listing, accessed 2026-11-08](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib); [Focus AI blocker explanation, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.getfocusai.com/ai-website-blocker).

**focus-shield — sparse firsthand feedback and exact cloud architecture**

- **One external malfunction report:** GitHub issue **#9**, opened **2026-09-18**, says AI functionality is not working and the reporter cannot enter a personal key on **Mac Chrome in the USA**. It had **zero comments** at the cutoff, so there is no public diagnosis, response, resolution, or evidence that all macOS installations fail. This is a firsthand issue report, not maintainer verification. — [focus-shield issue #9](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/issues/9).
- **Feature request, not malfunction:** Vito Colano’s **2026-06-09** Chrome review praises strict allow-list guardrails and timed AI access, then requests regex-based URLs and a visible focus timer. This directly supports a URL-granularity/feature complaint, not an allegation that AI classification failed. — [focus-shield Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-shield-ai-site-dist/ibmmihgadnkilmknmfmohlclogcifboj/reviews).
- **Resource objection in an otherwise favorable review:** Troddl Roflolmao’s **2026-01-25** five-star review says dislike of GenAI makes the extension “1000x better” because wasting time also means wasting Earth’s resources. It is sarcastic/mixed praise with a genuine GenAI-resource objection, not a negative reliability review or an energy measurement. — [focus-shield Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-shield-ai-site-dist/ibmmihgadnkilmknmfmohlclogcifboj/reviews).
- **Repository-documented cloud path:** the README says the local block page sends the blocked URL, page title, page description, typed reason, and recent attempts for that site to a Deno server; that server prompts Groq and returns validity/duration/message/follow-up fields. The default model is `openai/gpt-oss-120b`. Sites/history/reminders use `chrome.storage`, but the classification request itself is remote. — [focus-shield README, “How it works” and server configuration, accessed 2026-11-08](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md).
- **Store declaration is not proof of local inference:** current store privacy language should be read alongside the README’s explicit Deno/Groq request path. A declaration that the developer does not collect/use data for unrelated purposes does not demonstrate that page metadata never leaves Chrome; conversely, the README does not establish retention, sale, a breach, or misuse. — [focus-shield Chrome privacy listing, accessed 2026-11-08](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-shield-ai-site-dist/ibmmihgadnkilmknmfmohlclogcifboj); [focus-shield README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md).
- **Personal-key expectations need a production/self-host distinction:** the README says no API key ships with the production extension. Self-hosting instead requires npm, Deno, a `GROQ_API_KEY`, and loading the built extension unpacked; production points to the deployed bouncer. This explains why “enter my own key” is not a documented production feature, but does not resolve issue #9’s core claim that AI was unavailable. — [focus-shield README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md); [issue #9](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/issues/9).

**Brick and Unpluq — privacy conflicts and firsthand trust complaints**

- **Brick policy versus Play-listing shorthand:** the Google Play description, accessed **2026-11-08**, says “none of your usage information leaves your phone” and its data-safety summary says no data is shared with third parties. Brick’s privacy policy, updated **2026-10-21**, instead says blocked website addresses and Android blocked-app names are collected and stored on its servers; iOS blocked apps are represented by encrypted Apple Screen Time tokens that Brick cannot map to app names; and analytics/customer-support providers may store or access blocking data. These sources materially conflict if the Play sentence is read absolutely. The policy is not evidence of a breach, but it disproves using the broad listing shorthand as a complete architecture description. — [Brick Play listing/data safety](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.brickllc.brick&hl=en_US); [Brick privacy policy, blocking data and service providers](https://getbrick.com/pages/privacy-policy).
- **Unpluq allegation and explicit company denial remain a dispute:** a **2024-06-30** Play reviewer alleged that Unpluq collected/shared cookies and app usage with third parties. Unpluq replied on **2024-07-01**, “we never sell/share your data with any 3rd parties.” The review is firsthand evidence of distrust and an allegation, not technical evidence of actual third-party disclosure; the reply is a denial, not an independent audit. — [Unpluq Play review and developer response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.unpluq.beta&hl=en_US).
- **Unpluq’s own sources are not perfectly aligned:** the app privacy policy, last updated **2025-03-05** in its app section, lists account/device/crash data, session actions, schedules/blocking activity, blocked-app lists, and—where the applicable optional Android collection is enabled—other-app names/time. It says personally identifying information is not publicly/shared with third parties except as legally required and that it does not sell personal data. The Play data-safety panel, accessed **2026-11-08**, says the app may share **Personal info** with third parties, while the Play description says it does not sell or share data, personal or not. Preserve all three statements rather than declaring either the user allegation or absolute marketing wording resolved. — [Unpluq privacy policy](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/privacy); [Unpluq Play listing/data safety](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.unpluq.beta&hl=en_US).

### Inferences

- **Privacy evaluation must distinguish storage, transmission, and third-party processing.** Local settings can coexist with remote inference; encrypted iOS app tokens can coexist with server-hosted website lists; “not sold” can coexist with service-provider processing. The precise policy paragraphs are more useful than store badges or broad “private” labels ([Brick policy](https://getbrick.com/pages/privacy-policy); [Focus AI policy](https://www.getfocusai.com/legal/privacy-policy); [focus-shield README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md)).
- **Quick Check is a resilience mechanism, not evidence of classifier quality.** It limits the cost of some wrong blocks but deliberately gives a short bypass, so its value depends on the user’s goal; no cited user evidence establishes how often it is needed or abused ([Focus AI listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib)).
- **focus-shield’s availability risks are architectural as well as classifier-related.** Every validation requires the deployed Deno service, a live Groq model, and compliant structured output; issue #9 and the decommissioned-model history demonstrate different failure surfaces without proving they are the same incident ([README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md); [issue #9](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/issues/9); [maintainer PR #2](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/pull/2)).

### Gaps

- I found **no named Focus AI user report** specifically documenting a classifier false positive/false negative, classification latency, or model cost. Quick Check, the low aggregate rating, and remote Gemini calls cannot be converted into such a complaint; the examined Chrome and AMO reviews contain bypass, support, trial, security, and privacy feedback instead ([Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib/reviews); [AMO reviews](https://addons.mozilla.org/api/v5/ratings/rating/?addon=focus-ai&page_size=100)).
- I found **no focus-shield user benchmark** of wrong approvals/denials, latency, token/model cost, or energy use. The six visible textual Chrome reviews are too few and mostly positive to support a recurring-accuracy claim, while issue #9 concerns availability rather than relevance quality ([Chrome reviews](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-shield-ai-site-dist/ibmmihgadnkilmknmfmohlclogcifboj/reviews); [issue #9](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/issues/9)).
- No independent privacy/security audit or packet capture establishes retention, misuse, a breach, or actual third-party sharing for these four products. In particular, Unpluq’s reviewer allegation, company denial, policy, and Play disclosure do not resolve the underlying data-flow question ([Unpluq policy](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/privacy); [Unpluq Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.unpluq.beta&hl=en_US)).

## How do pricing, paid tiers, returns, platform availability, and support shape complaints?

### Takeaway

Brick currently offers the cleanest pricing answer—a US-listing price of **$59**, no current recurring software fee, a 30-day return route, and a two-year hardware manufacturing-defect guarantee—but users still object to account/legal requirements and unreliable software ([Brick terms, updated 2026-10-21](https://getbrick.com/pages/terms-of-service); [Brick return policy](https://getbrick.com/pages/return-policy); [arbitration objection, 2026-10-20](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json)). Unpluq’s Tag requires separate Premium, has deliberate free/per-schedule limits and customer-paid returns, and attracts subscription-value complaints; Focus AI uses three free sessions followed by **$9.99/month**, while focus-shield’s public pricing/terms and production-key model are much less clear ([Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq); [Tag-only listing](https://www.unpluq.com/products/unpluq-tag-only); [refund policy](https://www.unpluq.com/policies/refund-policy); [Focus AI homepage](https://www.getfocusai.com/); [focus-shield README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md)).

### Cited Findings

**Brick — price, warranty, returns, compatibility, and consumer terms**

- **Current first-party pricing/model:** the US storefront displays **$59** for Brick; terms updated **2026-10-21** say the current model is a one-time physical-hardware purchase with the mobile app/cloud services included without recurring subscription fees, while reserving the right to offer future opt-in subscriptions. The same terms give a seven-day public-promotion price-adjustment route under specified conditions. — [Brick homepage, accessed 2026-11-08](https://getbrick.com/); [Brick terms §§6.1–6.10](https://getbrick.com/pages/terms-of-service).
- **Current hardware guarantee:** terms §§12.1–12.2 provide a **two-year** replacement guarantee for manufacturing defects/hardware failure on Brick Standard and Brick Night devices, six months for accessories, and exclusions for misuse, accidents, normal wear, water damage, consumables, resale/gifting/third-party marketplace acquisition, and missing original-purchaser proof. Replacement shipping instructions are promised within two business days when replacement is warranted, with replacement shipping within five business days after the stated trigger. This is policy coverage, not evidence that every reported software failure qualifies for a hardware replacement. — [Brick terms, updated 2026-10-21](https://getbrick.com/pages/terms-of-service).
- **Return/shipping policy:** the current return page says request within **30 days of delivery**, ship within 14 days of approval using a supplied prepaid label, and return items in original condition for a full item refund after inspection. The FAQ says Brick ships worldwide with local fulfillment; the shipping policy lists processing in **1–2 business days**, warehouse/region estimates, checkout-calculated shipping, and potential duties outside local regions. Regional tax/duty and delivery cost therefore remain address-dependent. — [Brick return policy, accessed 2026-11-08](https://getbrick.com/pages/return-policy); [Brick FAQ](https://getbrick.com/pages/faq); [Brick shipping policy](https://getbrick.com/policies/shipping-policy).
- **Compatibility/account requirements:** the FAQ requires **iOS 18+** or **Android 12+**, permits up to **10 custom modes**, and says remote bricking is possible while physical unbricking still requires the device except for emergency overrides. Current terms require downloading the app and creating an account for full hardware functionality. Reviews object to this software dependency: **2026-10-26** reviewers asked why email/account verification was necessary, and a **2026-10-20** reviewer deleted the app over mandatory binding arbitration/class-action-waiver language. The last is a consumer-terms objection, not a legal judgment about enforceability. — [Brick FAQ](https://getbrick.com/pages/faq); [Brick terms §4.1](https://getbrick.com/pages/terms-of-service); [US App Store recent reviews, reviews 14726886277, 14713800881, and 14691585086](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json).
- **Support evidence is mixed, not uniformly absent:** the **2026-01-22** NFC reviewer called replies prompt despite unresolved failures, while the **2025-12-21** travel reviewer and **2026-02-13** setup reviewer reported unanswered questions or unsuccessful email-only remedies. These are separate firsthand support experiences, not a measured support SLA. — [NFC review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [travel and setup reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [current support contact in Brick returns page](https://getbrick.com/pages/return-policy).

The FAQ describes Free as two apps, one schedule, and two barriers; Premium as unlimited schedules/more barriers and nominally unlimited apps with **up to 49 per schedule**. This means “unlimited apps” and “49 per schedule” describe different scopes, not necessarily contradictory limits. — [Unpluq Tag-only listing, accessed 2026-11-08](https://www.unpluq.com/products/unpluq-tag-only); [Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq).

Review **10936410872**, dated **2024-02-13**, calls the two-app/one-schedule free tier inadequately disclosed. Review **11768276056**, dated **2024-09-26**, characterizes the subscription as “renting” the Tag at an exorbitant price and contrasts it with Apple Screen Time. - **Kickstarter/subscription allegation needs calibration:** review **13278053283**, dated **2025-10-17**, claims the reviewer backed the Kickstarter before a subscription model appeared and calls it “bait and switch.” This is one unverified backer allegation; no campaign terms or receipt was supplied in the review, so the stronger representation-of-terms claim is not established. — [US App Store helpful review 13278053283](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json).

These are current scope rules that matter to the price/value comparison. — [Unpluq FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq); [iTunes metadata, accessed 2026-11-08](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1673743726&country=us).

**Focus AI and focus-shield — browser availability and evaluation cost**

- **Focus AI pricing/trial:** the current homepage offers the first **three sessions free**, then **$9.99/month**. ### Inferences

- **One-time hardware pricing does not remove software dependency risk.** Brick includes cloud/app service without a current subscription, but the account requirement, app crashes, schedule state, and emergency-access failures determine whether the purchased object remains useful ([Brick terms](https://getbrick.com/pages/terms-of-service); [crash cluster](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6448794069/sortby=mostrecent/json)).
- **Unpluq’s recurring subscription is central to both value and recovery.** The physical Tag does not replace Premium, and lost-Tag fallbacks are capped; customers therefore pay for a continuing service while accepting deliberate emergency/limit rules ([Tag listing](https://www.unpluq.com/products/unpluq-tag-only); [FAQ](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq)).
- **Trial fairness and lock strength are different purchasing questions.** Focus AI’s three sessions may permit a feature trial but do not test every long-session/edge-case behavior; focus-shield’s no-key production model removes self-host setup while concentrating availability in a maintainer-operated service ([Focus AI homepage](https://www.getfocusai.com/); [focus-shield README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md)).

### Gaps

- Storefront prices are US/access-date snapshots, not a complete regional price/tax/shipping history; no sourced basis supports comparing total landed cost across every supported Brick/Unpluq destination ([Brick shipping policy](https://getbrick.com/policies/shipping-policy); [Unpluq refund/shipping guidance](https://www.unpluq.com/policies/refund-policy)).
- The **2024-01-21** shipping-country complaint does not establish current country support, and the **2025-10-17** Kickstarter allegation was not checked against contemporaneous campaign promises/receipts; both should remain historical, unverified company complaints ([Unpluq Play reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.unpluq.beta&hl=en_US); [App Store reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1673743726/sortby=mosthelpful/json)).
- No current public focus-shield subscription/refund terms or guaranteed hosted-service SLA was found in the examined store/repository sources. The store’s existence and README’s deployed Deno server do not establish either permanence or a price ([store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-shield-ai-site-dist/ibmmihgadnkilmknmfmohlclogcifboj); [README](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/blob/main/README.md)).

## Which problems have been explicitly fixed or mitigated, and what remains unresolved?

### Takeaway

There are real resolutions—Brick emergency replenishment now exists, Focus AI’s developer declared the November 2025 outage/support failures fixed, and focus-shield’s maintainer tested several August 2026 repairs—but none warrants declaring all complaints stale ([Brick Emergency UnBrick help, edited 2026-10-06](https://support.getbrick.com/en/articles/7950209); [Focus AI review/reply, 2025-11-10](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/focus-ai-website-blocker/ngboiiifeoioghodfglighnahefnjbib/reviews); [focus-shield PRs](https://github.com/moritzWa/focus-shield/pulls?q=is%3Apr)).
