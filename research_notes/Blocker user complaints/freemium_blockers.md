# Freemium blocker complaints: BlockSite and AppBlock

**Research cutoff: 2026-11-08.** Scope: BlockSite by BlockSite LP and AppBlock by MobileSoft s.r.o., across their browser extensions and iOS/Android apps. The note covers blocking, schedules and limits, synchronization, circumvention and lockout, battery/performance, permissions, privacy, billing, support, and ownership. Official pages and store metadata were checked on the cutoff date unless a source date says otherwise.

## How to read the evidence

- **Firsthand allegation** means a named store/community reviewer reports an experience. It is not a reproduced defect or evidence that other users are affected. **Recurring** means at least two independent reports of a theme in this sample, not that it is common.
- **Technical research** means an investigator describes a method and artifacts. **Policy-confirmed** means the vendor documents the behavior. **Deliberate restriction** means a paywall, unlock rule, or free-tier limit is intentional. **Platform limitation** means the vendor attributes the boundary to iOS, Android, Safari, or an OEM; that attribution is still the vendor's unless independently verified.
- **Independent corroboration** is used narrowly. A news article about a research report corroborates its existence and reported methodology, not necessarily a second experiment. Store permission warnings describe capability, not actual sensitive-data collection.
- Severity describes **potential impact**, not frequency: **Critical** = plausible loss of essential device access or data; **High** = core blocking, entitlement, privacy, or money can fail; **Medium** = substantial friction, inaccuracy, or feature mismatch; **Low** = narrower UX issue.
- Apple records below are US App Store RSS reviews; the linked page contains the cited numeric review ID. Dates and versions are store-supplied at retrieval and reviews can be edited. Google Play links contain stable review IDs. Unless stated, reviewers did not supply an OS build or device model. No prevalence claim follows from these samples.

# BlockSite

## Takeaway

The recurring operational complaints are **blocking or password protection not holding**, **timers/cooldown behaving unexpectedly**, **paid entitlement/cancellation and support friction**, and **sync not matching the broad cross-device impression**. A single detailed current-era Chrome report alleges serious content-script memory retention, and one iOS reviewer alleges heavy background battery use; neither establishes fleet-wide performance. The strongest independent security evidence is Q Continuum's February 2026 study of a specific older Chrome payload, while the current privacy policy itself discloses broad processing. The 2018 reporting is relevant history but cannot establish present ownership or behavior.

## Cited Findings

### 1. Blocking, schedules, and limits — **High**

- **Firsthand allegations:** On iOS 4.4.5, [14757787334, 2026-11-06](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) says website blocking stopped after updating. [14712151140, 2026-10-25](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) says three apps in one category share 15 minutes rather than receiving 15 minutes each, and also reports days with less or unlimited time. The first observation may be a rule-semantics misunderstanding; the inconsistent-time allegation would be a defect if accurately configured. No official source found states the intended per-app versus per-group behavior, so neither interpretation is promoted to fact.
- **Android allegations:** Erin Arnett's [2025-05-01, 2.9.2.3.8740 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.blocksite&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=85b76171-c0ce-4260-a1bc-cf59de020f81) reports both blocked sites remaining accessible and block lists disappearing. BlockSite replied on 2025-05-08 that the issues were “likely” a known Android Accessibility limitation and promised a stability update. John Thornton's [2025-03-31 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.blocksite&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=9a5f8e57-f7c9-4b27-9871-9df89d76fe48) alleges intermittent and random blocking; Ryan Green's [2026-07-26, 2.13.3.10167 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.blocksite&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=3444167f-6a19-4ff9-af13-4de3fe61d134) alleges cooldown sometimes relocks immediately and sometimes remains open for 5+ minutes. BlockSite called the latter behavior unexpected and requested a recording.
- **Policy-confirmed platform remediation:** [BlockSite's Android Accessibility guide](https://blocksite.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/17524362978845-BlockSite-not-blocking-or-disabling-itself-Accessibility-Android) says Android battery preservation can turn Accessibility off. It recommends disabling battery optimization and battery saver, locking BlockSite in recents, and enabling device administration where applicable. Those steps address service persistence; they do **not** prove that every failed block is Android's fault or that BlockSite itself drains the battery.
- **Android permission incident:** Michael Murray's [2026-08-03, 2.13.4.10238 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.blocksite&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=ed9bf8a4-1490-4f02-9ed7-278da9638090) generalizes from his failure to all Android devices. That generalization is unsupported. BlockSite's same-day response requests the device model/Android version and states Accessibility is required for app and website blocking.

### 2. Password bypass, uninstall protection, and account lockout — **High**

- **Firsthand iOS allegations across versions:** [12280404099, 2025-02-06, 4.1.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=7/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) reports no password request before block-list edits. [14354256092, 2026-07-27, 4.4.2](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) gives a specific path—Add website → Groups → Apps—to remove apps without the password. [14555045721, 2026-09-15, 4.4.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) again alleges editable settings after paying $100 for lifetime access and no support response. Recurrence supports a test hypothesis, not a conclusion that all password routes remained broken through 4.4.5.
- **Official iOS remediation:** [Password Protection troubleshooting](https://blocksite.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/33219522246045-Password-Protection-is-not-asking-for-a-password-iOS) says protection remains inactive until the email is verified, to prevent lockout, and a fully closed/freshly opened app is needed because an app left in the background may not prompt. This separates an **activation/configuration state** from the reviewers' alleged alternate edit path. [Deletion prevention](https://blocksite.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/34723015157789-How-to-prevent-deleting-apps-on-iOS-using-Screen-Time) relies on Apple's independent Screen Time restriction: Content & Privacy Restrictions → iTunes & App Store Purchases → Deleting Apps → Not Allowed, preferably with a Screen Time passcode.
- **Chrome allegation:** M D P's [2025-01-09 Trustpilot review](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/blocksite.co) says clicking the extension profile and logging out disables the lock in two clicks. No Chrome build or independent reproduction is available. A mirrored [MrMapyc Chrome review](https://extwatch.com/extensions/blocksite-block-websites-stay-focused-eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh), displayed “15 days ago” at cutoff (approximately 2026-10-24), alleges involuntary logout, a white sign-in screen, no support response, and a three-site free tier. This is the reverse problem—account lockout rather than bypass—and remains mirror-only.
- **Severity boundary:** No BlockSite allegation equivalent to AppBlock's factory-reset account was found in the selected evidence. Password failure is high impact because it defeats the core commitment mechanism, but it should not be described as bricking a device.

### 3. Battery and extension performance — **High allegation, unverified prevalence**

- **Firsthand battery allegation:** [13361508974, 2025-11-05, iOS 4.2.6](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=4/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) attributes 30–40% of daily battery use to BlockSite with zero screen time and says battery life roughly doubled after deletion. The reviewer explicitly guesses at a bug. There is no energy trace, device/iOS build, control period, or reproduction, so this is not proof of malicious behavior or typical drain.
- **Detailed but mirror-only Chrome allegation:** Cassan Bob's [ExtWatch-mirrored review](https://extwatch.com/extensions/blocksite-block-websites-stay-focused-eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh), displayed “9 days ago” at the 2026-11-08 cutoff (approximately 2026-10-30), reports Chrome 155 on macOS. It says long-lived tabs reached 2–3 GB after 24 hours versus 100–200 MB in a no-BlockSite profile; one 1.18 GB heap allegedly had ~96% of 6.25 million closures attributable to `overlays.js`/`content.js`, plus 456 detached `activeContent.js` script tags. The reviewer infers repeated injection without cleanup. The heap snapshot and test profile are not provided, so even the causal interpretation remains an allegation.
- **Version boundary:** That report predates current Chrome **8.0.1 (2026-11-05)** and does not establish whether 8.0.1 fixes or retains the behavior. No controlled battery benchmark or current-version memory reproduction was found.

### 4. Sync and cross-device expectations — **Medium**

- **Policy-confirmed current matrix:** [BlockSite mobile/desktop sync](https://blocksite.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/32609425309853-Does-BlockSite-sync-between-mobile-and-desktop-browsers) says desktop-to-desktop is automatic upon login; Android-to-Android is supported but requires manual activation; Android-to-desktop is being rolled out; and iOS sync is unavailable. [Desktop sync](https://blocksite.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/32609316831901-How-to-sync-my-block-list-across-multiple-browsers) currently names Chrome and Edge and requires the latest extension and the same login. [Separate-list limitation](https://blocksite.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/32609506700957-Can-I-disable-sync-or-have-different-block-lists-on-different-desktop-browsers-devices) says separate desktop lists cannot be maintained while browsers share the same account.
- **Complaint versus current scope:** John Thornton's [2025-03-31 Android review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.blocksite&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=9a5f8e57-f7c9-4b27-9871-9df89d76fe48) alleges paid PC/phone sync did not work. It predates the current rollout language and cannot prove the present state. Conversely, broad marketing such as “Sync on Multiple Platforms” should be read against the matrix rather than treated as universal iOS/Android/desktop parity.
- **Configuration-loss allegation:** Erin Arnett's disappearing-list report is included under blocking because the developer attributed likely cause to Accessibility; the evidence does not establish whether local state, account sync, or another defect caused it.

### 5. Permissions, privacy, and independent traffic research — **High**

- **Policy-confirmed current processing:** The [BlockSite privacy policy](https://blocksite.co/privacy), modified **2026-07-16**, discloses processing that can include browsing/activity, search, ads/commerce and AI-related information, as well as market-intelligence/affiliate uses. It documents Android Accessibility and iOS VPN-based operation and says opting out of some processing may remove features. This is broader than “only the block list is stored”; it is a disclosure, not by itself proof of unlawful use.
- **Permission capability is not telemetry:** The Chrome store/mirror warns that the extension can read and change websites visited. Blocking requires substantial page access, and the generic warning does not prove that passwords, cards, or all history were actually transmitted. Conversely, a blocking purpose does not negate the separately disclosed data uses above.
- **Technical research, older payload:** [Q Continuum's report and BlockSite section](https://github.com/qcontinuum1/spying-extensions#blocksite-block-websites-and-stay-focused), published with its 2026-02-08 [Substack write-up](https://qcontinuum.substack.com/p/spying-chrome-extensions-287-extensions-495), identifies exact Chrome ID `eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh`. Its pipeline used Chromium in Docker, an MITM proxy, synthetic URL workloads, and regression between URL payload size and outbound bytes. Its worked example decodes a request to `https://category.blocksite.co/category` with fields including `ul`, `ink`, `pel`, and `rr`, containing target/previous URL-like values and a referrer-like value. The payload has `org: "6.9.7"`.
- **Calibration:** The report supports that the investigated build emitted the decoded fields in the researchers' setup. It did not test current **8.0.1**, and its scanning source code/full setup was withheld, limiting independent replication. CSO's [2026-02-16 article](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4132712/leaky-chrome-extensions-with-37m-installs-caught-shipping-your-browsing-history.html) independently reports the study and wider findings, but is not a second BlockSite packet-capture experiment. Neither source proves every install exfiltrates every visited URL or establishes malicious intent.
- **Historical research:** [AdGuard, 2018-07-24](https://adguard.com/en/blog/big-star-labs-spyware.html) alleged that Block Site and other Big Star Labs apps/extensions collected browsing history through then-current browser/mobile mechanisms; [Ars Technica, 2018-07-25](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2018/07/snoopware-installed-by-11-million-ios-android-chrome-and-firefox-users/) reported the same campaign. Matching product identifiers make it relevant BlockSite history. They do not show what BlockSite LP's current apps do.

### 6. Billing, free-tier pressure, and support — **High**

- **Firsthand iOS billing/support allegations:** [14098287882, 2026-05-23, 4.3.6](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) alleges no response to support/cancellation emails, a subsequent $30 charge, and no finance reply. [14584006706, 2026-09-23, 4.4.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1474967653/sortby=mostrecent/json) alleges the website would not permit trial cancellation, support did not reply, and a yearly membership was charged. “Scam” is the reviewers' characterization, not a legal finding.
- **Duplicate-charge allegation and response:** On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/blocksite.co), Frankie Dun's **2026-09-15** review alleges two annual charges totaling **£80**, product failure, and refund refusal. BlockSite replied **2026-10-11** that separate subscriptions/accounts might explain duplicate charges and requested both receipts and possible email addresses. This documents a dispute and support response, not confirmation of either party's account.
- **Entitlement defect allegation:** Ryan Green's [2026-07-26 Android review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.blocksite&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=3444167f-6a19-4ff9-af13-4de3fe61d134) says at least five “renew premium” prompts appeared one week after a $100 one-time purchase, without a visible Premium settings entry. BlockSite's 2026-08-03 reply says this was unexpected and requests the account email and recording.
- **Deliberate monetization versus defect:** Louis's [2025-02-06 Trustpilot review](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/blocksite.co) alleges animated, difficult-to-dismiss upgrade pop-ups; the mirrored MrMapyc review alleges only three sites in the free tier. These concern product/paywall design and UX, not unexpected blocking defects. A different mirrored reviewer says a small “continue to limited” option exists, which cautions against saying payment is technically mandatory, but neither mirror establishes current universal tier limits.
- **Policy-confirmed billing routes:** [BlockSite's terms](https://blocksite.co/terms) say iOS subscriptions are cancelled through Apple; extension purchases use Stripe/PayPal/support routes. The terms generally exclude refunds except where law requires. Platform cancellation procedures do not resolve allegations that support failed to respond or that a website flow malfunctioned.

### 7. Ownership and legal/regulatory record — **High uncertainty, not high-confirmed wrongdoing**

- **Current first-party fact:** BlockSite LP is the contracting operator in the current [terms](https://blocksite.co/terms) and [privacy policy](https://blocksite.co/privacy).
- **Historical fact and unresolved chain:** The 2018 sources discuss Big Star Labs. No primary acquisition document found in this research establishes the full chain from that entity to BlockSite LP. Q Continuum calls Big Star Labs an apparent extended arm of Similarweb; that is researcher characterization/speculation, not proof that Similarweb owns BlockSite now.
- **Legal boundary:** No court judgment, regulator finding, consent decree, or current legal determination against BlockSite was established by the cited research. “Spyware,” “scam,” and “malicious” in headlines/reviews must not be converted into legal or intent conclusions.

## Inferences

1. **Test locking as a state machine, not one menu.** Email verification, warm versus fresh app launch, alternate Add/Groups edit routes, logout, uninstall, and Screen Time restrictions are separate states; one successful password-prompt test does not cover the others.
2. **Sync claims need a platform matrix in the UI.** A user's natural interpretation of cross-device sync is broader than BlockSite's current desktop/Android/iOS support. Explicit support and manual-activation requirements would prevent some disappointment even without new engineering.
3. **Privacy evaluation should be build- and endpoint-specific.** Q Continuum is more probative than generic permission complaints but less current than 8.0.1; the policy and a repeat capture of the exact installed build answer different questions and are both needed.
4. **The performance reports merit targeted replication.** A 24-hour two-profile tab test with saved heaps and an iOS energy log would be decisive; deleting the app or reading a review count is not.

# AppBlock (MobileSoft)

## Takeaway

AppBlock's best-corroborated complaints concern **iOS usage totals, stale/paused schedules, background geofencing, and Settings/Screen Time circumvention**; official documentation acknowledges important parts of those behaviors and gives remediation. Recurring **Premium entitlement loss** can disable paid blocking and deserves separate treatment from sync. Strict Mode includes intentional friction and paywall limits, but one severe iOS review alleges that residual restrictions led to a factory reset. Extensions are separately configured and currently free; Safari is a legacy, unreliable offering rather than a current macOS counterpart. Privacy is primarily local for core iOS blocking, with explicit server-side exceptions—not blanket “all data stays local.”

## Cited Findings

### 1. Usage limits, schedules, pauses, and opens — **High**

- **Recurring firsthand allegations:** [13745343058, 2026-02-13, 4.7.3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) reports more usage than a 1h30m allowance; [13754983667, 2026-02-16, 4.7.3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) reports a 60-minute limit ending around 25–35 minutes. [13807945812, 2026-03-02, 4.8.1](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) describes idle/background time, repeated warnings, spontaneous unblocking, and a pause arithmetic discrepancy.
- **Additional firsthand reports:** [13814030446, 2026-03-04, 4.8.1](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) says same-day opens exhausted scheduled limits; [13866572062, 2026-03-19, 4.8.3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) alleges out-of-schedule blocks and a displayed two-opens balance becoming zero; [14065615528, 2026-05-14, 4.9.2](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) alleges glitchy counters, early/late limits, and pauses requiring reloads. These reports do not share one proven root cause.
- **Policy-confirmed OS-data problem and remediation:** [Usage limits do not work](https://appblock.app/usage-limits-do-not-work-2/) says iOS 26 and iOS 18 can report Screen Time incorrectly, including minimal/zero usage counting toward limits. AppBlock says Apple does not expose the underlying real usage data, recommends refreshing blocking, disabling **Screen Time → Share Across Devices** when multiple devices are used, updating/restarting, and using **Open Limit**, which does not depend on system usage data. This is an official acknowledgment of an OS/input-data limitation, not proof that AppBlock has no accounting defects of its own.
- **Stale-block acknowledgment:** [Inactive schedules remain blocked](https://appblock.app/my-apps-are-still-blocked-even-though-my-schedules-are-inactive/) says iOS may not immediately apply pause/schedule changes and that a bug can also prevent normal activity resuming. It recommends **Profile → Help → Refresh blocking rules**, restart, and support if needed. The wider [iOS troubleshooting hub](https://appblock.app/help/apple/troubleshooting) also documents local-time schedule shifts after timezone/DST changes and browser/rule configuration causes for missed blocks.
- **Android schedule-loss allegation:** laura hardy's [2026-04-23, 7.17.0 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cz.mobilesoft.appblock&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=b96859c7-9ed3-4835-a80c-ccf9db31f80e) says pausing accidentally deleted schedules and no backups existed. The developer denied a recent pause/disable change but did not dispute the individual experience; support investigation was requested. One review does not establish intended deletion behavior.

### 2. Location schedules — **High**

- **Firsthand allegation:** [14091824364, 2026-05-21, 4.9.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json), whose author says “iPhone 17,” reports TikTok did not unlock at the gym or relock on leaving until AppBlock was manually opened. Device and OS build were not independently verified.
- **Official corroboration and remediation:** [Location-schedule troubleshooting](https://appblock.app/why-are-my-location-schedules-not-working-on-iphone/) requires Location Services, **Always**, and **Precise Location**. It recommends toggling Location Services, recreating the schedule, refreshing blocking, and restarting. It specifically says iOS 18 background-location changes sometimes caused activation only after opening AppBlock, that iOS 26.3 improved this, and that some devices still misbehave. This is strong corroboration of the reported pattern, not proof every geofence failure is Apple's fault.

### 3. Strict Mode circumvention and platform limits — **High**

- **Recurring firsthand iOS allegations:** [12765648751, 2025-06-12, 3.15.0](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=5/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json); [14227161671, 2026-06-25, 4.9.10](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json); [14494710992, 2026-08-31, 4.11.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json); [14516966118, 2026-09-06, 4.12.1](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json); and [14705101347, 2026-10-24, 4.13.5](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) report disabling AppBlock through Settings or Screen Time despite Strict Mode. Some mention a device or Screen Time passcode; their exact settings differ.
- **Policy-confirmed platform limitation:** [Why AppBlock cannot fully block all iPhone settings](https://appblock.app/why-appblock-cannot-fully-block-all-iphone-settings/) says Apple does not permit third-party apps to block Settings/Screen Time directly. Its Shortcuts redirect worked on iOS 16/17, Screen Time became unreliable on iOS 18, and neither Settings nor Screen Time blocking is reliable through Shortcuts on iOS 26+. [Related guidance](https://appblock.app/i-cant-block-screen-time-with-shortcuts-on-ios-18/) says iOS 26.4+ allows a Screen Time passcode separate from the device passcode; having a trusted person hold it is the recommended stronger barrier.
- **Other documented bypasses:** The [iOS limitations hub](https://appblock.app/help/apple/limitations) says private/incognito browsing and in-app browsers may bypass web blocking, a blocked page may briefly flash, websites only work with Time or Location schedules rather than Open/Usage Limits, and AppBlock cannot block individual Reels/Shorts/Stories inside an app. These are scope boundaries, not necessarily regressions.
- **Android Strict Mode allegation:** Quincy Rogers's [2026-08-28, 7.19.4 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cz.mobilesoft.appblock&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=6d77a3ae-374b-4574-b98b-2b3dc82d185b) says the app displays the long password needed to bypass its protections. The current [Android limitations](https://appblock.app/help/android/limitations) separately document Safe Mode, dual apps, floating windows, OEM Settings/Accessibility paths, unsupported/private browsers, and brief flashes. Whether a displayed emergency phrase is adequate friction is a design judgment; it is not equivalent to the iOS permission-revocation route.

### 4. Lockout, deletion prevention, and emergency unlock — **Critical single allegation; recurring High reports**

- **Most severe allegation:** [13837240075, 2026-03-11, iOS 4.8.3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) alleges Strict Mode persisted after deletion, the App Store disappeared, account deletion/support workarounds failed, and a factory reset was ultimately required. The review also says Apple Support did not resolve it. No device diagnostics, Apple statement, or independent reproduction exists. It is therefore a **Critical firsthand account**, not proof AppBlock normally survives deletion or requires wiping a phone.
- **Other lockout/deletion allegations:** [14201614263, 2026-06-19, 4.9.9](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) says blocks remained after trial cancellation and emails went unanswered; [14699329755, 2026-10-22, 4.13.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) reports being unable to delete without an unknown Screen Time password; [14465614756, 2026-08-24, 4.11.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) says Delete opened a retention screen even though the reviewer believed Strict Mode was off. “Virus” in the last title is rhetoric and is not adopted here.
- **Official stale-restriction explanation:** [Why can't I uninstall apps even after turning Strict Mode off?](https://appblock.app/why-cant-i-uninstall-apps-even-after-turning-strict-mode-off/) says AppBlock uses iOS Screen Time to restrict deletion and iOS occasionally leaves the restriction in place after Strict Mode is disabled. Remediation is Refresh blocking rules, restart, verify **Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions → Deleting Apps**, and allow time/support follow-up. Cancelling a subscription is not documented as a way to remove system restrictions.
- **Deliberate emergency friction:** [Android Strict Mode/unblocking](https://appblock.app/help/android/strict-mode-and-unblocking) documents PIN/Charger/Approval/Timer routes and emergency unblocking. Timer/Follow Schedules requires a text-retyping challenge and waits that escalate with repeated requests; other methods use a one-time support code. AppBlock explicitly calls the friction intentional. It also says support aims to respond within 24 hours and codes require stable internet. That design explains difficulty but does not excuse an unavailable emergency path.
- **Deliberate free-tier limit:** The same official section says **free Android Strict Mode expires after 24 hours**; battery optimization killing AppBlock and Follow schedules ending can also deactivate it. A free user seeing Strict Mode end at 24 hours is seeing a policy-confirmed limit, not necessarily a bug.

### 5. Premium entitlement, billing, and support — **High**

- **Recurring Premium-recognition allegations:** [13833070458, 2026-03-10, 4.8.3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) says annual Premium never appeared. [14357251374, 2026-07-27, 4.11.0](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) and [14451377946, 2026-08-20, 4.11.4](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) describe intermittent lost Premium plus pause problems. [14756761537, 2026-11-06, current 4.14.0](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) says yearly Premium is forgotten every few days and blocking stops. These are independent reports, but they do not isolate store receipt, RevenueCat, account, or app-state causes.
- **Android account/support allegation:** Greg Colvin's [2026-06-26, 7.18.2 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cz.mobilesoft.appblock&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=2b77da3e-427d-4c73-a5ca-c5f36af640c2) says email and Google account setup consistently timed out after an annual purchase and in-app help was unavailable. Support replied on 2026-06-27 requesting direct email.
- **Official entitlement remediation:** [iOS payment Help](https://appblock.app/how-to-restore-purchases-on-iphone/) says use the purchasing Apple ID/account and **Profile → Restore Purchase**. Its cross-platform guidance says Premium entitlement syncs through the same AppBlock account. [Android restoration](https://appblock.app/how-to-restore-my-purchase-appblock-premium-not-recognized-2/) says Restore purchases and warns reinstalling erases blocking settings unless a backup exists. **Entitlement sync is not the same as block-rule sync.**
- **Policy-confirmed billing:** [iOS payment Help](https://appblock.app/help/apple/payments-and-subscriptions) says renewal and trials are managed through Apple, cancellation occurs in Apple Subscriptions, and refund approval is Apple's discretion via `reportaproblem.apple.com`; duplicate Apple IDs can create separate subscriptions. [Android trial guidance](https://appblock.app/how-does-the-trial-period-work-2/) says a new-user seven-day trial converts to yearly Premium unless cancelled, with a day-five reminder; cancellation/refund routes run through Google Play and AppBlock support. A post-trial charge after no cancellation is therefore expected under the policy, while nonfunctional entitlement is not.
- **Deliberate paywall complaint:** David B.'s [2026-07-30, 7.19.0 review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cz.mobilesoft.appblock&hl=en_US&gl=US&reviewId=617b657d-f406-44e0-bf6c-504b089752ff) objects to Pause being Premium and another subscription. The developer confirmed Premium placement and offered one-time Lifetime. This is a packaging dispute, not a technical defect.

### 6. Sync, Chrome/Edge/Brave, and Safari parity — **Medium**

- **Firsthand mobile sync complaint:** [12944293449, 2025-07-27, iOS 3.18.0](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=5/id=1515753232/sortby=mostrecent/json) says signing in on additional devices did not bring over block lists and also criticizes long update interstitials. Current privacy documentation describes an **opt-in cloud Backup** for restoring configuration on the same or another device; that is not evidence of continuous automatic rule sync across every device.
- **Extension policy-confirmed status:** [AppBlock extension FAQ](https://appblock.app/help/extensions/faqs/) says Chrome, Edge, and Brave extensions are free with all features unlocked and [mobile-app synchronization is unsupported](https://appblock.app/synchronize-my-extension-with-app/). Thus rebuilding extension plans after reinstall is consistent with a real product limitation even if the triggering failure is a defect.
- **Mirror-only Chrome allegation:** Tokisaki Kurumi's [ExtWatch review](https://extwatch.com/extensions/appblock-block-sites-stay-focused-lniombipmmhhighllijmcdncnefpjloi), displayed “2 months ago” at cutoff, says the extension window and settings stopped opening; only full reinstall helped; all plans then needed rebuilding because there was no sync; and support reportedly could not reproduce the issue after two weeks. Exact absolute date, browser build, and extension build are unavailable.
- **Safari firsthand complaints:** The [2024-07-21 review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/appblock-block-websites/id6470454261?mt=12) alleges timed Strict Mode repeatedly stopped despite 90-day configuration and reinstalls. The developer proposed cache clearing, full permissions, disabling conflicting extensions, and `safari@appblock.app`. [12413257994, 2025-03-12](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/appblock-block-websites/id6470454261?mt=12) reports per-site permission friction, a Deny-based Strict Mode bypass, and expected iOS block-list portability. The developer confirmed the extension cannot block macOS apps or fully sync iOS lists, is a separate free product, and said Safari otherwise requires manual per-site approval.
- **Current Safari status:** [Safari troubleshooting](https://appblock.app/i-updated-my-macos-and-my-safari-extension-does-not-work/) now says newer macOS releases are incompatible, problems cannot reliably be fixed due to Apple/Safari changes, the extension was removed from the public offering, and previous installers may still find it inconsistent. This current Help page is newer operational guidance than the still-visible listing; the listing should not be read as a promise of ongoing support.
- **Permission calibration:** Safari's warning can mention reading/altering pages, history, and potentially sensitive form data. It communicates capability. The reviewer's quotation and the developer's denial do not independently prove what data was accessed.

### 7. Android reliability, battery/background behavior, and permissions — **High platform sensitivity**

- **Policy-confirmed OEM/background limitation:** [AppBlock Android limitations](https://appblock.app/help/android/limitations) says Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei, and other OEM battery managers can disable Accessibility or stop background activity. It recommends excluding AppBlock from battery optimization and checking Accessibility/background permissions. This covers **service termination caused by battery management**, not a demonstrated complaint that AppBlock itself uses excessive energy.
- **Android 17 platform interaction:** AppBlock's [2026-09-10 article](https://appblock.app/androids-new-advanced-protection-affects-appblock-from-blocking/) says Advanced Protection/Device protection can revoke Accessibility, breaking website blocking and floating overlays while app blocking continues. It provides a route to relax the relevant protections. This is scoped vendor documentation, not a claim that standard Android 17 universally disables AppBlock.
- **Permissions model:** The Android [privacy policy](https://appblock.app/privacy-policy-google-play) describes Accessibility and related blocking permissions. Accessibility is powerful because it supports detection/enforcement, but the permission itself does not prove misuse.
- **Evidence boundary:** Unlike BlockSite, this research did not find a suitable controlled or detailed AppBlock battery-drain allegation to report. It would be misleading to convert background/OEM troubleshooting into a battery-consumption complaint.

### 8. Privacy and ownership — **High sensitivity; current policy evidence**

- **Current operator:** Both current policies identify **MobileSoft s.r.o.**, Českoskalická 650, 549 41 Červený Kostelec, Czech Republic, ID **025 73 857**. The Chrome listing also names MobileSoft s.r.o. No conflicting current first-party owner was found.
- **iOS local-first model with exceptions:** The [iOS policy](https://appblock.app/privacy-policy-app-store) says Apple supplies opaque encrypted tokens for chosen apps/categories/sites and MobileSoft cannot map them back to names. Core blocking, schedules, and detailed usage statistics are local. However, account/device registration, push tokens, optional cloud backups, support diagnostics, purchases, onboarding, analytics/attribution, and crash reports involve servers or processors. Backups can contain schedules, selected-place coordinates/radius/name, website/keyword rules, Strict Mode settings, usage summaries, and opaque tokens. Support diagnostics contain configuration metadata. Therefore “all data stays local” would be inaccurate.
- **Android server-side exceptions:** The [Android policy](https://appblock.app/privacy-policy-google-play) discloses uploading installed package names for categorization, some analytics events containing package names acted upon, and narrow crash diagnostics that can include blocked-page URLs. Accounts, backups, support, and purchases also involve server processing. Describe these as documented exceptions to local processing, not proof of indiscriminate browsing-history sale.
- **Legal/regulatory boundary:** No court judgment, regulatory action, or ownership dispute concerning current AppBlock was established in this research. Store complaints and broad permissions do not supply that missing evidence.

## Inferences

1. **Separate three clocks and three kinds of sync.** Device/local schedule time, Apple-reported usage time, and pause/cooldown state can diverge. Premium entitlement sync, optional configuration backup/restore, and live rule sync are likewise distinct features and should not share one “Synced” indicator.
2. **Strict Mode needs an honest recoverability model.** iOS permission revocation, Apple Screen Time passcodes, stale deletion restrictions, Android Accessibility/OEM controls, and AppBlock's intentional emergency friction are different paths. Product copy should make residual risk and recovery prerequisites visible **before** a long lock is activated.
3. **Open Limit is a useful fallback, not equivalent accounting.** It avoids unreliable Apple usage totals but counts launches rather than minutes; recommending it solves one measurement problem by changing the commitment rule.
4. **The extension and Safari complaints are partly expectation failures.** Free extensions without app sync and a withdrawn Safari product should not be presented as mobile-like macOS clients. At the same time, “expected limitation” does not explain the reported unopenable Chrome window.
5. **Privacy copy should enumerate exceptions.** “Core selections use opaque Apple tokens and core rules remain local unless Backup/support/etc.” is supportable; an absolute local-only claim is not.

## Gaps

1. **No lab reproduction or representative prevalence estimate.** The store/community record is a targeted complaint sample, not a random sample. Ratings, review counts, or repeated wording must not be extrapolated to all users.
2. **Review chronology is imperfect.** Reviews can be edited; a store-supplied version is not necessarily the first affected build. Most reviews omit OS/device details. The detailed Chrome mirror reports have relative dates only, and their extension builds are unknown.
3. **Mirror and access limitations.** ExtWatch is a mirror, not the first-party Chrome review feed. Trustpilot direct retrieval returned 403 during research; search extraction supplied the quoted texts, dates, and company replies but not review-specific permalinks. The linked Trustpilot profile is therefore the available direct source page.
4. **Firefox evidence is thin.** Firefox review retrieval returned 404, and no comparable current Firefox-specific complaint set was established. Nothing here should be read as clearing or condemning Firefox builds.
5. **BlockSite current-version gap.** Neither Q Continuum's 6.9.7 payload nor Cassan Bob's pre-8.0.1 memory report tests current Chrome 8.0.1. A fresh build-pinned capture and heap profile are needed.
6. **Battery causality is unresolved.** BlockSite has one anecdotal iOS battery report and one detailed but unreproduced Chrome memory report; AppBlock has official background/OEM troubleshooting but no comparable controlled energy evidence. No controlled battery benchmark was found for either.
7. **Root cause cannot be apportioned from reviews.** Apple's opaque Screen Time data and Android/OEM permission changes can cause real failures, but vendor attribution does not exclude application state bugs. Conversely, failure after updating does not by itself prove a vendor regression.
8. **Safari documentation tension.** AppBlock's visible store listing and current withdrawal/incompatibility Help coexist. Current Help is the better operational source, but no exact public-removal date was established.
9. **Ownership chain is incomplete.** Current first-party contracting operators are documented, but that is not an exhaustive ultimate-beneficial-ownership investigation. A primary record connecting 2018 Big Star Labs to current BlockSite LP was not found, and Similarweb ownership was not independently established. Corporate speculation is excluded from factual conclusions.
10. **Legal/regulatory gap.** The cited historical and current research is not a court or regulator determination. No current legal liability, statutory violation, or malicious intent is inferred for either company.
11. **Support outcomes are mostly open.** Developer replies often request email, receipts, recordings, or device details. Public records rarely show a final fix/refund, so neither resolution nor non-resolution should be assumed unless explicitly cited.
12. **Sync terminology is easy to overread.** BlockSite's support varies by platform; AppBlock separates Premium entitlement, optional backups, and unsupported app-to-extension sync. No universal bi-directional, real-time sync claim is supported for either product.
