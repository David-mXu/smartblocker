# Popular mobile blocker complaints: Opal, one sec, ScreenZen, and Forest

**Scope and evidence rules.** Research cutoff: **2026-11-08**. Products were verified as Opal (`opal.so`, `com.withopal.opal`), one sec (`one-sec.app`, iOS `wtf.riedel.one-sec`, Android `wtf.riedel.onesec`), ScreenZen (`screenzen.co`, `com.screenzen`), and Forest (`forestapp.cc`, Seekrtech, `cc.forestapp`). “Recurring” below means that the research found at least two independent reports of the same theme, not that it is common among all users. “Anecdote” means one report or a narrow feedback thread. Store reviews are allegations, not independently reproduced defects. Dates are review/update dates and versions are the store-supplied app versions; reviews can be edited, so neither necessarily marks the bug’s first appearance. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the OS version and device are not stated. Official pages and store metadata were accessed on the cutoff date. No prevalence or “most users” claim is supported by these samples.

## Opal (`opal.so`): which failures defeat blocking, and which company, billing, and OS issues persist?

### Takeaway

The strongest recurring complaint is not merely that determined users can bypass Opal: it is that the shield or usage clock behaves incorrectly on its own—apps are blocked before use, remain blocked during allowed periods, fail to block, or are recategorized after being allowed. Those failures persisted in several 2026 versions after the 4.x redesign. Billing-after-cancellation and difficult human support recur in 2026 reviews, while official policies show why App Store/Play refunds must go through the platform; privacy allegations are much weaker than the documented local-processing model.

### Cited Findings

**Recurring, core-promise failure: missed blocks, false limits, and inability to unblock.**

- **2026-03-11, iOS app 3.151, OS unstated:** one paid user reported that Opal sometimes failed to block apps after their limit and “frequently” blocked apps not used at all; “Reloading blocks” did not help. The same reviewer saw a weekly total under four hours alongside 5h15 elsewhere in Opal, undermining confidence in its measurements. — [US App Store review, “Too many bugs”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Same date/version, independent iOS review:** another user reported that an allowed “break” opened a bank app while its Zelle browser flow stayed blocked, and that apps showed their daily limit reached without having been opened. Resetting limits then reduced the reviewer’s focus percentage, linking a recovery action to a score penalty. This is an anecdote about Zelle, but the false-limit/reset problem repeats the preceding report. — [US App Store review, “FIX IT!!”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **2026-07-07, iOS app 4.3.2, OS unstated:** a long-time free-tier user reported waking to TikTok/Instagram already at their limits despite not having used them, inaccurate screen time, and no practical reset path after the redesign. This places a reset/clock complaint after the 4.3.x reset fixes rather than only in older releases. — [US App Store review, “Inconsistent Blocking/New Update”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **2026-08-06, iOS app 4.8, OS unstated:** repeated “halfway there” notifications appeared outside the limited app, Opal failed to eject the user after the limit, and blocking rules remained inconsistent. — [US App Store review, “So many bugs, so little time”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **2026-08-17, Android app 4.7.0, OS unstated:** “doesn't block the apps… I have to remind opal.” Opal responded that it specifically experienced problems on **Redmi/Xiaomi** phones because of manufacturer policies. This is device-family-specific evidence, not proof that every Android device has the same failure. — [Google Play review and developer response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=090f1432-d1e6-4975-b545-cb5dea60ee16)

**Recurring blocking-accuracy and schedule/time-zone problems.**

- **2026-08-21, Android app version not stated; 2026-08-30, Android app 4.9.0; Android OS unstated:** two reviewers independently said apps repeatedly moved from “always allowed” back to distracting/blocked; one needed those apps for work. Opal called the app-category problem a “known bug” and said a fix was being developed. This defeats both accurate blocking and user intent. — [2026-08-21 review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=125c08cf-699a-4a7f-8cbb-590c8caa383d); [2026-08-30 review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=07bfafc7-0b0c-4901-a9d7-73574971b009)
- **2026-08-13, Android app 4.7.0, OS unstated:** a reviewer said screen-time tracking was substantially inflated, including 24 hours in one day, while blocking itself worked. Opal acknowledged a known measurement issue and said it was working on more reliable numbers. — [Google Play review and response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=f0ad1cdf-0ab9-47fe-895f-f8f8fb159273)
- **Latest directly dated time-zone report found: 2026-11-04, Android app 4.17.0, OS unstated:** after travel, a scheduled rule applied two hours early. Opal replied on November 5 that it would test and escalate if it could reproduce the issue. A separate **2026-08-10 Android 4.7.0** review reported blocking on wrong days/times and alleged a charge after cancellation. Together these are recurring schedule complaints; only the November report isolates travel/time-zone conversion. — [Time-zone review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=2171457d-f9e6-4c2c-bac1-95a1a5554957); [wrong-day/time review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=b6bb0f43-c69d-49f3-8419-8ef224f5abb5)
- **Older but still relevant accuracy example, 2024-11-06, iOS app 3.85.1:** blocking Facebook also blocked Messenger even though Messenger was meant to remain allowed. This is a single app-grouping anecdote and should not be generalized to the 2026 rules engine. — [US App Store review, “Not Impressed”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)

**Screen Time shield/bypass evidence needs a distinction between accidental failure and deliberate circumvention.**

- **2024-08-23, iOS app 3.74:** a former Pro subscriber cancelled because blocks were easy to disable; Shortcuts-based hardening was buggy and effectively interfered with Settings. The reviewer blamed Apple’s third-party constraints as well as Opal and did not identify which difficulty mode was used. — [US App Store review, “Too easy to get around”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **2026-06-12, iOS app 4.0.5:** a website timer reportedly worked rarely and sometimes blocked a site never used; separately, a standard active block allowed settings changes after a short wait, weakening its friction model. The reviewer did **not** say this was a paid Deep Focus session, so this does not falsify Opal’s specific “Deep Focus can’t be cancelled” claim. — [US App Store review, “The new update ruined it”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- Opal currently advertises “Deep Focus” as the maximum difficulty that cannot be cancelled or bypassed. The strongest core failure supported by the reviews is therefore **unintended unreliability and bypassable ordinary rules**, not a demonstrated universal Deep Focus bypass. — [Current Opal App Store listing, accessed 2026-11-08](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/opal-screen-time-control/id1497465230)

**Focus Score, resets, account state, and what changed over time.**

- **Old Focus Score evidence, 2023-08-25, iPhone 11/iOS 16.6 thread:** after 33 minutes of screen time and 14 pickups, a user said Opal still displayed a distraction-free 10.0 flow state. Another participant on iOS 16.6 said schedules often required opening Opal before the next session activated but had improved days later. This is useful version-specific evidence, but it is not evidence of the current Focus Score. — [Opal community, “Decline in accuracy and reliability,” August 2023](https://community.opal.so/t/decline-in-accuracy-and-reliability/1853)
- **2025-10-09, iOS app 3.131.1:** re-logging after Opal’s prompt allegedly reset a 288-day streak to zero while retaining the 200-day trophy; the reviewer said support blamed being logged out. **2026-03-11, iOS 3.151** separately reported focus-percent loss when resetting false limits. Account state and metric integrity are thus recurring themes, but the streak restoration outcome is unknown. — [Streak review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [reset/metric review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Fixed/remediated old architecture:** a **2022-08-23, iOS 2.65** reviewer described the old VPN blocking unrelated websites, turning itself off, and requiring manual reopening; another **2022-11-05, iOS 3.4** review said moving from VPN to Apple’s newer blocking setup stopped hotspot/call interference. Opal’s 2022 engineering article explains that it had built a local VPN before adopting Apple’s Screen Time API. Do not present VPN-era hotspot/call bugs as current. — [August 2022 review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [November 2022 review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Opal engineering background, 2022-04-07](https://www.opal.so/blog/opals-screen-time-framework)
- **Partial fixes, not a clean bill of health:** the store’s 4.3.1 notes said “resets are back,” usage would accumulate after a limit, blocking would self-repair in more cases, and a streak-calculation issue was fixed. Other current release notes mention detecting/repairing OS-level shield problems and preventing an expired limit from relocking after midnight. The later iOS 4.3.2 and 4.8 complaints above show that false limits and inconsistent shields were still being reported; they do not prove every listed fix failed for everyone. The latest iOS metadata at cutoff was **4.22.1, released 2026-11-04, minimum iOS 18**, with Live Activities rather than a blanket reliability fix. — [Opal listing/version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/opal-screen-time-control/id1497465230); [iTunes metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1497465230&country=us)

**Recurring company-level billing/paywall complaints; distinguish allegations from store policy.**

- **2026-04-02, iOS app 3.155:** a reviewer alleged a US$99 charge despite cancelling a trial early and no Opal email response; **2026-08-10, Android 4.7.0** separately alleged a charge after cancellation. **2026-10-06, Android 4.13.0** alleged an unauthorized GCash charge. These are independent payment complaints but not proof that cancellation was correctly completed or that Opal initiated a store charge. — [iOS trial review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Android August review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=b6bb0f43-c69d-49f3-8419-8ef224f5abb5); [Android October review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=ea0c722e-0d5a-4493-b998-87b76df4fdc8)
- **Free-tier/paywall friction is current, not just old:** **2025-07-25, iOS 3.120.2** complained about an annual charge after a trial and inability to obtain an Apple refund; **2026-08-29, Android 4.9.0** called three days too short and risky relative to subscription price; **2026-09-12, Android version unstated** objected to reaching payment only after onboarding. Opal replied that users could decline the trial and continue free, or ask support for a longer evaluation. These reports establish perceived paywall pressure, not a universal current price—prices varied by storefront, plan, and region. — [2025 iOS review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [three-day trial review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=e43faa2e-9ce2-4a3d-8a36-6b2d187cd26f); [onboarding-paywall review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=c5617e0f-b3a7-4ea4-b473-08847bdc03de)
- **Official cancellation/refund boundary:** Opal says uninstalling does not cancel; Apple trials/subscriptions should be cancelled more than 24 hours before conversion/renewal; Apple alone can refund App Store purchases, and Opal cannot override an Apple rejection. Website/Stripe purchases have a 30-day refund route through Opal, whose human-support target is two business days. Store-purchase refund frustration therefore cannot automatically be attributed to Opal refusing a refund it controls. — [Opal cancellation/refund help](https://www.opal.so/help/how-can-i-cancel-my-subscription); [Apple trial explanation](https://www.opal.so/help/how-your-free-trial-with-apple-works); [Opal terms/privacy and refund policy](https://www.opal.so/terms)

**Support and company-conduct evidence is mixed by channel.**

- **2026-01-19, iOS 3.145:** cloned old rules and a limit stuck at maximum were compounded by what the reviewer experienced as AI-chat troubleshooting and Apple-Mail-dependent support. **2026-10-01, App Store version displayed 3.106:** another user described being routed through community forums instead of a direct contact form and criticized the annual cost. These support-friction reports recur, but the second entry’s old version number cautions against using it to date the app build precisely. — [January review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [October review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Calibrating counter-evidence:** on the August–November 2026 Android reviews cited above, Opal often replied within one to five days, acknowledged some known bugs, supplied settings guidance, or requested an engineering reproduction. That is responsive visible conduct even when the underlying problem remained unsolved; it should not be erased by the iOS “no response” anecdotes. — [Example: time-zone response, 2026-11-05](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=2171457d-f9e6-4c2c-bac1-95a1a5554957); [category-bug response, 2026-08-30](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=07bfafc7-0b0c-4901-a9d7-73574971b009)

**Privacy and sensitive-permission claims.**

- Opal’s current terms say iOS uses Apple’s Screen Time API, Android uses Accessibility and Usage Stats, and detailed app-usage data from those mechanisms remains on-device. Opting into Social Features shares daily aggregate screen time with confirmed friends through Opal servers; contact numbers are hashed on-device for friend discovery. Thus “all data stays local” would be too broad, but the policy offers a specific rebuttal to claims that detailed usage is uploaded by default. — [Opal terms/privacy, privacy principles and Social Data](https://www.opal.so/terms)
- **Anecdote, not verified misconduct:** **2026-10-09, Android 4.14.0** said “Probably selling a lot of your data”; Opal pointed to the policy without confirming the allegation. A **2026-07-10, iOS 4.5** reviewer perceived Face ID onboarding and questions as demands for biometrics/excess data, but supplied no evidence that Opal received biometric templates. These reveal onboarding trust concerns, not established data sales or biometric access. — [Android review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=5882b81e-6f4a-4c6d-8a38-c8ebc5916d5b); [iOS review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json)

### Inferences

- The most damaging Opal defects are **state and time errors**: a blocker that blocks before use, fails after a limit, or leaves an allowed transaction inaccessible makes its core enforcement adversarial rather than merely porous. The recurrence across iOS 3.151→4.8 and Android 4.7→4.17 suggests ongoing regression risk rather than one retired VPN implementation, without estimating affected-user share. — [Representative iOS evidence](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1497465230/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [latest time-zone evidence](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=2171457d-f9e6-4c2c-bac1-95a1a5554957)
- Android has distinct failure surfaces: manufacturer background restrictions, Accessibility/Usage Stats, category reassignment, and schedule conversion. iOS evidence centers on Apple Screen Time shields, shield reset state, Focus calculations, and redesign usability. A single cross-platform explanation would miss those differences. — [Opal platform privacy description](https://www.opal.so/terms); [Xiaomi-specific response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.withopal.opal&hl=en_US&reviewId=090f1432-d1e6-4975-b545-cb5dea60ee16)
- Company complaints combine a genuinely frustrating support experience with platform payment boundaries. A fair report should separate “charged after cancellation” allegations, which need receipt/store investigation, from Opal’s documented inability to issue Apple/Google refunds. — [Official refund policy](https://www.opal.so/help/how-can-i-cancel-my-subscription)

### Gaps

- No comparably specific **2025–2026 Focus Score-only** defect was found; recent evidence concerns screen-time totals, focus percentage, and streaks. The exact current score algorithm and whether it has been corrected remain open.
- The November 2026 travel report is strong evidence of one time-zone failure, but no second equally precise travel/time-zone report was found. Wrong-day/wrong-time reports support the broader schedule theme, not necessarily the same timezone root cause.
- No independent audit, regulator decision, or technical dataset verified selling of detailed usage data, biometric collection, or systematic deliberate dark patterns. Those are user perceptions/allegations, not established company conduct.
- Store samples cannot determine refund eligibility, exact cancellation timestamps, country-specific price, tax, or whether a user accidentally subscribed via a different account. No valid product-specific Trustpilot corpus was located, so no Trustpilot prevalence claim is made.

## one sec (`one-sec.app`): where do Shortcuts, Accessibility, interventions, parity, and subscriptions disappoint?

### Takeaway

The iOS complaint pattern is delayed or fragile Shortcuts-based interception and occasional schedule/Safari failures; the current Android pattern is more severe missed/false interventions tied to Accessibility, battery restrictions, and device manufacturers. Website support is explicitly absent on Android rather than a broken promised feature. Free-tier pricing resentment is persistent, while duplicate/lifetime-charge and subscription-restore incidents are serious but less frequent; recent visible developer replies are generally prompt, even when they do not resolve the device-specific problem.

### Cited Findings

**Recurring iOS Shortcuts setup/lag/fragility; old failures are not all current.**

- **2024-02-05, iOS app 3.7.4, OS unstated:** the intervention arrived after roughly five seconds of scrolling, allowing consumption before the pause, and several desired apps were unavailable in setup. **2024-06-24, iOS 3.9.1** separately described selecting an app twice, an incomplete/common-app selector, and launches that sometimes opened a Safari page rather than the intended app. These reports directly attack immediate, accurate interception. — [Lag/app-list review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1532875441/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [setup/linking review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1532875441/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Latest-year continuation, 2026-06-25, iOS app 5.2.6:** after performing the Shortcuts steps, the reviewer said “it didn’t even work” and felt the paid product was mainly instructional container content. **2025-06-22, iOS 4.1.6** reported scheduled-block apps available several times per day and setup reappearing whenever the app was opened to reactivate blocking. This establishes continued fragility after the 2024 setup reviews, not the same exact selector bug. — [2026 review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1532875441/sortby=mostrecent/json); [2025 schedule/setup review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1532875441/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- The store description retrieved at cutoff still says one sec uses **Shortcuts Automation** for app-open interventions and one target app is free; current iOS metadata was **6.1.1, released 2026-11-02, minimum iOS 15**. Older Shortcuts criticism therefore cannot be dismissed merely by asserting that all iOS intervention has migrated away from Shortcuts. Current Google Play metadata separately listed Android **3.0.0, updated 2026-10-26**; the sampled Android complaints run through 2.9.5, so they do not directly test 3.0.0. — [US App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/one-sec-screen-time-focus/id1532875441); [iTunes metadata](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1532875441&country=us); [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US)

**Android Accessibility/background failures: missed and false interventions persist in 2026.**

- **2026-01-28, Android app 2.5.14, OS unstated:** Accessibility switched itself off after the first day, making the paid app ineffective; the developer acknowledged provider-specific permission difficulties. **2026-05-13, Android 2.6.14** repeats Accessibility automatically turning off alongside an in-app intervention failure after subscribing. — [January review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=1049643f-b7b9-4f12-a6a5-5e27f2d87701); [May review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=f227b2a9-489e-4af4-b2ba-8d8817f80187)
- **2026-02-17, Android 2.5.14, Xiaomi HyperOS:** re-intervention set for 15 minutes worked only the first time despite permissions. **2026-06-22, Android 2.7.0, Xiaomi Pad 8:** interventions appeared only sometimes; the developer recommended “Open new windows while running in the background” and no battery restrictions. These are concrete OS/device-specific missed-intervention reports. — [HyperOS review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=deb8573b-a7ef-4dad-a6a9-62dee592d50d); [Xiaomi Pad 8 review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=d3c4264c-fd11-4f0c-b4e9-fccf63ef4b52)
- **False/frozen interventions:** **2026-04-26, Android 2.6.7** reported Telegram attempts incrementing while Telegram remained open. The developer identified this as a known **Samsung** Accessibility bug where in-app actions were read as new opens. **2026-02-17, Android 2.5.14** reported Reels blocking leaving Instagram permanently stuck until manually unblocking/reblocking. These are different failure modes from merely missing an intervention. — [Samsung/Telegram review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=77c854cc-39c5-4917-be22-b2782c0aebf8); [Reels lockup review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=bbad6323-3d57-474e-9ced-7099cfe2ed81)
- **Official limitation/root-cause documentation:** Android setup requires Accessibility and overlay permissions, with app-usage access as an alternative to Accessibility; instructions differ by Android version. The troubleshooting guide says missed interventions can result from Intentional App Switching, missing Accessibility, battery optimization, and manufacturer background settings. Its manufacturer guide explains that OEM battery saving terminates background apps and lists separate Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei, Oppo, and OnePlus workarounds. It also says an **Android 13** patch addressed an animation-freeze case; that old fixed animation issue should not be conflated with 2026 missed interventions. — [Android permission setup](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3034754); [Intervention not showing, edited nine months before access](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3262274); [Additional Android Background Settings](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3310402)
- **Official iOS-26-specific false trigger:** one sec’s 2025-10-29 guide explains that iOS 26’s full-screen screenshot editor backgrounds the current app, so returning can legitimately look like a new app open and cause another intervention. Disabling the editor or enabling Intentional App Switching is the documented workaround. This is an OS behavior/product-interaction limitation, not proof of arbitrary random triggering. — [one sec iOS 26 screenshot guide, 2025-10-29](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/5719810)

**Safari, websites, widgets, and platform parity.**

- **Old Safari failures, no recent corroboration found:** **2023-12-04, iOS 3.7.2** reported a website intervention whose button no longer worked; **2024-06-02, iOS 3.8.2** said only one site was intercepted, the return button failed, and refreshing bypassed the intervention. They are useful historical failure reports but should not be described as unresolved in 2026 without newer evidence. — [2023 website review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=4/id=1532875441/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [2024 Safari review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1532875441/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Official Android website limitation:** the help center says browser extension functionality is absent on Android because Android browsers do not offer the required extension support. **2026-04-09, Android 2.6.4** complained that the app blocked apps but not websites; the developer explicitly confirmed website blocking was not yet available on Android. This is parity disappointment backed by an official limitation, not a fabrication. — [Official website-intervention guide](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3309954); [Android review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=1087f530-8458-4b1a-952e-4a9b99931ca1)
- **Broader paid parity complaint:** **2026-06-02, Android 2.6.17** said an annual purchase lacked several iOS features and was inconsistent. The developer acknowledged Android development began later and that it was trying to catch up. **2026-03-25, Android 2.6.4** specifically missed specific block times and reported high background battery/time usage, but the latter was not independently quantified. — [June parity review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=e45b73a1-12ff-41f9-bb36-1b59e7d9b6ee); [March block-time/battery review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=wtf.riedel.onesec&hl=en_US&reviewId=42887490-0d28-4f01-a7c3-1646e671ddd2)
- **Safari is intentionally multi-step on iOS:** users must enable the extension in iOS Settings → Safari → Extensions, allow it, and add a website from Safari’s extension button. This makes setup confusion plausible but does not itself prove the 2024 bypass remains. — [Official iOS Safari setup](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3035586)
- **Widget evidence is weak but specific:** a feedback page accessed 2026-11-08 contains an iOS report that the widget “only said 1x open” and a merged request saying countdown/live activity worked for manually started but not scheduled blocks. The visible post says “Jan 5” without a year, so it is an undated product-board report, not a current recurrence finding. — [one sec iOS widget feedback](https://feedback.one-sec.app/p/widget-ios)

**Free tier, subscription/trial/refund, and support.**

- **Persistent free-tier resentment:** the current store description says one target app is free and more require Pro. Complaints recur from **2024-04-15 iOS 3.8.2** through **2026-07-22 and 2026-09-10, Android app versions not stated**, where users said one app offered too little practical value. ## ScreenZen: when do delay, strict blocking, counting, permissions, and monetization backfire?

### Takeaway

ScreenZen has the clearest very recent failure cluster: iOS 4.0.0 and Android 2.9.4 reviews in September–November 2026 describe apps that cannot be unlocked, blocks that apply at zero usage, unlimited repeated sessions, or restrictions persisting after deletion. Strict-mode tradeoffs are concrete—forgetting a password can lock the owner out, while deletion/break/cooldown loopholes can defeat enforcement. The product has moved beyond a purely tip-supported image into promotions, subscriptions/passes, and Halo, producing recurring 2026 complaints about ads and extra engagement, though its terms still keep store refunds with Apple/Google.

- ScreenZen’s most serious failure is **rule-state divergence**: its counter says zero while the shield says exhausted, its unlock delay finishes without a corresponding shield release, or its open budget does not decrement across repeated sessions. - Strict settings create a genuine two-sided design problem. Easy password/deletion/break paths undermine self-binding, but forgotten passwords or stale shields can imprison legitimate apps, including work/banking apps. Recovery design is therefore part of core reliability, not secondary UX. — [Forgotten-password evidence](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1541027222/sortby=mostrecent/json); [banking false-block evidence, 2026-08-05, Canadian iOS 1.2.178](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1541027222/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- 2026 monetization complaints are not simply resistance to any payment: reviewers specifically object when promotional taps, games, streaks, and alternative activities compete with the intended delay or when a paid “no ads” expectation appears unmet. This creates a business-model/core-purpose tension even if the basic blocker remains free. — [GB ads evidence](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=1541027222/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [Canadian Practice-game evidence](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1541027222/sortby=mosthelpful/json)

### Gaps

- Only one direct forgotten-password case was found, all on iOS 1.2.178. No verified password-reset outcome or equivalent current Android strict-lock recovery report was located, so passcode lockout should remain an anecdote.
- Android reviews rarely expose OS build/device. Battery optimization and Accessibility recurrence is well supported, but no measured average battery cost or universal OEM matrix can be inferred.
- There is no complete official feature-parity matrix for iOS/macOS/Windows/Android in the collected sources. Different version numbers and repeated reports establish different release trains, not which entire feature set is absent on each platform.
- Marketing “free/no subscription” and terms covering subscriptions may refer to different core versus optional features. Without dated historical terms, the exact transition date and whether any previous “lifetime no ads” promise legally covered **product promotions** as opposed to third-party ads remain unresolved.

This documents older list-editing friction, not current blocking accuracy. — [US App Store review, “Editing the allowed app list is PAIN”](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=866450515/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Current Android Allow List bypass:** **2026-09-11, Android 5.26.0** said removing Forest from Recents during a session made every app open regardless of the Allow List; Forest asked for an in-app bug report. **2026-10-02, Android 5.28.1** independently reported Allow List failures, including after clearing tabs. This recurring recents/background dependency defeats the block without requiring the user to change Forest settings. — [September review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=3662777f-ba3a-405a-8983-00558bda2dbe); [October review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=e783cee5-221f-43bc-8fa9-32dd53ade2fd)
- **Complete Deep Focus failure:** **2026-10-29, Android 5.33.0** said no apps blocked even with all permissions. Forest told the reviewer to check **Accessibility Service and Usage Access**, restart, and send device/version details. This supports an Android permission/background failure surface, not an iOS Screen Time diagnosis. — [Google Play review/response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=e63ec34d-0686-4d05-9fe3-a6db7a3442f3)
- **Official limitation/monetization boundary:** **2026-08-06, Android 5.23.0** complained of penalties for changing music or answering messages because Allow List was paid. Forest replied that Deep Focus is free but App Allow List is Pro/Plus, and users can disable Deep Focus to avoid penalties; **2026-10-16, Android 5.31.0** response also confirmed pausing is Pro/Plus. Unwanted withering without an allow list is therefore partly a tier/design limitation, while apps opening despite Deep Focus is a defect. — [Allow-list tier response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=05a9c303-ca9c-40d3-846e-dffdaacb7358); [Pause-tier response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=bf54b272-e68d-478c-b3e3-6e76e80a4e25)
- **Official Android background-process limitation, FAQ dated 2026-03-02:** Forest says Android can automatically close background processes, causing trees to wither or allowing use of non-allow-listed apps while planting. It provides device-specific settings for Huawei, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Samsung, and Pixel but warns that even after all settings are applied, Forest may still be closed because the developer cannot control the OS. This directly supports the Android Recents/background failure mechanism and is an explicit unresolved platform limitation, not an iOS fault. — [Forest official Android background FAQ, 2026-03-02](http://help.forestapp.cc/zh-CN/articles/13904563-%E4%BD%9C%E4%B8%9A%E7%B3%BB%E7%BB%9F%E8%87%AA%E5%8A%A8%E5%85%B3%E9%97%AD%E8%83%8C%E6%99%AF%E7%A8%8B%E5%BC%8F); [Xiaomi permission guide, 2025-11-21](http://help.forestapp.cc/zh-CN/articles/12761260-%E5%B0%8F%E7%B1%B3%E6%9D%83%E9%99%90%E8%AE%BE%E5%AE%9A%E6%95%99%E5%AD%A6)
- **Historical core-mechanic change:** **2025-10-21, iOS 4.99.0** edited an older positive review to one star because leaving an app no longer withered the tree automatically and instead required an allow list. Treat this as a version-specific user-reported behavior change, not proof that every current Forest mode behaves that way. — [US App Store helpful reviews, page 3](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=3/id=866450515/sortby=mosthelpful/json)

**Gamification pressure and cheating: rewards can conflict with focus, but distinguish bugs from chosen bypasses.**

- **Challenge-induced interruption:** **2026-09-03, iOS 5.24.0** said Focus Challenge counted timer starts rather than the multiple trees already generated by one long session, encouraging the user to stop/restart every 30 minutes. This incentive directly opposes uninterrupted work. — [US App Store review](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=866450515/sortby=mosthelpful/json)
- **Engagement pressure:** **2026-09-09, Android 5.25.0** said digital overconsumption and Plus’s 3x coins caused anxiety, especially after real-tree utility was capped; **2026-10-04, Android 5.29.1** said challenges/new trees kept the user engaged and did the opposite of the original purpose. **2026-10-17, Android 5.31.0** similarly said “boost” rewards distracted from the timer. These independently recur in the latest releases. — [Anxiety/Plus review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=33fa0de3-1048-4075-9b3e-7d2e60ba3145); [engagement review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=4ca843f9-aea1-41f3-987f-2bbbb3b55203); [boost-reward review](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=e96ceb20-d563-4f42-9e6b-5de24d43beb7)
- **Cheating/bypass calibration:** **2026-10-31, Play review listing old app version 4.95.0** told users opening active apps or web versions avoided restrictions; Forest acknowledged unreliable blocking and requested details. This is a user-described deliberate workaround with an old version marker, while the 5.26/5.28 Recents-Allow List reports above are stronger current bug evidence. ### Inferences

- Forest depends on users trusting an accumulated visual history more than a conventional blocker does. Losing trees or coins is therefore as damaging as allowing a distracting app: it retroactively removes the user’s apparent work history and weakens future motivation. Recurrence from 4.x to Android 5.33 makes this the clearest persistent core concern. — [2024 progress-loss baseline](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=866450515/sortby=mosthelpful/json); [2026 5.33 persistence](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=f9e516ef-7dca-4ebc-973b-30fd0701c9c4)
- Platform-specific mechanisms matter. Android Deep Focus requires Accessibility/Usage Access and is vulnerable to Recents/background behavior; iOS complaints concentrate on old allowed-list editing, changed withering behavior, timers, and redesigned flows. Cross-platform account sync exists, but store purchases intentionally do not transfer between Apple and Android. — [Android Deep Focus response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=e63ec34d-0686-4d05-9fe3-a6db7a3442f3); [official platform purchase FAQ](http://help.forestapp.cc/zh-TW/articles/12749108-%E6%88%91%E5%B7%B2%E7%B6%93%E8%B3%BC%E8%B2%B7%E9%81%8E-ios-android-pro%E7%89%88%E6%9C%AC%E7%9A%84-forest-%E7%82%BA%E4%BB%80%E9%BA%BC%E6%8F%9B%E4%BA%86%E5%AE%89%E5%8D%93-%E8%98%8B%E6%9E%9C%E6%89%8B%E6%A9%9F%E5%BE%8C%E9%82%84%E9%9C%80%E9%87%8D%E6%96%B0%E8%B3%BC%E8%B2%B7)
- The monetization backlash has two separable components: legitimate uncertainty/resentment over a changed paid-app relationship and documented new friction (ads, reward boosts, Plus prompts, challenges, removed Classic Home). Developer assurances that Pro is permanent answer only the first part; they do not resolve the second. — [Pro permanence response](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=f9e516ef-7dca-4ebc-973b-30fd0701c9c4); [ad-reward evidence](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.forestapp&hl=en_US&reviewId=695c2356-1922-4e1f-ac62-126f90160e8c)

### Gaps

- The current five-real-tree cap is supported by two user reviews but not by a current official terms page in this corpus. Trees for the Future verifies total funded planting, not per-user quotas, Plus eligibility, or whether more planting events will occur.
- The privacy policy exposes extensive categories and advertising services, but no independent data-flow audit or evidence of sale was found. Privacy concerns should be framed as collection/tracking scope and user trust, not misconduct proven by reviews.
- Android OS/device is often absent. Only the Redmi Pad Pro disappearing-tree report and one unrelated Redmi 9i display report have explicit hardware here; broader manufacturer-specific conclusions would exceed the evidence.
- Some Play reviews contain old app-version markers or describe another platform, suggesting edited reviews or user error. The notes avoid using the “auto-updated on iOS” Play entry as Android evidence and do not assign old 4.95 behavior to current 5.33.
- The current complete Free/Pro/Plus entitlement matrix and historical dates of each feature migration were not located. The report therefore limits itself to developer-confirmed Allow List/pause boundaries, Pro permanence, and Classic Home/My Favorite changes.
