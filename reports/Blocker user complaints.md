# Blocker user complaints: products, failure modes, and company practices

**Research and audit cutoff:** predominantly **2026-11-08**. Older cases retain their original dates; a recent storefront version or documentation update is not evidence that an old complaint persists or was fixed. This report covers **31 separately identified products and projects**, including those for which little or no firsthand complaint evidence was found.

## Executive summary

The strongest cross-product pattern is not simply that people dislike being blocked. It is **ambiguous enforcement state**: a product can appear active while a scheduled session never starts, a timer counts the wrong usage, a shield remains after a limit expires, configured websites remain reachable, or access is restored without the product saying why. Complaints are especially disruptive when users cannot distinguish an intentional lock from a defect and cannot reach a reliable recovery path.

The broadest supported themes are:

| Pain pattern | What the evidence supports | Representative examples, not a prevalence ranking |
|---|---|---|
| **False-active protection** | Apps, rules, or schedules look enabled, yet the promised intervention does not occur. | Freedom's Android schedule allegation; Opal time-state reports; FocusMe Firefox and Android-keyword reports; SelfControl allowlist issues; Forest blocking and tree-loss reports. |
| **Incorrect time, allowance, or entitlement state** | Products lose track of elapsed time, usage, active limits, premium status, or earned rewards. | StayFocusd timer reviews; ScreenZen unlock/counting complaints; Jomo and Roots Screen Time accounting reports; AppBlock recurring Premium-recognition allegations. |
| **Overblocking, bypass, or recovery friction** | Blocking may affect an unlisted site, disappear through a setting/browser route, or survive an attempted exit. | SelfControl collateral blocking; AppBlock Screen Time bypass allegations; Cold Turkey and FocusMe removal accounts; Plucky's deliberately slow support/uninstall design. |
| **Platform configuration is part of the product** | Apple Screen Time, Android Accessibility/OEM behavior, browser private modes, extensions, syncing, and host application integrations all affect results. | Apple Screen Time allowances; Android Advanced Protection; LeechBlock Android changes; BlockSite's Safari listing; MCP host compatibility. |
| **Payment and company practices can compound failure** | Complaints include renewal and refund disputes, expensive short trials, changed paywalls, lost purchases, and slow or prescriptive support. Public evidence does **not** establish systematic unauthorized billing. | Roots paid-access/trial-charge allegations; BePresent trial-reminder/cancellation allegations; Freedom refund/deletion-case disagreements; BlockSite billing disputes; ScreenZen's changing packaging. |
| **Privacy concerns require scope, not slogans** | Broad policies, older third-party research, and user inferences can identify questions but do not by themselves prove covert recording, sale, illegality, or misconduct. | BlockSite's versioned research/policy comparison; StayFocusd's policy/store wording; ScreenZen diagnostics; school filtering and local-AI claims. |
| **The evidence trail is very uneven** | Some projects have detailed issue histories; others have no public user review corpus. Absence of found complaints is not evidence of absence. | `moritzWa/focus-shield`, Focus AI, brom.ai, AWF blocker, LockIn MCP, and Distraction Killer AI remain explicitly evidence-limited. |

Several patterns deserve emphasis:

1. **Schedules and counters are core promises, not ancillary features.** Freedom's Android reviewer said scheduled sessions started only after opening the app; the vendor promised version **7.14.1** on **2026-08-20**, but no affected-user confirmation is shown ([store exchange](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=to.freedom.android2&hl=en_US)). Opal staff acknowledged early Time Limit exhaustion, attributed it to an iOS 26 defect, and later reported a qualified iOS 26.5 test result, which is not proof that all subsequent failures were resolved ([vendor chronology, 2026-01-27 to 2026-04-10](https://community.opalapp.com/t/time-limit-feature-isnt-working-correctly/9116/27)). StayFocusd has recurring timer allegations in individually readable mirror reviews, but no controlled diagnosis or verified current-version failure rate ([timer reviews](https://extensiondownloader.com/en/extension/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji/reviews?q=timer)).
2. **The same symptom can have different causes.** LeechBlock NG's old Android content-script injection defect was verified fixed upstream, while its January 2025 `commands` API regression was separately diagnosed and repaired. A later Android blocking complaint turned out to require a wildcard for mobile YouTube. These cases should not be collapsed into “Android does not work” ([Mozilla resolution](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1872600); [#531 repair](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/531); [configuration follow-up](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/531#issuecomment-3678422497)).
3. **Strictness is sometimes intentional, while failure of the stated unlock safety valve is different.** Plucky documents delayed approvals and serious configuration tradeoffs; Unpluq limits switching from a lost Tag to a non-Tag barrier to five times per account lifetime; Focus 2 reduced a formerly configurable lock to a 24-hour maximum. Those are design choices. Reviews alleging an entitlement stopped working, a support request went unanswered, or residual restrictions survived deletion concern failures beyond that advertised friction ([Plucky delay system](https://docs.pluckeye.net/delay); [Unpluq recovery rules](https://www.unpluq.com/pages/faq); [Focus lock change](https://heyfocus.com/changelog/)).
4. **Recent maintenance cuts both ways.** FocusMe published a dense sequence of narrowly scoped browser, time-zone, startup, and blocking fixes in late 2026, showing active regression repair but not universal resolution ([official changelog](https://focusme.com/download/changelog.txt)). SelfControl has maintenance work but an unreleased rewrite, leaving open issue reports unresolved in the reviewed record ([maintainer exchange](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/pull/918)). Conversely, a current store update by itself does not demonstrate remediation.
5. **The most alarming stories remain allegations.** Cold Turkey factory-reset/System Restore narratives, FocusMe login/removal difficulty, AppBlock residual locks, ScreenZen passcode-recovery and screen-recording allegations, and various unexpected-charge accounts were not reproduced with device images, receipts, logs, packet captures, or independent adjudication. They are included as attributed experiences, not established permanent damage or fraud ([Cold Turkey review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ac27edf7df5762524253cc6); [FocusMe exchange](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/69326c42e2ab8b7ca471dcc3); [ScreenZen profile](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.screenzen.co)).
6. **Company descriptions and observed experience can diverge without proving bad faith.** SelfControl's restart guarantee is inconsistent with one reported post-restart failure; ScreenZen's local-first description coexists with documented optional diagnostic and analytics uploads; BlockSite's broad current policy permits substantially more processing than a simple “blocker” mental model suggests. A policy permitting collection is not evidence that collection was covert or abusive ([SelfControl FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ); [ScreenZen policy](https://screenzen.co/policies/privacy-policy); [BlockSite policy](https://blocksite.co/privacy)).

The research does **not** support representative prevalence rankings, a universal claim that blocking software fails, or a complaint narrative for every scoped project. Its most useful result is a map of recurring claims and sharply bounded official/code-derived risks, with the strength and recency of evidence kept visible.

### Evidence vocabulary

- **Recurring** means multiple independent-looking firsthand reports of a related symptom in the examined sample. It does not mean common, representative, independently identity-verified, or caused by one defect.
- **Isolated allegation** means one usable firsthand account was found. It may be serious without being generalizable.
- **Official limitation or policy** is what a vendor says the product requires, permits, or does not support—not independently reproduced behavior.
- **Hands-on finding** means the author documented a personal test; it is not equivalent to a multi-device benchmark.
- **Architecture/code-derived risk** follows from source code, package contents, or project architecture. It is not a user complaint or proof that the risk materialized.
- **Fixed** is confined to the named behavior and version. Vendor release notes, an issue's closed state, a support reply, and affected-user recovery are different levels of evidence.

## Detailed findings

## 1. Strict desktop blockers and cloud-backed desktop services

### Cold Turkey

**What users value and dislike.** Longtime users in the examined review/forum material praise unusually hard-to-evade blocking, multiple block types, and—in some accounts—a one-time Pro purchase felt worthwhile. The negative side is not merely discomfort with discipline: reviewers describe Firefox or application blocking failing, locks firing unexpectedly, a floating timer acting inconsistently, inability to edit an active schedule, 24-hour breaks, substantial CPU use, recovery delays, and poor support experiences. The store/database evidence supports repeated themes, not a measured incidence rate ([review corpus](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/getcoldturkey.com); [official support](https://getcoldturkey.com/support/)).

**Core-promise and historical failure reports.** A **2026-03-26** reviewer alleged browsers remained accessible, applications closed after 60 seconds, BIOS/boot-keyboard navigation was affected, and recovery required System Restore and Chrome reinstallation. The headline says years of work were nearly destroyed, but the body identifies no lost documents; no diagnostic evidence establishes that Cold Turkey modified BIOS or destroyed work ([firsthand review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/69c52539762069ec568a2c8c)). A separate **2026-07-07** reviewer described prolonged blue-screen, browser, and installation problems after removal and said they expected a factory reset/Windows reinstall. No forensic evidence established that outcome or its cause, and an October update to the review page is not the original review date ([firsthand review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ac27edf7df5762524253cc6)). Official release notes record now-fixed historical defects: Firefox with many domains/trailing spaces in **2024-12-23**, scheduler/millisecond issues in **2024-01-29**, and backup migration before **4.6 on 2025-04-01** ([release notes](https://getcoldturkey.com/news/)). These repairs should not be recast as current outstanding bugs.

**Configuration, recovery, and support.** Timer placement/size/opacity and F10 shutdown choice are configurable, but infinite-width screens, taskbar hiding, and some legacy settings have explicit boundaries ([official support](https://getcoldturkey.com/support/)). Official reinstall instructions distinguish a removal/reinstall path from disabled blocking; there is no support for manually deleting clock/security files as a shortcut ([recovery instructions](https://getcoldturkey.com/support/reinstall-instructions/)). The vendor says roughly ten seconds of high CPU after launching an elevated app can be normal before it falls below 1%, and that open Settings while time changes are blocked can also cause high CPU; that guidance is not an independent benchmark ([support](https://getcoldturkey.com/support/)).

**Company/pricing evidence.** The current offer is a **US$39 + tax one-time desktop licence** after a seven-day trial, with a 30-day initial-purchase money-back guarantee; “Lifetime” is limited to the software/service's commercial lifespan. Those current terms do not adjudicate a **2026-01-27** refund-denial allegation that lacks purchase/request dates ([pricing](https://getcoldturkey.com/pricing/); [terms, effective 2026-10-15](https://getcoldturkey.com/terms/); [refund policy](https://getcoldturkey.com/refunds/); [refund allegation](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/69ecb058cdfa0a7c6a45672a)). A **2026-08-11** reviewer said past problems had been fixed and the developer responded quickly; a **2025-09-24** reviewer reported multiple replies within hours. Other reviewers allege delayed or scripted support. These conflicting individual accounts do not constitute a support-response dataset ([2026 positive support review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a9004b28b549cacb6c38f16); [2025 positive support review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ab56b7fb0909973e27a159e); [2026 nonresponse allegation](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ac27edf7df5762524253cc6)). The source research did not establish a verified unexpected recurring-charge pattern, destroyed files, unrecoverable lockout after the documented workflow, purchased reviews, or a proven refund-policy violation. **Evidence assessment:** recurring firsthand functional/support themes plus severe isolated allegations; no independent reproduction of the most destructive accounts.

### FocusMe

**What users value and dislike.** FocusMe's appeal is high configurability across desktop and mobile, with users variously praising strict blocking and personalized help. Complaints concern complexity, browser rules, schedules, forced plans, notifications/overlays, CPU, expired-trial escape, and support. Distinct platforms and versions matter: a **2024-08-13** Firefox user said URL detection failed and a second user agreed on **2024-09-30**; later Firefox changelog work means that old thread is not a current-version test. Separately, an **Honor X6c** Android user reported multi-word keyword rules failing through app **4.3.6** despite support suggesting simple keywords rather than regex. Neither proves a cross-platform defect ([Firefox thread](https://community.focusme.com/t/firefox-not-getting-url/228); [Android thread, 2026-07-29 to 2026-09-01](https://community.focusme.com/t/focusme-app-potential-bug/893); [changelog](https://focusme.com/download/changelog.txt)).

**Removal, system recovery, and billing history.** An **M4/Sequoia** customer described Zoom-assisted removal followed by further failure and Windows login trouble; FocusMe replied that its own M4 testing on Sequoia/Tahoe had not reproduced the failure. The customer was able to remove it, and this is not evidence of a current permanent Apple-Silicon lock ([review and vendor updates, 2025-12-04 to 2025-12-09](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/69326c42e2ab8b7ca471dcc3)). A **2023-09-23** expired-trial account is historical: **8.0.9.9 on 2026-10-04** explicitly added license-key entry/sign-out after trial expiry. The changelog does not prove every old schedule/UI case resolved ([trial review](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/650efcf1c25a5cc80baeafc2); [changelog](https://focusme.com/download/changelog.txt)). A Linux unsaved-work issue is tied to the claimed fix in **8.1.4.6**, not current generalized data loss ([same official changelog](https://focusme.com/download/changelog.txt)).

**Recency must be exact.** **8.1.4.9 is dated 2026-10-31**, not November 1, and concerns plan timing. Time-zone work is in **8.1.5.2 (2026-11-01)** and further Firefox work in **8.1.5.6 (2026-11-02)**. Versions **8.1.6.7–8.1.7.6, through 2026-11-07**, separately claim Chrome/Brave launch, Ubuntu 22.04 startup, Chrome-profile, code-editor, and Windows-site-blocking repairs ([official changelog](https://focusme.com/download/changelog.txt)). These are vendor fix claims, no affected-user blanket confirmation.

**Company practices and evidence.** Forum replies include staff assistance, ticket escalation, occasional extended support and device/OEM diagnosis, but other users report ineffective or generic replies ([Android support exchange](https://community.focusme.com/t/focusme-app-potential-bug/893); [official contact/support route](https://focusme.com/contact-us/)). Official help says plans and activity remain local without sign-in; signing in synchronizes plans, tasks, and daily totals rather than full page addresses/titles, while optional Coach reporting and Android's current-app/site visibility have broader data implications. These are architecture/policy statements, not an independent network audit ([FAQ](https://focusme.com/faq); [privacy policy](https://focusme.com/privacy/); [Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.focusme.app&hl=en_US)). **Evidence assessment:** comparatively rich firsthand and first-party history, with active repair counterevidence and several unresolved individual cases; no basis for “systematically ignores support” or permanent current lockout claims.

### Freedom

**What users value and dislike.** Freedom's synchronized multi-device blocking and Locked Mode appeal to users who want one system across desktop and mobile. Negative reports describe scheduled sessions not starting, intermittent blocking, desktop/app networking problems, iOS/Android platform dependencies, uninstall or account-deletion difficulty, and refund friction. The **2024-11-21** App Store review titled “Solid protection” pairs satisfaction with the concept and “randomly cease to work”; Freedom replied recommending the latest version and support follow-up, without a verified resolution ([App Store review record](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/freedom-screen-time-control/id1269788228?see-all=reviews)).

**Corrected Android core-failure case.** The scoped **2026-08-17** Android review says scheduled sessions repeatedly failed to start automatically and began only after opening Freedom, causing unintended doomscrolling. It is not a verified Accessibility/Firefox/restart diagnosis. The vendor promised **7.14.1 on 2026-08-20**; the reviewed store record has no user confirmation after that release ([Google Play exchange](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=to.freedom.android2&hl=en_US)). Official Android troubleshooting acknowledges Android/OEM dependencies, premium blocking limits on some workspaces, and **Android 17 Advanced Protection** blocking the Accessibility-based website path; the macOS guide acknowledges slower performance/app Internet loss in proxy mode and suggests Non-Proxy Mode. These establish documented constraints and workarounds, not frequency or an OS-only cause ([Android guide, 2026-07-14](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529927-freedom-for-android-troubleshooting); [Mac guide, 2026-03-11](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4385704-non-proxy-mode-for-mac); [iOS Connectivity Assist guide, 2026-09-18](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4529937-freedom-for-ios-troubleshooting)).

**Billing, deletion, and company responses.** Some reviewers describe subscription/cancellation friction and being charged for time when blocking allegedly failed; others allege inability to remove an account or obtain a refund. Freedom's billing FAQ directs cancellation/refund questions through its store/support channels; allegations of “no cancellation route” or categorical “no refunds” are therefore too broad ([billing FAQ, modified 2026-10-22](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/4530040-freedom-subscription-and-billing-faq)). Current account-deletion documents conflict for store trials: the mobile FAQ says cancel and wait, while the deletion guide permits removal but stresses separately cancelling Apple/Google billing; paid Premium accounts must finish cancellation and expiry ([mobile FAQ, 2026-07-24](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/8648447-mobile-app-free-trial-faqs); [deletion guide](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/11165071-how-to-delete-your-account)). Old complaints cannot be dismissed using only the newer instructions.

Freedom says desktop browser/content blocking is local rather than routing all browsing through its cloud; a categorical surveillance inference is unsupported ([privacy Q&A](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/1347493-freedom-privacy-questions-and-answers)). Its broader privacy policy also covers identifiable account/order/configuration data and stated analytics, so local blocking does not mean zero server-side data ([privacy policy](https://freedom.to/privacy)). **Evidence assessment:** recurring firsthand failure/billing themes, explicit platform boundaries, and unresolved contradictions in deletion guidance; no systematic unauthorized charging or universal cloud-routing finding.

### RescueTime

**What users value and dislike.** Users value automated timelines, categories, and focus analytics, but the service's core promise breaks for them when logging fails, categorization is wrong, cross-device usage is misleading, or the interface/sync state becomes unreliable. Reviews criticize unexpected tracking after installation, support, subscriptions, and missing/refactored functionality; these are individual reports rather than an incidence measure ([Trustpilot profile](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.rescuetime.com)).

**Official limits and current/historical incidents.** RescueTime documents unlimited computers but no per-device report split: simultaneous activity can exceed wall-clock time, and group/category aggregation can confuse users who expect a device ledger ([How RescueTime Works, updated 2026-06-05](https://help.rescuetime.com/article/245-how-rescuetime-works)). Its November 2025 incident statement said no records were lost, but that reassurance is confined to that incident. In the **2026-04-23 to 2026-04-27** processing outage, the vendor first said no data would be lost and then warned that a small number of files might be delayed or, in rare cases, unrecoverable; resolution did not enumerate every file ([official April incident](https://stspg.io/fdb8wg5vwzg0)). A separate **2026-05-29** processing incident was reported recovered with no data loss, alongside a firsthand sync/queue complaint ([official May incident](https://stspg.io/wcfqmtwjnwxk); [community report](https://community.rescuetime.com/t/unable-to-sync-since-may-28-2026/1697)). Neither a continuous years-long outage nor proven widespread permanent loss is supported.

**Billing, privacy, and support.** A reviewer described an annual charge after disuse, login failure, and no usable support route; another reported a refund after a card/privacy objection on installation. These support individual billing/support disputes, not systematic unauthorized charging ([Mr Ethical review, 2025-04-07](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.rescuetime.com)). Privacy complaints can reflect discomfort with detailed activity recording; supplied data categories should not be converted into proof of sale or exfiltration ([RescueTime privacy](https://www.rescuetime.com/privacy)). **Evidence assessment:** direct complaints plus unusually useful official incident records, with explicit incident-to-incident calibration. The former multi-computer citation was dead at audit and has been replaced with the live official explanation above.

### Focus (heyfocus)

**Identity and user experience.** This is the Apple-platform app at `heyfocus.com`, not FocusMe, Focus AI, or `moritzWa/focus-shield`. Users like custom schedules and scriptable Mac automation, but report a recurring collision with authentication, Touch ID, productivity websites, statistics, scheduling, and support. The configurable **Unspecified** category can accidentally broadly block sites; Safari/Touch ID automation depends on a helper and named browser permissions ([support](https://heyfocus.com/support/)). Focus's own documentation acknowledges overlapping schedules and stateful rules, making incorrect configuration a relevant test case without assuming every reporter erred.

**Historical lock behavior versus current reports.** Focus 2 capped a lock at **24 hours on 2024-11-09**, replacing the older possibility of a 600-hour timer; the old figure is not current product behavior ([changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog/)). A **2025-02-07** reviewer alleged lost statistics and absent help, while a **2025-03-30** reviewer said the app “stopped working entirely”; these are two visible Trustpilot accounts, not a meaningful support-performance sample ([review profile](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/heyfocus.com)). On **2026-10-31**, a separate Mac user described no visible UI, automation errors, and removal with CleanMyMac requiring timing around Focus Agent. The author's abandonment speculation and “since 2004” rhetoric are not independently established facts ([firsthand report](https://johnbeales.com/micro/2026-10-31t114104-0400/)).

**Maintenance, recovery, and company friction.** The changelog's latest displayed release was **2025-08-01** at cutoff, supporting a maintenance/recency question rather than a conclusion that Focus is abandoned or incompatible with macOS Tahoe/26 ([changelog](https://heyfocus.com/changelog/)). Official recovery now offers a Disable Focus request and one-time email unlock token; availability was not tested in every version/mode ([support/unlock instructions](https://heyfocus.com/support/)). **Evidence assessment:** sparse firsthand reviews, a more recent hands-on removal account, and useful official configuration/recovery documents; no robust proof of permanent lockout, abandonment, or recurring billing misconduct.

## 2. Local-first open-source blockers and browser extensions

### Pluckeye / Plucky

**What users value and dislike.** Plucky's appeal is extreme resistance to impulsive disabling, allowlist-oriented filtering, and configurable delays. Its main supported complaints are the high learning curve, delayed approvals/help, browser/network compatibility, and recovery risk—not a fresh representative user survey. The official documentation itself says delay changes, installation/uninstallation, and approvals can be deliberate obstacles; intentional friction should not be labeled malware-like irreversibility without a failed documented recovery ([delay system](https://docs.pluckeye.net/delay); [uninstall guide](https://docs.pluckeye.net/how-to-uninstall)).

**Official limitations and failure surfaces.** Plucky documents which browsers are supported and that unsupported browser choices can leave gaps; network failures and filtered-network dependencies can look like unblocking or overblocking ([manual](https://docs.pluckeye.net/); [browser guidance](https://docs.pluckeye.net/browsers)). Current update instructions say to update over an existing installation rather than uninstalling first or lose accumulated settings, and specifically not to run `pluck repair` during an in-progress update because it can damage both installations and cause loss of Internet. The hope that a repair bug was fixed in **1.15.11** is not an all-version guarantee ([update guide, 2026-04-21](https://docs.pluckeye.net/how-to-update)). The displayed latest release note is **1.16.13 (2024-04-19)** despite current documentation updates ([release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)).

On Android, documentation now recommends **Microsoft Edge from the Play Store + Andoff 2.13.16+**, then the beta “Enable Plucky filtering in supported browsers.” Andoff is separately the device-lockdown tool. This is migration work, not proof that the old Plucky browser's tab/performance limitations or all Android failures are fixed; the guide still recommends one or two tabs and a small configuration ([Android guide, updated 2026-08-06](https://docs.pluckeye.net/pluckeye-on-android)). Fixed **1.13.18 (2022-11-02)** uninstall blockage, **1.16.13** lingering blackout, **1.0.56 (2021-06-23)** configuration loss, and **1.14.30 (2023-07-01)** sync repairs must remain historical/scoped ([release notes](https://docs.pluckeye.net/release-notes)).

**Support/company practice.** Plucky says it is a small team, replies can take **24 hours or more**, and users should search/self-help first. Emergency text/phone escalation is specifically for a paying subscriber who has lost Internet and cannot uninstall, subject to staff location/availability; manually approved forum posts may become irrelevant before publication ([help guide, 2026-04-21](https://docs.pluckeye.net/help)). This supports slow-support/recovery friction while acknowledging a defined escalation route. **Evidence assessment:** strong official architecture/recovery evidence, weak fresh firsthand complaint evidence; no verified irreversible machine damage or no-exit claim.

### SelfControl

**What users value and dislike.** SelfControl's attraction is a free, local, deliberately hard-to-interrupt Mac block with a simple list/timer model. Public issues instead document allowlist failures, unlisted-domain blocking, Safari discrepancy, expiry/cleanup problems, and isolated VPN/recovery failures. Open issues are not proof of typical behavior or current universal failure, but they give more technical context than anonymous praise/hostility ([repository issues](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues)).

**Allowlist and collateral blocking.** Open **#722** includes later reports in **2025** and a detailed **4.0.2 / macOS Sequoia 15.7.3 / Chrome 151** test account on **2026-08-08**; certain allowlisted sites/ports and the linked-site option affected that user's results, but the user diagnosis is not a general maintainer fix ([issue #722](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/722)). In **#904**, a reporter said adding `web.whatsapp.com` in allowlist mode made **all websites accessible** on **4.0.2 / Sequoia 15.3.2**, with no proxy. A later multi-part-URL explanation is community hypothesis, not confirmed repair ([issue #904](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/904)). **#903** supplies useful counterevidence to a single-cause explanation: unlisted-site blocking across browsers followed one imported list and disappeared with a new list, then returned with the old list ([reproduction/outcome](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/903)).

**Browser, VPN, and exit behavior.** Open **#867** reports Safari discrepancies and includes a community iCloud Private Relay hypothesis; neither a general cause nor the deleted **#920** route proves a shipped repair ([issue #867](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/867)). The only direct Proton-style bypass case in this evidence is **#906**, on **4.0.2 (410), macOS Monterey 12.7.6, Proton VPN 4.8.0**. The FAQ says VPN blocking is unsupported/not technically feasible for the project and discusses partial state-restoration risks; the FAQ is an architectural limitation, not an independent second case ([issue #906](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/906); [FAQ](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/wiki/FAQ)). Separate **#852** and **#879** contain isolated serious Private Relay/Internet-recovery allegations without a maintainer diagnosis or reproduced permanent damage ([issue #852](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/852); [issue #879](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/879)). Closed **#642** cannot prove recovery, and a visible running block is not necessarily continued network filtering ([maintainer distinction](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/issues/642#issuecomment-703172195)).

**Maintenance and privacy.** The official download still offers **4.0.2**; public `master` shows a merge on **2025-11-29**, while the maintainer said on **2026-10-20** that a rewrite was basically done but unreleased. This is mixed maintenance and release lag, neither proven abandonment nor proof open complaints were fixed ([download](https://selfcontrolapp.com/); [commit history](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/commits/master/); [PR #918](https://github.com/SelfControlApp/selfcontrol/pull/918)). Its local-first design is not a universal compatibility/privacy guarantee, but the corpus has no independent covert-data-collection finding. **Evidence assessment:** recurring, sometimes detailed firsthand reports with open/closed/version distinctions; several severe cases isolated and no general fix established for #722/#904/#852/#867/#879/#906.

### LeechBlock NG

**What users value and dislike.** Users value detailed schedules, time allowances, block/allow sets, overrides, and cross-browser operation. That configurability creates hostname, keyword, time-period, inheritance, sync, and private-window difficulties. The strongest complaint pattern is not simple weakness: historical settings loss, Android failures, resource use, and time-allowance synchronization have different evidence and resolution states ([project repository](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG)).

**Historical settings loss and current resource/sync issues.** Old **#63/#75** settings-loss reports led to a mitigation in **0.9.10**: the version history dates the release **2019-03-18**, while the maintainer announced it on **2019-03-19**. It was risk reduction, not guaranteed eradication, and is not a current **1.8** defect ([version history](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/blob/master/VERSIONS.md); [#63](https://github.com/proginosko/LeechBlockNG/issues/63)). ### LessWrong GPT-4 blocker / AWF (`coolvision/awf`)

**Exact artifact and intended approach.** Sergii's **2023-09-26** LessWrong linkpost, “GPT-4 for personal productivity: online distraction blocker,” points to the author's implementation article and **AWF: AI Web Filter**, repository **`coolvision/awf`**, release **0.1 (2023-09-24)**. AWF is a self-described Manifest V3 **prototype**, not a `ForumMagnum` feature or unrelated AWF extension. Direct metadata at cutoff showed one public commit/release, no subsequent visible code change, no open issues, and `archived: false`; that does not establish either active maintenance or private abandonment ([LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/denTWbbYyTfbYLtE5/gpt-4-for-personal-productivity-online-distraction-blocker); [implementation article](https://grgv.xyz/blog/awf/); [release](https://github.com/coolvision/awf/releases/tag/0.1); [repository metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/coolvision/awf)).

**One concrete user bug, with source corroboration.** On **2023-09-27**, Jay Bailey reported that removing the last blacklist item made every non-whitelisted page behave as blacklisted and that adding an item restored normal behavior. The 2023 source supplies a plausible mechanism: splitting an empty blacklist produces `""`, every URL contains that substring, and the block path runs. This is source corroboration of one firsthand test, not a new runtime reproduction or evidence of prevalence ([user report](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/denTWbbYyTfbYLtE5/gpt-4-for-personal-productivity-online-distraction-blocker/comment/NiYcrciaFG7hmL7P7); [pinned `content.js`](https://github.com/coolvision/awf/blob/9b8de42b6031c65f384965aae24b943d66a334ad/content.js)). The author replied “thanks!” but the sole public commit exposes no later fix; “no public fix is visible” is safer than claiming every private build remains defective ([commit history](https://github.com/coolvision/awf/commits/master/)).

**Author-run cost/latency and architectural risks.** The developer's few-day trial found correct decisions “in most cases” without a denominator and reported approximately **$1/day**, extrapolated to **$10–30/month** at historical 2023 prices; multi-second waits could break research momentum. The article's upper-latency wording is malformed, so this report says only “several seconds,” not an invented range ([author's dated observations](https://grgv.xyz/blog/awf/)). Active AWF sends the URL and first **2,000 visible body-text characters** to OpenAI for uncached, non-exempt visits, stores the user's key in `chrome.storage.local`, and enables browser OpenAI calls. The author explicitly warned sensitive-page users about this path. Other static risks include decisions from only 2,000 characters, blocking whenever a response includes `YES`, and model/page-derived strings written to `innerHTML` without escaping. The last is an unsafe pattern requiring exploit testing, not a confirmed XSS incident or CVE ([source/settings](https://github.com/coolvision/awf/blob/9b8de42b6031c65f384965aae24b943d66a334ad/content.js); [settings storage](https://github.com/coolvision/awf/blob/9b8de42b6031c65f384965aae24b943d66a334ad/main.js)). Razied's later claim that removable extensions are “essentially worthless” is a general self-control critique, not a reported AWF bypass test ([comment, 2024-07-24](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/denTWbbYyTfbYLtE5/gpt-4-for-personal-productivity-online-distraction-blocker/comment/ALgmzmTCXztC2YfKh)). **Evidence assessment:** one narrow user defect and developer-reported historical cost/latency/privacy limitations, supplemented by static-code findings; no recurring complaint corpus, current price/latency benchmark, public fix, paid-support record, or observed security exploit.

## Methodology and evidence limitations

### Research approach

1. **Corpus and structured enumeration.** The final synthesis draws on nine primary product-group research files, five correction/recency and audit ledgers, the company/practice comparison, and the browser-verification ledger. The 31 products were explicitly inventoried so that macOS versus iOS, Chrome versus Safari, Roots versus Touch Grass, and Focus/FocusMe/Focus AI/`focus-shield` would not silently exchange evidence.
2. **Parallel research.** Researchers separated strict desktop, cloud desktop, open source, popular mobile, freemium, newer mobile, hardware/AI, platform/school filtering, and sparse projects. Subsequent source-quality and recency audits rechecked original citations and narrowed claims rather than merely collecting more dramatic examples.
3. **CLI-assisted retrieval and searches.** Completed research used `curl`, Firecrawl scraping, Tavily (`tvly`) searches/extraction, Exa (`exa-search`) sparse-project searches, RSS/API review feeds, official help/changelog queries, and GitHub/issue/repository retrieval. Where plain `curl` failed, representative pages were attempted through Firecrawl; extracted text was not treated as stronger evidence merely because it rendered.
4. **Source audits.** Desktop researchers checked **133 unique cited URLs** on **2026-11-08**, finding one dead old RescueTime multi-computer link, later replaced with official “How RescueTime Works.” Other reviews and versions underwent targeted fidelity checks. Plain `curl` returned HTTP 403 for Reddit and Trustpilot links, but Firecrawl successfully rendered representative Trustpilot pages; a bot-wall response did not establish that a page was dead. HTTP statuses and successfully rendered page shells were retrieval outcomes, not verification of the associated claims.

5. **Edge verification.** Selected accessible primary pages were actually read in the user's Edge browser through `browser-use`, with the exact opened-page boundary recorded in [`browser_verification.md`](../research_notes/Blocker%20user%20complaints/browser_verification.md). The ledger distinguishes the store listings, review records, version metadata, and official help pages that were opened from material that was only discovered in search. These checks verified visible page content; they did not execute the products, test code, or reproduce transactions.
6. **Recency calibration.** Corrections from **2026-11-08** supersede earlier summaries. Examples include Freedom's Android schedule symptom and **7.14.1** promise, FocusMe's **8.1.4.9 on 2026-10-31**, one sec's limited Android website blocking after **2026-08-04**, BlockSite Chrome **8.0.1 on 2026-11-05**, Forest's official five-tree baseline, and LeechBlock's fixed Android regressions.

### Retrieval and sample limitations

- **Self-selection:** reviews, bug trackers, app-store RSS feeds, and forums oversample people with unusually bad or good experiences. The mobile audit's **2,250 records / 2,183 unique IDs** from **45 Apple RSS feeds** describes a purposive corpus, not 2,183 distinct people or all users. “Recurring” is therefore qualitative.
- **Mutable identities:** App Store/Play reviews, help documents, prices, vendor replies, and version metadata can be edited or localized. Review dates/version fields can change, and same-version reports do not ensure identical device/OS/settings.
- **Bot walls and snippet-only evidence:** Trustpilot and Reddit frequently returned 403 to plain retrieval. Devpost, Product Hunt, npm, GitHub APIs, Reddit JSON, and other resources encountered bot walls, rate limits, mirrors, deleted routes, or partial/index-only retrieval at different stages. No specific Reddit allegation was retained solely from an inaccessible page or indexed snippet. Tavily plan-usage limits and unauthenticated GitHub API rate limits constrained some searches.
- **Third-party mirrors:** ExtensionDownloader represents itself as independent of Google/Chrome Web Store. Its individual StayFocusd reviews were used cautiously; its AI-generated summary and reconstructed history were not treated as primary evidence. Mirror identity/completeness cannot be independently confirmed.
- **No independent reproduction:** The synthesis did not conduct new device tests, purchases, cancellations, support-response measurements, packet captures, heap profiles, or controlled performance benchmarks. A vendor's policy, test claim, release note, or developer response establishes what was said—not underlying universal causation or customer recovery.
- **Sparse projects:** For Focus AI, `moritzWa/focus-shield`, brom.ai, AWF blocker, LockIn MCP, and Distraction Killer AI, source/demo/package analysis is not complaint evidence. Similar limitations apply in part to Deledao and Plucky, where official/technical records outweigh fresh independent user reports.
- **Privacy and safety allegations:** Research publications, permissions, privacy policies, and user reviews answer different questions. No broad malware, data-selling, paid-review, permanent-damage, factory-reset, or unauthorized-charge conclusion was independently adjudicated in this corpus. This does not prove such events never occur; it limits what this report can assert.
- **Policy comparison:** Company practice comparisons followed `company_complaint_policy_mismatch.md`, especially the distinction between frustration with disclosed terms and evidence contrary to those terms. Policy-permitted collection is not proof of abuse, and evidence-sparse official claims are not treated as independent audits.

### Interpretive choices

Older complaints were retained when they reveal product history or repeated themes, but each important case is tied to date, platform, and version where available. Fixed releases were reported as narrow counterevidence rather than ignored or generalized. Where documents conflict—such as Freedom's account-deletion instructions, ScreenZen's terms versus homepage, and RescueTime's successive incident updates—the conflict is preserved.

## Conclusion

Across this scoped corpus, users become most frustrated when a blocker cannot reliably answer three basic questions: **Is it enforcing now? What time, allowance, purchase, or reward state does it believe? How can the user safely regain control?** Failures in those answers generate more consequential complaints than simple strictness. Polished interfaces and physical/AI novelty do not compensate for unreliable schedules, stale Screen Time state, inconsistent browser enforcement, lost Premium/lifetime entitlements, or unclear recovery.

The evidence also cuts against an undifferentiated “blockers are scams” narrative. Open-source maintainers and commercial vendors sometimes document and repair precise defects; Apple/Android/browser constraints genuinely complicate enforcement; intentional delay and physical separation can be the feature users requested. Active maintenance, a policy update, or an official support reply is nevertheless not proof that a particular customer's problem ended.

Accordingly, this report should be read as a dated evidence map, not a ranking of the worst products. Established technical issues, recurring anecdotal themes, isolated destructive or billing allegations, disclosed design limits, and purely static risks have different weights. For the six evidence-sparse AI/MCP/prototype entries in particular, the supportable conclusion is **insufficient public user evidence**, not inferred user anger. The most defensible overall finding is narrower and more useful: blocking reliability depends on time/state correctness, platform integration, explicit recovery, and trustworthy purchase/support behavior—and public complaint evidence measures those dimensions unevenly.
